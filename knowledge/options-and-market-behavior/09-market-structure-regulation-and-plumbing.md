## Core concepts
- **Listed (standardized) option**: exchange-traded contract with OCC-standardized terms (strike, expiry, multiplier 100, exercise style). Legally a *security* (Exchange Act s.3(a)(10)) when the underlying is a security or securities index; an *option on a futures contract* or a *swap/event contract* is a CFTC commodity interest.
- **Multi-listed (fungible) option**: equity/ETF option that any SEC-registered options exchange may list under the Options Listing Procedures Plan (OLPP); one OCC contract, quoted on up to 20 venues, routable across them.
- **Proprietary (exclusively listed) option**: index option licensed to one exchange group. SPX, SPXW, XSP (Mini-SPX, 1/10th), VIX, RUT, MRUT, OEX, DJX trade only on Cboe Options (C1); NDX/NQX/XND only on Nasdaq exchanges (PHLX/ISE). Cboe's Nanos (1/100th XSP, $1 multiplier, launched Mar 14, 2022) still appear in Cboe's XSP product navigation and subscription list as "Nanos S&P 500" (Oct 14, 2026) but the standalone product URL returns 404 and volume is negligible; confirm listing status against Cboe's delisting notices before using the symbol in a dataset [uncertain].
- **OPRA (Options Price Reporting Authority)**: the consolidated options tape (quotes and trades from every options exchange), operated by SIAC (NYSE/ICE subsidiary) under a national market system plan. Not a routing or clearing entity. Reg NMS Rule 603(b) requires brokers to show the consolidated quote; depth-of-book is exchange-proprietary (Cboe Multicast PITCH, Nasdaq ITTO, NYSE Options feeds, MIAX ToM/AIS). OPRA charges professional per-device fees and much lower non-professional fees [uncertain on amounts].
- **OCC (Options Clearing Corporation)**: founded 1973; the *sole* clearinghouse (CCP) for all US listed securities options; SEC-registered clearing agency, CFTC-registered DCO (clears CFE VIX futures, security futures), designated a SIFMU in 2012. Novates every trade (buyer to every seller, seller to every buyer).
- **DMM / LMM / PMM / specialist**: a market maker with an allocation entitlement in assigned classes in exchange for heightened quoting obligations. Names: DPM/LMM (Cboe), PMM/CMM (ISE/GEMX/MRX), Specialist/e-Specialist (NYSE American/Arca), PMM (MIAX), LMM (PHLX/BOX), Specialist/Directed Market Maker (IEX).
- **Customer priority**: on "classic"/pro-rata exchanges, non-professional customer orders at the best price trade ahead of market makers and broker-dealers regardless of time. **Professional (customer)**: per Cboe Rule 1.1 and equivalents, any non-broker-dealer that places more than 390 orders in listed options per day on average during a calendar month for its own beneficial account(s), counted across all options exchanges; members re-evaluate each calendar quarter on the prior quarter's activity, and a person who qualifies must be marked Professional on every exchange. Professionals lose customer priority and customer fee treatment but are still "customers" for margin and SIPC purposes.
- **Pro-rata vs price-time**: pro-rata splits an incoming order among resting quotes at the best price in proportion to size (after customer priority and any DMM entitlement); price-time fills strictly first-in-first-out.
- **Maker-taker vs classic (payment-for-order-flow) fee model**: maker-taker pays a rebate to resting liquidity and charges the aggressor; classic exchanges charge market makers, give customers free/cheap executions, and let members pay "marketing fees" that fund PFOF to retail brokers.
- **Price-improvement auction (PIA)**: a 100-millisecond-class exposure auction where a member submits a customer order paired with its own contra at a price at or better than the NBBO; other members respond; the initiator typically keeps a guaranteed share (commonly 40% if it matches the best response). Names: AIM (Cboe), PIM (ISE), PRIME (MIAX), PIXL (PHLX), CUBE (NYSE), PIP = Price Improvement Period (BOX).
- **Complex order**: multi-leg order (spreads, straddles, stock-option combos) executed as a package on a Complex Order Book (COB), often via a complex order auction (COA); legs may execute at net prices that are not individually at the NBBO subject to leg-protection rules.
- **FLEX option**: OCC-cleared contract with negotiable strike, expiry (up to 15 years), exercise style and settlement (AM/PM, cash/physical) traded through exchange FLEX mechanisms (Cboe Rules 4.20-4.22, FLEX AIM/COB); same clearing, margin treatment and (for equity FLEX) position limits as standard options, with broad-based index FLEX generally unlimited; minimum-size requirements have largely been removed [uncertain]. Used by defined-outcome ("buffer") ETFs and structured-note hedges, so FLEX open interest is a visible slice of dealer positioning (cross-link 05): FLEX ADV ~2.8M contracts = 3.8% of volume in Q2 2026, volume +46% and OI >+40% YoY (Cboe).
- **Linkage**: the Options Order Protection and Locked/Crossed Markets Plan (approved July 30, 2009; effective Aug 31, 2009) obliges each exchange to avoid trading through a better protected quote on another exchange (via routing or intermarket sweep orders) and to avoid locking/crossing. Replaced the 2000 Options Intermarket Linkage Plan.
- **Penny Interval Program** (the "penny program"): made permanent by SEC order in 2020 (pilot since Jan 26, 2007). Penny classes quote in $0.01 below $3 premium and $0.05 above; SPY, QQQ, IWM quote in $0.01 at all prices; non-penny classes quote $0.05/$0.10.
- **Reg T** (Federal Reserve Regulation T): 50% initial margin on stock; long options must be paid in full; sets the framework FINRA 4210 builds on. **Portfolio margin (PM)**: risk-based alternative (FINRA 4210(g)) approved by the SEC in 2006-07 using OCC's TIMS theoretical model.
- **Account-level rules**: FINRA Rule 2360 (the options rule) covers position/exercise limits, reporting, account approval, suitability ((b)(19)), discretionary accounts, confirmations, the exercise cut-off and assignment allocation ((b)(23)); SEC Rule 9b-1 requires delivery of the Options Disclosure Document ("Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options") before the first trade. **Option approval levels** (Level 1 covered calls/protective puts; 2 long options; 3 spreads; 4 naked equity; 5 naked index) are broker constructs built on 2360(b)(16) due-diligence duties, not SEC categories. **SIPC** covers listed options held at a failed broker as securities (up to $500,000 per customer incl. $250,000 cash); futures and CFTC event contracts are not SIPC-covered. **Rule 15c3-5** (market access, Nov 2010) forces every broker with exchange access to pre-trade credit, capital and erroneous-order checks on each order, including sponsored-access and market-maker flow.
- **Exercise-by-exception (Ex-by-Ex)**: OCC automatically exercises expiring options in the money by $0.01 or more unless the clearing member submits contrary instructions. It applies only at expiration; early exercise always needs an affirmative instruction.
- **Event contract / binary option**: pays a fixed amount if a defined event occurs. CFTC-regulated event contracts (Kalshi, Polymarket US, Crypto.com, CME, Robinhood via DCM partners) are swaps/futures-style instruments; SEC-regulated binary *options* (e.g., Cboe Predicts on XSP, launched 2026) are securities listed on an options exchange.

## Mechanisms
**Why 20 options exchanges exist.** Each additional licence is cheap once a group has a matching engine and buys (i) more DMM/LMM seats to sell to market makers, (ii) a second fee model (classic for retail-PFOF flow, maker-taker for professional flow) and (iii) extra protected quotes that rivals must honour under linkage. The SEC's April 16, 2026 options market-structure roundtable (led by Trading & Markets Director Jamie Selway) aired the cost: Kevin Tyrrell (NYSE) said new-exchange incentives add fragmentation and industry cost; Andrew Schultz (Susquehanna) called connectivity the main barrier to entry; John Fischer (Citadel Securities) cited roughly two million listed strikes, about half with zero open interest.

**Why market makers quote.** Quoting obligations are the price of (a) allocation entitlements, (b) reduced fees, (c) cheaper margin via OCC's market-maker (haircut-based, 15c3-1a) treatment and (d) exemption from position limits for hedging. Concretely (Cboe Rules 5.52/5.54 and equivalents): a competitive market maker must quote continuously, two-sided, in about 60% of the series of each appointed class for about 90% of the day; DPMs/LMMs/PMMs owe roughly 90% of series at a higher time standard; quotes must sit within rule-defined maximum widths that scale with the bid (opening-width tables of roughly $0.25 for bids under $2 to about $1 above $20, wider intraday) [uncertain on exact percentages and width tables, which differ by exchange and are periodically amended]. Quote mitigation (the 2024 OLPP strike-range amendment, series-count caps) exists because every listed series is a quoting obligation. Cboe's Q3 2026 guidance puts revenue per contract near $0.05 for multi-listed vs $0.95 for proprietary index options, which is why Cboe defends SPX/VIX exclusivity and rivals push SPY/XSP-type substitutes.

**How retail options flow is handled (PFOF).** Retail brokers route most marketable options orders to wholesalers (Citadel Securities, Susquehanna/G1 Execution, Wolverine, IMC, Optiver; Jane Street entered in 2024 [uncertain]; Morgan Stanley sold its electronic options market-making book in 2025 [uncertain on buyer]). Options cannot be internalized off-exchange, so the wholesaler either (i) submits the order to a price-improvement auction where it keeps a guaranteed share, or (ii) routes it to an exchange where it is DMM and collects the entitlement (first 5 contracts of small orders plus 40-60% of the remainder [uncertain; varies by exchange]). Ernst & Spatt (2026, RFS; existence and thesis verified) report the top three wholesalers hold ~74% of DMM seats, seats are rarely reassigned, sole-DMM status is associated with wider spreads, and one-lot auction trades pay higher spreads (winner's curse) [figures uncertain]. Citadel Securities alone paid ~$732M for options order flow in the first nine months of 2024 (~$943M incl. equities; Global Trading, Feb 20, 2025); industry options PFOF rose ~40% YoY in Q3 2024 [uncertain; behind registration wall]. Rule 606 quarterly disclosures (2018 amendments) list per-venue payments for options separately; Schwab, Robinhood and IBKR were the largest retail options sources in Cboe's Q2 2026 review. **Rule 605 execution-quality reports cover NMS stocks only** (the Mar 2024 modernization did not add options), so no public per-broker execution-quality statistic exists for options; the **Consolidated Audit Trail (CAT)** captures every options order, route and execution (simple orders from Dec 2021, complex from 2022) but is regulator-only.

**Fee stack a trader actually pays** (per contract, on top of commission): (1) exchange fee/rebate: zero or a small rebate for non-professional customers on classic and most maker-taker venues (EDGX Oct 2026: $0.01 rebate for customers removing liquidity in penny and non-penny classes, tiered to $0.10-$0.22), versus $0.36-$0.45 per SPX contract and $0.07 per XSP contract (10+ lots) for customers on C1; market makers and professionals pay the fees that fund this; (2) **Options Regulatory Fee (ORF)**: assessed by each exchange on customer contracts cleared by its members wherever executed, reset semi-annually (EDGX: $0.00286 per contract side, Oct 2026; others differ [uncertain]); (3) **OCC clearing fee**: flat per contract with a per-trade cap, about $0.02 capped near $55 per trade [uncertain; OCC fee page unreachable Oct 14, 2026]; (4) **FINRA Trading Activity Fee** on option sells [rate uncertain]; (5) **SEC Section 31 fee** on sells, reset by the SEC each fiscal year and mid-year to hit its appropriation target ($27.80 per million in FY2025; set to $0.00 for periods when collections ran ahead, so check the current rate [uncertain]); (6) CAT funding fees passed through by exchanges. Zero-commission retail therefore still pays ORF/TAF/Section 31, and exchange fee changes show up directly in Cboe's and MIAX's revenue per contract.

**SEC 2022-2024 reform cycle and outcome.** The Dec 2022 proposals (Order Competition Rule/Reg NMS Rule 615, Regulation Best Execution, tick-size/access-fee, Rule 605 expansion) targeted equities but would have reached options through best-execution duties. On June 12, 2025 the SEC withdrew Reg Best Ex and the Order Competition Rule with 12 other proposals; only Rule 605 modernization (Mar 2024) and the equity tick/access-fee rule (Sept 2024, litigated) survived, so options best execution still rests on FINRA Rule 5310 and exchange rules. The April 2026 roundtable put strike rationalization, the 5-lot DMM entitlement, auction fee asymmetries and protected-quote status for small exchanges on the table; no rulemaking had followed by Oct 2026.

**Clearing and settlement chain.** Trade on exchange -> locked-in trade to OCC -> OCC novates and nets by clearing member (CM) -> overnight margin via STANS (System for Theoretical Analysis and Numerical Simulations; Monte Carlo over ~10,000 scenarios, 99% expected shortfall, two-day horizon, portfolio-level with correlation [uncertain on parameters]) -> CMs post collateral by 9:00 a.m. CT [uncertain]; intraday calls possible. Exercise/assignment: holder instructs CM -> CM submits to OCC -> OCC assigns randomly across short CMs overnight -> CMs allocate to customers by FIFO or FINRA-approved automated random selection (FINRA 2360(b)(23)(C); method filed with FINRA and disclosed in writing) -> the assigned customer learns the next morning before the open -> physical delivery settles through NSCC's CNS on T+1 (since May 28, 2024; previously T+2); cash-settled index exercises settle next business day. **Dividend plumbing**: with T+1, ex-date = record date, so dividend capture requires exercising by the broker's cut-off the day *before* ex-date; a short ITM call assigned that night leaves the writer short stock on ex-date, liable for the dividend, with notice arriving only the next morning. OCC's Clearing Fund is sized monthly by Comprehensive Stress Testing to a "Cover 2" standard (simultaneous default of the two largest CM families) under the 2018 Financial Safeguards Framework, which replaced a fixed $1.8B "prudential margin" [uncertain on the $1.8B]. **SR-OCC-2025-018** re-weights CM contributions from 70% total risk (margin) / 15% open interest / 15% cleared volume to 70% stress-loss shortfall over margin (SLOIM) / 15% margin / 15% cleared volume: approved by delegated authority Dec 11, 2025 (34-104359), stayed by Fidelity's petition (granted Feb 13, 2026, 34-104846; Schwab projected a ~40% rise in its contribution), then approved by the Commission on Oct 2, 2026 (34-106573, setting aside the delegated order, as modified by Partial Amendment No. 1). **Cross-margining**: OCC-CME cross-margining has long covered CM proprietary and market-maker accounts; an enhanced house-account arrangement for equity-index futures and options went live in 2024 and extension to end-customer accounts was announced for 2025 [uncertain whether launched; OCC/CME pages unreachable Oct 14, 2026].

**Jurisdiction.** SEC: options on stocks, ETFs, securities indexes, and security futures (jointly with CFTC). CFTC: futures, options on futures (e.g., E-mini S&P options, CME Bitcoin options), swaps, and *event contracts* under CEA s.5c(c)(5)(C). The 1982 Shad-Johnson accord drew the line; index options vs index futures options are economically near-identical but sit in different regimes (margin: Reg T/4210 vs SPAN; clearing: OCC vs CME Clearing; customer protection: SIPC vs CFTC segregation). **Tax boundary that drives product choice**: cash-settled broad-based index options (SPX, XSP, NDX, RUT, SPEQF) and options on index futures are Section 1256 contracts (60/40 long/short-term, marked to market at year-end), whereas options on ETFs (SPY, QQQ, IWM) are *equity options* taxed as ordinary short-term capital gains; Cboe's XSP page states the 1256 eligibility explicitly. This, plus European exercise, cash settlement and no dividend/early-assignment risk, is why XSP is marketed as the retail SPY substitute (no tax domain file exists in this kb yet; the broad-based/narrow-based test is left to it).

**Options on futures: the parallel CFTC plumbing (CME).** E-mini S&P 500 (ES), Nasdaq-100 (NQ) and Russell 2000 (RTY) options trade on CME Globex roughly 23 hours a day, Sunday 6:00 p.m. to Friday 5:00 p.m. ET, with a daily 5:00-6:00 p.m. ET maintenance break and an equity-index trading halt 4:15-4:30 p.m. ET [verify current CME schedule; CME pages returned 503 on Oct 14, 2026]. Expirations: weekly options on every weekday (Monday through Friday weeklies [uncertain on when Tuesday/Thursday were added]), end-of-month options, and quarterly options that expire with the futures on the third Friday at the AM special opening quotation. They are American-style and exercise into the underlying futures contract (not cash), are margined by CME SPAN (migrating to SPAN 2) at an FCM with no Reg T, are not OCC-cleared or SIPC-covered, and are Section 1256 contracts. They are the only liquid hedge for SPX/XSP delta during Cboe Curb and GTH, and the reason index options trade to 4:15 p.m. ET.

**Hours and why they differ.** Equity options stop at 4:00 p.m. ET because the underlying's closing print sets the exercise decision; a handful of highly liquid ETF options (SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA and others on exchange lists) trade to 4:15 p.m. ET to match the 4:15 close of cash-settled index options (SPX, XSP, VIX, RUT, NDX), which trade to 4:15 because their value depends on the index, whose futures trade to 4:15; on their last trading day PM-settled index options close at 4:00. Cboe then runs Curb 4:15-5:00 p.m. ET and Global Trading Hours 8:15 p.m.-9:25 a.m. ET (order acceptance from 8:00 p.m.) for SPX/SPXW/XSP/VIX/RUT, i.e. near-24x5 (Cboe hours page, Oct 14, 2026; GTH ADV records 228K in Q3 2026 and 263K in Sept 2026; GTH+Curb +32% YoY in Q1 2026, driven by Asia-Pacific [uncertain]). On May 28, 2026 the SEC approved Cboe Options trading ~20 multi-listed single-stock options (Mag-7, PLTR, AVGO, AMD) 7:30-9:25 a.m. and 4:00-4:15 p.m. ET on C1 only, planned from July 13, 2026 subject to a related rule filing; eligibility: six-month ADV >= 150,000 contracts, underlying market cap >= $50B, underlying ADV >= 10M shares, refreshed semi-annually; with only C1 open there is no NBBO or linkage protection in those windows. Cboe's hours page still showed no such session on Oct 14, 2026 [uncertain whether launched]. Equities 23x5 (8 p.m.-4 a.m. ET, Sun-Thu) is scheduled for Dec 6, 2026 on 24X, Nasdaq, NYSE Arca, Cboe EDGX and MEMX after SIP amendments approved June 26, 2026; options are not part of that plan.

**Opening process and settlement values.** Equity options open on each exchange only after the underlying's primary listing market has printed its opening trade or quote (opening rotation/auction), so quotes before roughly 9:30:30 are indicative and the NBBO is often absent in the first seconds; SPX opens at 9:30 on the index value even though components open staggered. AM-settled index options (SPX third-Friday monthlies, NDX and RUT monthlies) settle to a Special Opening Quotation computed from each component's opening print (SET for SPX, RLS for RUT), published mid-morning once all components have opened, which can differ materially from the 9:30 index print; VIX futures and options settle on the VIX SOQ computed from the opening auction prices of the SPX options series used in the index on the Wednesday settlement morning. Quad-witching third Fridays (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) stack index futures, AM index options, stock options and single-stock futures expiries, and the Russell reconstitution (last Friday of June, moving to semi-annual [uncertain]) concentrates volume in the closing auction (cross-link 04 and 01 for SOQ mechanics).

**Halts and circuit breakers.** When an underlying stock is paused (LULD pause or regulatory halt), every options exchange halts trading in its options; trading reopens after the underlying resumes via an opening rotation. Market-wide circuit breakers key off the S&P 500 versus the prior close: Level 1 (-7%) and Level 2 (-13%) each trigger a 15-minute halt only if hit before 3:25 p.m. ET; Level 3 (-20%) halts for the rest of the day at any time. MWCB halts apply to options and equities together and options reopen with a rotation after the equity reopening; index options halt only on an MWCB, not on single-stock LULD pauses; after a Level 3 halt trading resumes the next day (the 23x5 plan keeps the next-day resumption at 4:00 a.m. ET).

**Obvious and catastrophic error rules (why a bad fill may not stick).** Since the 2015 harmonization every options exchange has near-identical rules (Cboe Rule 6.5; Cboe EDGX/BZX Rule 20.6; NYSE American Rule 975NY; Nasdaq ISE Options 3, Section 20; MIAX Rule 521). A trade is an *obvious error* if it is at least a threshold away from the exchange-computed theoretical price (roughly $0.25 for theoretical under $2, scaling to about $2.00 above $100) and a *catastrophic error* at wider thresholds (roughly $0.50 to $4.00) [uncertain on exact table values; verify current rule text]. Notification: within 30 minutes (obvious) or by 8:30 a.m. ET next day (catastrophic); customer-side obvious-error trades are generally nullified while non-customer trades are adjusted to theoretical plus or minus a small buffer, and catastrophic errors are adjusted to the threshold. Exchange **risk controls** explain the rest of the odd fills: market-order and drill-through price protection (execution only within a buffer through the NBBO, remainder cancelled or held), per-member kill switches, cancel-on-disconnect, market-maker quote-rate and risk-limit monitors (e.g., Cboe's Risk Monitor Mechanism) that purge quotes at loss or volume thresholds, and self-trade prevention; these are the exchange side of Rule 15c3-5.

**Corporate actions.** OCC's Securities Committee (the "adjustment panel", OCC plus exchange representatives) decides adjustments under OCC By-Laws Art. VI s.11A: whole-share splits multiply contracts and divide strikes; other splits/stock dividends change the deliverable (e.g., 3-for-2 -> 150 shares, symbol gets a "1" suffix); cash special dividends of at least $12.50 per contract ($0.125/share) reduce strikes by the dividend; ordinary cash dividends are not adjusted; mergers convert the deliverable into the cash/stock package; spin-offs add the spun-off shares to the deliverable.

**Strike and expiration proliferation.** Exchanges add strikes and expiries because each series is a free option on volume; the cost (OPRA capacity, market-maker quoting load, fragmentation of open interest) is externalized. OLPP sets strike intervals ($2.50 below $25, $5 to $200, $10 above; $1 and $0.50 strike programs for low-priced names; index rules differ) and ranges around the underlying; a 2024 OLPP amendment tightened ranges as quote mitigation [uncertain on date], and IEX's Oct 2026 rule 20.140A imports those ranges. Expirations: standard third-Friday monthlies; weeklies (2005 Cboe pilot, now standard); SPX gained Tuesday/Thursday expiries in 2022 making it daily (Mon-Fri); SPY/QQQ followed in late 2022 and IWM in 2023 [uncertain on exact dates]; NDX daily by 2023 [uncertain]; in 2026 single-stock Monday/Wednesday expiries (TSLA, NVDA, AAPL, IBIT, AMZN, META, AVGO, GOOGL, MSFT) reached ~6M contracts/day with more names expected in Q3 2026 (Cboe Q2 2026). OPRA sizing responded: 96 multicast lines since Feb 5, 2024; July 2026 projection 16.134M messages per 100 ms aggregate per stream (~161M msg/s burst), 287B messages/day, rising to 18.9M/100 ms and 330B/day by Jan 2028; median SIP latency under 19 microseconds (SIAC notice, Mar 25, 2026).

## Formulas and quantitative rules
```
Reg T / FINRA 4210 strategy-based margin (per contract, x100 multiplier)
Long option:            pay 100% of premium (no loan value if <9 months to expiry)
Naked equity call:      max(20% * S + P - OTM, 10% * S + P)
Naked equity put:       max(20% * S + P - OTM, 10% * K + P)      [put floor uses strike]
Naked broad-based index call (SPX, XSP, NDX, RUT): max(15% * I + P - OTM, 10% * I + P)
Naked broad-based index put:                       max(15% * I + P - OTM, 10% * K + P)
  (4210(f)(2)(E): short-put floor = premium + 10% of aggregate exercise price)
Covered call:           long stock at 50% Reg T; no additional margin on the call
Vertical debit spread:  pay net debit; credit spread: margin = strike difference - credit
  S = underlying price, I = index level, P = premium, K = strike, OTM = out-of-the-money amount
Worked example: short 1 SPY 600 put, SPY = 620, P = 3.00
  20% * 620 = 124.00; OTM = 20.00; 124 - 20 + 3 = 107.00 -> $10,700
  floor: 10% * 600 + 3 = 63.00 -> $6,300;  requirement = max = $10,700
```
```
Portfolio margin (FINRA 4210(g), OCC TIMS): requirement = worst loss across 10 equidistant
shocks of the underlying within the class group range, netted within a product group:
  high-cap broad-based index (SPX, NDX, RUT, SPY...):  +6% / -8%      (4210(g)(2)(F))
  non-high-cap broad-based index:                     +/-10%
  single stocks, narrow-based/sector indexes:          +/-15%
  leveraged ETFs: range scaled by leverage factor
Rule minimums: no dollar minimum equity for listed-option PM accounts in 4210(g); brokers
  impose house minimums (commonly $100,000-$150,000, some higher) [broker-specific].
  $5,000,000 equity to carry unlisted derivatives ((g)(4)(C)); accounts under $5,000,000
  must be margined intraday on the same basis as end-of-day ((g)(1)(K)); per-contract floor
  $0.375 x multiplier = $37.50 per standard listed contract, capped at the market value of
  longs ((g)(7)(B)); members must monitor concentration ((g)(1)(I)) and may charge more
  ((g)(1)(H)), which is where house stress ranges wider than -8% come from.
Same SPY 600 put under PM (ignoring vol shock): loss at -8% (SPY 570.4) ~ (600-570.4)*100 = $2,960 + premium effects
  -> roughly 3-4x less capital than strategy-based for an OTM short put, which is why
     institutional/professional short-premium books run under PM (the share of short-premium
     volume originating in PM accounts is not published).
```
```
Intraday margin standard (FINRA 4210(d)(2), effective June 4, 2026; replaces PDT):
  For each margin account, on any day with an IML-reducing transaction (a short sale, or any
  purchase other than a short cover), compute intraday margin deficit =
    max(0, intraday margin requirement - margin excess at the time of the transaction)
  Deficit must be met as promptly as possible; it stays "outstanding" until met or until the
  close of the 15th business day. A customer with a practice of leaving deficits unmet past
  the 5th business day gets a 90-day freeze on new debits/short positions; deficits
  <= min(5% of account equity, $1,000) do not count toward that practice.
  Firms may either block trades that would create a deficit in real time or compute
  end-of-day and issue calls. Firms may phase in until Oct 20, 2027.
  Old rule (removed): >= 4 day trades in 5 business days and > 6% of trades -> "pattern day
  trader" -> $25,000 minimum equity and 4x day-trading buying power.
```
```

  Holder's exercise decision cut-off: 5:30 p.m. ET on expiration day; members may not accept
    customer or non-customer instructions after 5:30 (1 h 30 min after an announced early close)
  Contrary Exercise Advices / Advice Cancels to OCC: customer accounts until 7:30 p.m. ET;
    non-customer (member proprietary) accounts until 7:30 p.m. ET only via an electronic
    time-stamped process, otherwise 5:30 p.m. ET                  (2360(b)(23)(A)(iv))

  Equity physical settlement: T+1 (since 2024-05-28);  cash index options: next business day
```

## Practitioner heuristics
- Treat 4:00-4:15 p.m. ET as a separate regime: single-stock options and cash equities are closed, but SPY/QQQ/IWM/DIA and index options are open because E-mini futures (ES, NQ, RTY) trade to 4:15; spreads widen and the day's gamma hedging is already in the cash close. Hedge index-option delta here with futures (or the ETF), never single-stock options; stock-option combos and any complex order with a single-stock leg are rejected after 4:00, while pure index/ETF spreads keep trading on the complex book to 4:15. ES then halts 4:15-4:30 p.m. ET and 5:00-6:00 p.m. ET [verify current CME schedule]; during Cboe Curb (4:15-5:00) and GTH (8:15 p.m.-9:25 a.m.) futures are the only liquid hedge, which is why GTH spreads track ES liquidity. (Cboe's 2026 single-stock extended session would add 4:00-4:15 for ~20 names on C1 only [uncertain whether launched].)
- Expiration-day index options (PM-settled SPXW, XSP, NDXP, RUTW) stop at 4:00, not 4:15; the SET/AM-settled monthly (SPX third-Friday, VIX Wednesday) settles on the opening prices, so overnight exposure after Thursday's close is unhedgeable with the same contract and the settlement print can sit well away from the 9:30 index level.
- Exercise decisions: brokers impose cut-offs earlier than 5:30 p.m. ET (often 4:30-5:00); an ITM-by-$0.01 long will be auto-exercised unless you say otherwise, and after-hours moves can flip "ITM at 4:00" to "OTM at 5:29" (pin risk on the short side is uninsurable after your broker's cut-off). Assignment notices arrive the next morning; for dividend capture the exercise must be in by the broker's cut-off the day before ex-date.
- Venue choice is the broker's or wholesaler's, not the end customer's: a retail customer's only levers are the broker's Rule 606 disclosures and any directed-routing option. If you run your own router as a DMA member: resting non-professional customer orders get priority (and typically zero or rebated fees) on customer-priority venues (C1, EDGX, PHLX, NYSE American, MIAX, GEMX/MRX/Emerald/ISE, MX2, IEX) and belong there; when taking liquidity as a professional, compare maker-taker take fees against classic venues' professional fees per class and penny/non-penny status; work large multi-leg orders via complex-order auctions or floor brokers. Averaging >390 orders/day across all exchanges in a calendar month makes you a Professional and strips customer priority and fee treatment, which changes which venue is cheapest.
- Penny vs non-penny classes change the economics of resting orders: in $0.05 names, a limit one tick better than the market is a 5-cent concession; in penny names the queue is deep and pro-rata venues reward size.
- Use PM where allowed for short-premium and spread books, but size to the house number, not to TIMS: the +6/-8% index range is the FINRA/OCC floor, brokers must monitor concentration (4210(g)(1)(I)) and may charge more (4210(g)(1)(H)), and in practice house PM stress ranges for index/ETF products are wider than -8% and include vol-up scenarios and concentration add-ons [broker-specific; verify your broker's PM disclosure]. Long OTM puts placed just outside the TIMS scan range reduce the TIMS requirement but not necessarily the house requirement or the broker's liquidation trigger.
- After June 4, 2026 the PDT rule is gone, but brokers may keep legacy restrictions until Oct 20, 2027; check each broker's intraday-margin implementation before assuming unlimited day trades in small accounts.
- Position limits bite on ETFs sooner than on SPX: hedge books that would exceed SPY's 3.6M-contract limit migrate to SPX (no limit) or to futures; IBIT's 1,000,000 limit now equals the large-ETF tier (IWM/EEM/EFA/FXI), four times the top single-stock tier of 250,000.
- Fees: budget ORF, OCC, TAF and Section 31 pass-throughs on top of "zero commission"; on SPX/XSP the exchange customer fee ($0.36-$0.45 per SPX contract) is a real cost on 0DTE churn, and a one-lot that is auction-matched rather than exchange-swept may carry a wider effective spread (Ernst & Spatt).
- Bad fills: a print far from theoretical can be busted (customer) or adjusted (non-customer) under the obvious-error rules within 30 minutes, so never hedge a suspiciously good fill until the window has passed, and expect market orders in illiquid series to be price-protected and partially filled rather than swept.
- Corporate actions: an adjusted contract (symbol with a "1" suffix) has a non-standard deliverable and far lower liquidity; close before the ex-date if you need exit optionality.
- Market data: OPRA burst rates are ~10x the average; a feed handler sized to average throughput drops packets exactly when it matters (open, data releases, 0DTE expiries). Budget to SIAC's projected aggregate MPHM (16.1M msgs/100 ms per stream for July 2026, 18.9M for Jan 2028), not to observed daily averages, and remember the per-line ceiling is 625K msgs/100 ms.
- Prediction markets: the "price" is a probability only if the contract is fully collateralized and settlement is unambiguous; watch state-law exposure (Ninth/Sixth Circuit states) because a platform can be forced to geofence overnight.

## Common misconceptions and pitfalls
- "There are 17 US options exchanges." Correct only between Sept 2023 and Aug 2024; 18 after MIAX Sapphire, 20 as of Oct 13, 2026 (MX2, IEX Options). BOX is frequently omitted from lists.
- "OCC uses SPAN." CME's SPAN is used for futures margin at CME Clearing (and by FCMs for options on futures); OCC uses STANS for clearing-member margin and TIMS for customer portfolio margin.
- "Options are internalized like stocks." Every listed option trade prints on an exchange; "internalization" happens via auctions and DMM entitlements, which is why exchange rules, not ATS rules, govern retail execution quality, and why Rule 605 statistics (equities only) say nothing about options.
- "SPY options close at 4:00." SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA and other listed ETF/index options trade to 4:15 p.m. ET; most single-stock options close at 4:00 (Cboe's 2026 extended session would add 4:00-4:15 for ~20 names [uncertain whether launched]).
- "Exercise deadline is 4:00 p.m." The OCC/FINRA holder cut-off is 5:30 p.m. ET; brokers set earlier internal deadlines; members can submit contrary exercise advices to OCC until 7:30 p.m. ET for customer accounts (non-customer accounts only via an electronic time-stamped process).
- "T+1 made options settle faster." Options trades already settled T+1 (premium) before 2024; what changed is the physical delivery on exercise/assignment (T+2 -> T+1), which shortened the window for covering an assignment and moved the dividend-capture exercise deadline to the day before ex-date.
- "Event contracts are binary options and therefore SEC-regulated." Only when listed on a securities exchange on a securities underlying (Cboe Predicts on XSP). Kalshi/Polymarket/Crypto.com contracts are CFTC-jurisdiction DCM products, not SIPC-covered, and not subject to Reg T/4210.
- "Position limits apply to SPX." Removed in 2001 [uncertain on pilot vs permanent]; only reporting and exchange surveillance remain. They do apply to SPY (3.6M), QQQ (1.8M), IWM (1M) and single names (up to 250,000).
- "Portfolio margin requires $100,000 by rule." FINRA 4210(g) sets no dollar minimum for listed-option PM accounts; the $100,000-$150,000 figures are broker house minimums, and the rule's own thresholds are $5M for unlisted derivatives and intraday monitoring below $5M.
- "Maker-taker is always cheaper for the customer." Retail customers on classic venues typically pay zero exchange fees and receive auction price improvement; maker-taker's advantage accrues to liquidity providers and professional takers.
- "The pattern-day-trader rule still requires $25,000." Eliminated effective June 4, 2026 (SEC Release 34-105226, Apr 14, 2026; FINRA RN 26-10), subject to the phase-in.
- "0DTE is two-thirds of all options." The ~66% figure is SPX-specific [uncertain for 2026 months]; across all products 0DTE is roughly a quarter of volume (Cboe Q2 2026, derived).
- "IEX's 350 microseconds is its risk-parameter trigger." The 350 microseconds is the symmetric speed bump on inbound messages; the Options Risk Parameter is a separate optional quote type whose calculation the speed bump makes possible.
