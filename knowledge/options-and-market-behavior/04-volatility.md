## Core concepts

- **Volatility**: annualized standard deviation of log returns, quoted in percent (16 = 16%/yr); **variance** = vol^2. Variance-swap (log-contract) payoffs are linear in realized variance; a listed option's price is roughly linear in vol near the money (ATM straddle ~ 0.8 x S x sigma x sqrt(T); Brenner-Subrahmanyam 1988), which is why practitioners quote vol. Because sqrt is concave, a vol swap or ATM straddle is worth less than the square root of the variance-swap rate; the gap (the "convexity adjustment") grows with vol of vol and is a few vol points at 1-3 months for equity indices (Demeterfi-Derman-Kamal-Zou 1999).
- **Realized vol (RV)**: vol computed from observed prices over a past window; estimator-dependent. **Implied vol (IV)**: the vol that makes a pricing model (Black-Scholes-Merton for listed options) reproduce the market price; a forecast plus a risk premium.
- **Close-to-close estimator**: root mean squared daily log return. **Range estimators** (Parkinson, Garman-Klass, Rogers-Satchell, Yang-Zhang) use open/high/low/close to extract more information per day. **Intraday realized variance**: sum of squared high-frequency (typically 5-minute) returns; converges to integrated variance until **microstructure noise** (bid-ask bounce, discreteness, stale quotes) dominates at fine sampling ("volatility signature plot").
- **Stylized facts**: **clustering** (large |returns| follow large |returns|), **persistence** (slow decay of vol shocks), **mean reversion** (fluctuation around a long-run level), **fat tails** (kurtosis far above 3, partly from time-varying vol), **leverage effect** (negative return/vol-change correlation; Black 1976), **long memory** (hyperbolic decay of |r| autocorrelations; Ding-Granger-Engle 1993).
- **Models**: **GARCH(1,1)** (conditional variance = constant + last squared shock + last variance); **EGARCH** (Nelson 1991; log variance, asymmetric); **GJR-GARCH** (Glosten-Jagannathan-Runkle 1993; extra weight on negative shocks); **HAR-RV** (Corsi 2009; next RV on daily/weekly/monthly averages of past RV, the practical standard). **IV versus time-series forecasts**: implied vol is a biased-high but informationally efficient forecast of subsequent RV; once IV is in the regression, GARCH/historical terms add little (Christensen-Prabhala 1998; Blair-Poon-Taylor 2001; Poon-Granger 2003 survey). Practical standard for RV forecasting: HAR-RV augmented with log VIX; for a point forecast of realized vol, de-bias IV by the regime's RV/IV ratio.
- **Expected move**: 1-sigma price change implied by IV: S x IV x sqrt(T). **Straddle-implied move**: ATM straddle price ~ 0.8 x the 1-sigma move ~ risk-neutral expected absolute move.
- **IV rank**: position of today's IV between the 52-week low and high (0-100). **IV percentile**: share of lookback days with IV below today's.
- **Variance risk premium (VRP)**: implied variance minus subsequently realized variance (loosely IV minus RV in vol points); positive on average for equity indices, small or insignificant for most single stocks (see Mechanisms). Academic papers often use the opposite sign ("negative variance risk premium").
- **Skew/smile**: IV versus strike at fixed expiry. Index IV falls with strike through the range that carries most of the vega (roughly 80-110% moneyness) and typically turns up again in the far-OTM call wing at short maturities; single stocks, commodities and FX show a tilted, two-sided "smile". Upside skew can flatten or invert in call-buying episodes (2020-21 single names; 2024-25 NDX/semiconductors [uncertain]). **25-delta risk reversal (RR)**: IV(25d put) - IV(25d call) (equity convention; FX quotes call minus put). **Butterfly**: average wing IV minus ATM. **90-110 skew**: IV at 90% moneyness minus IV at 110%. **SKEW index** (Cboe): 100 - 10 x risk-neutral 30-day skewness of SPX log returns.
- **Term structure**: ATM IV or the VIX-family indices versus maturity; **contango** (longer above shorter, normal), **backwardation** (front above back, stress). VIX, VIX3M, VIX6M and VIX1Y are spot-starting variance rates; VIX futures are forward-starting 30-day claims, a different curve (see Formulas, forward variance). **Vol of vol**: variability of IV; **VVIX** = VIX-method IV of VIX options.
- **VIX options smile**: IV of VIX options rises with strike (call wing rich, put wing cheap), the mirror image of SPX, because VIX is positively skewed and mean-reverts from above; OTM VIX call IV routinely runs well above 100% while near-the-money VIX put IV is far lower [levels: practitioner range, uncertain]. VVIX is the VIX-method 30-day index of this smile and is therefore dominated by the call wing. VIX options are priced and hedged off the matching VIX future (the option's forward), never spot: the put wing is bounded by how far the future can fall toward the calm-regime floor (~10-12), the call wing is not (Sepp 2008; Bergomi 2016).
- **VIX**: Cboe Volatility Index; model-free 30-day expected SPX vol from a 1/K^2-weighted strip of OTM SPX/SPXW options in the two Friday expiries bracketing 30 days. Siblings: **VIX1D**, **VIX9D**, **VIX3M**, **VIX6M**, **VIX1Y**. VIX futures/options settle to a **Special Opening Quotation (SOQ)**, symbol **VRO**, on expiration morning (usually Wednesday). **VIX ETPs**: VXX, VIXY (1x long, 30-day constant-maturity futures index); UVXY (1.5x since late Feb 2018 [date uncertain]) and UVIX (2x, Volatility Shares, 2022); SVXY (-0.5x since late Feb 2018 [date uncertain]) and SVIX (-1x, fund inception 2022-03-28); XIV (-1x ETN) terminated Feb 2018.
- **Sticky strike / sticky delta**: competing descriptions of how the surface moves with spot (IV fixed per strike vs per moneyness); the **skew-stickiness ratio** (Bergomi) quantifies where a market sits (see Mechanisms). **Vol crush**: IV collapse after an event resolves. **Ex-event vol**: diffusive vol left after removing the event's variance.
- **Vol regime**: persistent low/high state; **vol targeting**: exposure scaled to 1/recent vol; **VRP harvesting**: systematic option/variance selling (PUT, BXM indices; strangles).
- Cross-asset indices: **VSTOXX** (EURO STOXX 50), **VXN** (Nasdaq-100), **RVX** (Russell 2000), **MOVE** (ICE BofA Treasury-option vol, bp/yr), **OVX** (crude via USO options), **GVZ** (gold via GLD), **DVOL** (Deribit BTC/ETH 30-day). **Implied correlation** (Cboe COR1M/COR3M, successors of ICJ/JCJ): the average pairwise correlation that reconciles SPX IV with constituent IVs; the quantitative counterpart of the correlation explanation of index skew and of dispersion trading.

## Mechanisms

- **Why IV exceeds RV (VRP)**: (1) crash-insurance demand: end users are net long index puts (Garleanu-Pedersen-Poteshman 2009; whether they are also net short index calls was not verified against the paper [uncertain]); dealers absorbing the put inventory are short in the crash state and charge a premium rising with inventory; (2) priced jump risk after 1987 (Rubinstein 1994; Bates 2000); (3) variance is a priced state variable because vol rises when wealth falls, so claims paying in high-vol states earn negative expected returns (Bakshi-Kapadia 2003; Carr-Wu 2009); (4) limits to arbitrage: short-vol capital is scarcest after drawdowns, when the premium is widest. The premium concentrates in the front of the curve and in the put wing (skew premium).
- **Term structure of the premium and the VIX futures premium**: Dew-Becker, Giglio, Le and Rodriguez (2017) find that claims to realized variance over the coming month carry a large negative expected return while forward variance claims starting more than about one month ahead earn roughly zero: the VRP is a front-month, realized-variance phenomenon, so carry strategies should concentrate exposure at the front and treat selling 3-6 month vol as a directional view, not harvesting. Cheng (2019) measures the ex-ante premium of VIX futures over statistically expected VIX and finds it falls, often below zero, as risk rises, and that it forecasts futures returns: VIX futures tend to under-react to spot on the way into a spike and over-price mean reversion on the way out.
- **Index vs single-name VRP and the correlation premium**: the premium is concentrated in index options. Carr and Wu (2009) find a strongly negative (short-vol-positive) variance risk premium for SPX and other indices but insignificant premia for most individual stocks; Driessen, Maenhout and Vilkov (2009) show the index-minus-single-name gap is a priced correlation risk premium: implied correlation (COR1M/COR3M) sits above subsequently realized correlation on average and spikes in sell-offs. Consequences: short single-name vol earns little carry and bears idiosyncratic jump risk; dispersion (short index vol, long single-name vol, vega- or correlation-weighted) isolates the correlation premium and loses in correlation spikes.
- **Why index skew is steep**: 1987 memory and jump risk; leverage effect and volatility feedback (Campbell-Hentschel 1992; Bekaert-Wu 2000): any stochastic-vol model with negative spot-vol correlation produces a downward-sloping smile; demand pressure (structural put buying for protection/collars/tail funds plus structural call selling by overwriters); and correlation: index vol is driven by cross-stock correlation, which spikes in sell-offs, so index downside variance exceeds what single names imply (Bakshi-Kapadia-Madan 2003; dispersion desks trade the gap). Single-name smiles are flatter and two-sided because idiosyncratic jumps (earnings, M&A) cut both ways. Commodity smiles tilt toward the scarcity side (calls in supply shocks); FX risk reversals follow carry and intervention risk.
- **Skew dynamics**: in vol points, put-minus-call IV widens in sell-offs (surface and vol of vol rise); relative to ATM level it is often steepest in calm, low-VIX markets and flattens in spikes because ATM IV rises faster than wing IV. Hence SKEW-index records cluster in calm periods (183.12 on 2025-02-18, VIX mid-teens) and high SKEW is a poor timing signal. Vol of vol raises wing prices directly (wing options are convex in vol).
- **Skew term structure and skew-stickiness ratio**: index skew measured as vol per unit log-moneyness (or per 10 delta) is steepest at the front and flattens with maturity roughly as 1/sqrt(T) (Gatheral 2006; Mixon 2007), so a fixed 90-110 or 25d RR cannot be compared across expiries without normalizing by sqrt(T) or working in delta space. **SSR** (Bergomi 2009) = (d sigma_ATM / d ln S) / (d sigma / d ln K): sticky strike implies SSR = 1, sticky delta SSR = 0; SPX empirically runs near 1.5 from one month to a year, i.e. ATM vol falls by about 1.5 times the local skew slope per 1% rally. SSR x skew slope gives the practical "skew-adjusted delta"; the ratio drifts in stress.
- **Why the curve is normally in contango**: VIX mean-reverts (daily log-VIX autocorrelation 0.98, half-life ~34 trading days, 1990-2026). Below the long-run level, expected future vol exceeds spot, so longer maturities and VIX futures price above spot; risk premia add a term/vol-of-vol layer. In stress, spot exceeds the long-run level and the curve inverts. Computed 2009-2026: VIX3M > VIX on 92% of days; 100% when VIX < 15, 55% when VIX is 30-40, 0% when VIX > 40.
- **VIX construction logic**: a log contract is statically replicated by OTM puts and calls weighted 1/K^2 (Neuberger 1994; Carr-Madan 1998; Demeterfi-Derman-Kamal-Zou 1999); its price is risk-neutral expected variance. VIX = 100 x sqrt(interpolated 30-day variance). Consequences: VIX is a variance-swap rate, not an average IV; it exceeds 30-day ATM IV by a skew contribution: for a smile linear in strike with slope b (decimal vol per unit moneyness), K_var ~ sigma_ATM x sqrt(1 + 3 T b^2) (DDKZ 1999). With calm-market SPX 1-month slopes (b ~0.5-0.8) the linear term gives roughly 0.5-1.5 points; the convex far-put wing, heavily weighted by 1/K^2, adds more, so the observed gap is commonly put at 1-3 points [magnitude uncertain; measure it from the surface], wider when skew is steep relative to ATM (calm tapes) and narrower in spikes. Options with a zero bid or zero ask are excluded (truncation after two consecutive such strikes), which biases the index slightly low; wide quotes raise mid-prices and can spike it (2024-08-05). **Filtering**: Cboe applies an index-level filtering algorithm to spot VIX (Methodology section 3.2; rules and thresholds in the Mathematics Methodology, currently 0.50 vol points over a 2-minute threshold period for the RTH session) to handle mid-quotes that do not reflect expected volatility, and republishes the last valid value when the index cannot be calculated (section 3.3). The filter applies to spot VIX, not to the SOQ.
- **Settlement mechanics**: the SOQ uses opening-auction trade prices (Cboe HOSS) in the single SPX/SPXW expiry 30 days from settlement, a Cboe pre-announced strike range, zero-bid options included, no interpolation. Settlement is linear in deep-OTM put prices (each contributes ~ price x dK/K^2), so modest orders in illiquid wings can move it; see Contested for the manipulation evidence.
- **VIX futures, basis, roll**: a VIX future is a forward on VIX at its expiry, i.e. on the square root of the 30-day variance-swap rate starting at expiry. By Jensen's inequality F_VIX <= sqrt(forward variance implied by the SPX surface), with the shortfall increasing in vol of vol (Carr-Wu 2006), so VIX futures sit below the forward-starting variance strip even with no risk premium, and they cannot be arbitraged against spot. **Beta to spot**: VIX futures move much less than spot VIX; front month roughly 0.5-0.7 points per point of spot (higher near expiry and in spikes), second month roughly 0.4-0.5, declining further out, so a 30-day constant-maturity product (VXX/VIXY/SVIX index) captures about half of a spot-VIX move [practitioner estimate; compute from CFE daily settlements by days-to-expiry before quoting]. Size futures/ETP hedges with the futures' own beta, not with the spot-VIX beta table below. **Roll yield** is not a cash flow from rolling: it is the convergence of each future toward spot as expiry approaches. Over a holding period the long's P&L is approximately (change in spot VIX) minus (the slope the curve had priced in), so contango bleeds only to the extent spot rises by less than the curve forecast, which on average it does by the futures premium (Simon-Campasano 2014; Cheng 2019). Rule of thumb: with the front-second spread at s%, a 30-day constant-maturity long loses about s% per month if spot is unchanged; shorts earn it with catastrophic convexity in spikes.
- **Leveraged/inverse ETPs and Feb 2018**: a daily-rebalanced product with leverage L and assets A trades L(L-1) x A x r in futures after a futures return r. For L = -1 and L = 2 the coefficient is +2: both inverse and 2x products BUY after a rise. On 2018-02-05 (SPX -4.10%; VIX +20.01 to 37.32, +115.6%, the largest percentage rise on record) the complex of leveraged and inverse ETPs (about $4bn at end-2017, BIS) had to buy into the window before the 16:15 NAV; other participants began bidding up VIX futures at around 15:30 in anticipation; 115,862 VIX futures traded within one minute at 16:08, which BIS describes as "roughly one quarter of the entire market" (base not stated; average daily open interest was ~590k in 2017); dealers hedged by selling E-minis. XIV "fell 84%" (BIS; basis and period not specified; the closing indicative value on 2018-02-05 is widely reported as ~$4.22 vs $108.37, about -96% [uncertain]), triggering its acceleration clause (loss of 80% or more from the prior indicative value); Credit Suisse announced termination on 2018-02-06 with a last trading day of 2018-02-15 [uncertain]. ProShares cut UVXY to 1.5x and SVXY to -0.5x shortly after (announced 2018-02-26, effective 2018-02-28 [uncertain]).
- **VIX-SPX relation**: VIX rises when SPX falls because expected vol rises (leverage/feedback), put demand and skew reprice, and a fixed-strike skew mechanically raises the IV read at the new ATM. The relation is convex: SPX -1% days average +3.0 VIX points, -3% days +7.6 (2016-2026, log-return buckets).
- **Vol targeting feedback**: vol-control, risk-parity and CTA mandates scale exposure to 1/RV; after a shock they sell for days regardless of price, extending drawdowns; in calm regimes they lever up, suppressing RV and the VRP (Moreira-Muir 2017 show the single-portfolio benefit; the aggregate feedback is a stability concern). Short-dated option selling (0DTE, covered-call/put-write ETFs) adds a dampener via dealers' long gamma (see 05-dealer-positioning-and-option-flows).
- **Earnings and scheduled events**: an announcement adds a lump of variance at a known time; expiries after it price diffusive + event variance, expiries before do not, so the front expiry's annualized IV sits far above the next ("kink"); afterward the event variance disappears and IV collapses ("vol crush") although diffusive vol is unchanged. For FOMC/CPI/NFP/elections the same holds for SPX; VIX1D shows it (elevated at the close before the event, collapsing after), while the 30-day VIX, containing ~1 event day among ~21 trading days (30 calendar days), moves little from the event itself. **Mechanical event drop**: when the event passes, its excess variance leaves the 30-day window, so VIX falls by a computable amount even if diffusive vol is unchanged (formula below; about 0.5 points for a priced 1.5% FOMC move at VIX 15). Net this out before reading an event-day decline as "relief"; read the event variance from VIX1D or the front weekly minus the next.
- **Clocks**: VIX measures time in calendar minutes; VIX1D in business minutes (Cboe methodology). Dealers price short-dated options in business/event-weighted time (weekend and holiday days near-zero weight, scheduled event days extra weight), so a calendar-time pricer shows IV "collapsing" on Friday afternoons and "jumping" Monday mornings although nothing repriced (Jones-Shemesh 2018 on option prices around non-trading periods); VIX itself drifts down mechanically ahead of holidays and long weekends as low-variance calendar days enter its window and drifts back after. Compare 0-5 DTE IVs only in business time with explicit event weights; VIX1D and VIX are not on the same clock.

## Formulas and quantitative rules

```
O,H,L,C = open/high/low/close; r_t = ln(C_t/C_{t-1}); n = days; A = 252 (trading-day annualization; 365 for calendar/crypto conventions)
u = ln(H/O), d = ln(L/O), c = ln(C/O)
Close-to-close:   sigma^2 = A/(n-1) * sum (r_t - rbar)^2        (zero-mean variant A/n * sum r_t^2 preferred for short windows)
Parkinson 1980:   sigma^2 = A/(4 n ln2) * sum [ln(H/L)]^2       (~5x efficiency vs CC under GBM, zero drift, no gaps)
Garman-Klass 1980:sigma^2 = A/n * sum [0.5*(ln(H/L))^2 - (2 ln2 - 1)*c^2]   (~7.4x efficiency)
Rogers-Satchell 1991: sigma^2 = A/n * sum [u(u-c) + d(d-c)]      (drift-independent)
Yang-Zhang 2000:  sigma^2 = A*[sigma_o^2 + k*sigma_c^2 + (1-k)*sigma_RS^2],  sigma_o^2 = var ln(O_t/C_{t-1}), sigma_c^2 = var ln(C_t/O_t),
                  k = 0.34/(1.34 + (n+1)/(n-1))   (minimum-variance; handles gaps and drift; still biased by jumps/discrete sampling)
Intraday RV (Andersen-Bollerslev 1998; ABDL 2003): RV_day = sum_{i=1..M} r_{5min,i}^2 (M = 78 per 6.5h session) + overnight r^2;
                  sigma_ann = sqrt(A * mean RV_day). Noise-robust: two-scale RV (Zhang-Mykland-Ait-Sahalia 2005), realized kernels
                  (Barndorff-Nielsen et al. 2008), pre-averaging; bipower variation (Barndorff-Nielsen-Shephard 2004) separates jumps.
Scaling: sigma_daily = sigma_ann/sqrt(252) = sigma_ann/15.87 ("VIX/16"); sigma_monthly = sigma_ann/sqrt(12).
   VIX 16 -> 1.0% daily 1-sigma; VIX 32 -> 2.0%; VIX 15.27 (2026-10-13) -> 0.96%.  Realized has typically run ~VIX/22 (RV/VIX median 0.72, 2016-2026).
```

```
GARCH(1,1): sigma_t^2 = w + a*e_{t-1}^2 + b*sigma_{t-1}^2; persistence a+b (0.95-0.99 daily equity indices); LR variance w/(1-a-b);
            half-life = ln0.5/ln(a+b); typical a 0.05-0.10, b 0.85-0.93
GJR:        sigma_t^2 = w + (a + g*1{e_{t-1}<0})*e_{t-1}^2 + b*sigma_{t-1}^2, g > 0 = leverage effect
EGARCH:     ln sigma_t^2 = w + b*ln sigma_{t-1}^2 + a*(|z_{t-1}| - E|z|) + g*z_{t-1}
HAR-RV (Corsi 2009): RV_{t+1} = b0 + b_d*RV_t + b_w*mean(RV_{t-4..t}) + b_m*mean(RV_{t-21..t}) + e  (often in logs; coefficients each ~0.2-0.4 [uncertain]);
            HAR-RV-IV adds b_iv*ln VIX_t (practical benchmark)
Overlapping-window inference (VRP, RV forecasts): 21-day windows => ~n/21 independent observations; use Newey-West/HAC s.e. with lag >= 21
            (Hansen-Hodrick 1980; Newey-West 1987) or a block bootstrap; report non-overlapping windows per bucket.
```

```
Expected move (1-sigma): EM = S*IV*sqrt(T), T in years (DTE/365 with IV on a 365 basis; for 0-5 DTE use business time with event weights, see Clocks)
Risk-neutral E|move| (normal approx) = EM*sqrt(2/pi) = 0.798*EM
ATM straddle (Brenner-Subrahmanyam 1988) ~ 2*0.3989*S*IV*sqrt(T) = 0.8*EM  =>  straddle ~ E|move|;  1-sigma ~ 1.25*straddle
   (broker rules "0.85 x straddle" or straddle/strangle averages approximate the same object)
Example: S=500, IV=20%, 30 DTE: EM = 500*0.20*sqrt(30/365) = 28.7; straddle ~ 22.9.
P(|return| > 1 EM) = 31.7% if normal; empirically 17.9% of SPX days exceeded prior-day VIX/sqrt(252) and 1.8% exceeded 2x (2016-10 to 2026-10) because VIX embeds the VRP.
   Decomposition: scaling by realized sd instead (VIX/22) restores ~32% at 1-sigma but leaves 2- and 3-sigma exceedances above the normal
   4.6%/0.3% because of kurtosis (19.8 in sample): VIX's over-prediction masks fat tails, it does not remove them.
IV rank = (IV - min52w)/(max52w - min52w)*100;  IV percentile = share of lookback days with IV < today
```

```
Forward variance between T1 < T2 (annualized): sigma_f^2 = (sigma_2^2*T2 - sigma_1^2*T1)/(T2 - T1)
   Calendar-arbitrage bound: total variance sigma^2*T must be non-decreasing in T; a negative forward variance is a data error or stale quote.
   Example: VIX 15, VIX3M 19 => forward 30-day vol over months 2-3 = sqrt((19^2*90 - 15^2*30)/60) = 20.7; the comparable VIX future (starting
   ~2 months out) prices this forward minus a vol-of-vol convexity discount (Carr-Wu 2006). Spot index ratios (VIX3M/VIX) and futures ratios
   (M2/M1, M1/spot) are different objects with different normal ranges.
Variance-swap strike vs ATM vol (DDKZ 1999, linear skew in strike, slope b): K_var ~ sigma_ATM*sqrt(1 + 3*T*b^2)
   Example: sigma_ATM 15%, T = 1/12, b = 0.6 (6 vol points per 10% moneyness) => K_var = 15*sqrt(1.09) = 15.7 (+0.7 pts); b = 0.8 => 16.2.
VRP_t = IV_t^2 - RV_{t->t+30}^2 (variance) or VIX_t - RV_{t+1..t+21} (vol points, practitioner)
Variance swap: P&L = N_var*(RV^2 - K^2), N_var = N_vega/(2K);  vol swap strike ~ K_var - convexity adjustment (grows with vol of vol)
Roll yield (constant-maturity long VIX futures): front-second spread s% per month => ~s%/month bleed if spot is unchanged; realized bleed =
   curve slope - realized change in spot [typical 5-10%/month contango range uncertain]
Daily-rebalance drag (log): extra drag = L(L-1)*sigma^2/2 per year beyond L x underlying; L = -1 or 2 => sigma^2 (VIX-futures vol 70% => ~49%/yr)
Close rebalance demand: dNotional = L(L-1)*AUM*r_futures  (L=-1 or 2: 2*AUM*r; L=1.5: 0.75*AUM*r; L=-0.5: 0.75*AUM*r)
Ex-event vol from two expiries (diffusive vol s_d, event variance V_E, T in years):
   s_1^2*T1 = s_d^2*T1 + V_E ;  s_2^2*T2 = s_d^2*T2 + V_E  =>  s_d^2 = (s_2^2*T2 - s_1^2*T1)/(T2 - T1);  event move = sqrt(s_1^2*T1 - s_d^2*T1)
   Example: 7-day IV 60%, 35-day IV 35%: s_d^2 = (0.1225*35/365 - 0.36*7/365)/(28/365) = 0.0631 -> s_d = 25.1%;
            V_E = 0.36*7/365 - 0.0631*7/365 = 0.00569 -> implied 1-sd event move 7.5%.
Mechanical event drop in VIX (excess event variance E = V_E - (VIX/100)^2/252, decimal, vs an ordinary day at the VIX rate):
   VIX_after ~ sqrt(VIX_before^2 - E*(365/30)*10^4)
   Example: VIX 15, priced 1.5% 1-sigma FOMC move (ordinary day 0.945%): E = 0.000225 - 0.0000893 = 0.000136 => VIX_after = sqrt(225 - 16.5) = 14.4.
   From VIX1D (business-time) at the prior close: E ~ ((VIX1D/100)^2 - (VIX/100)^2)/252.
```

## Practitioner heuristics

- **VIX/16 = daily 1-sigma priced; VIX/22 = daily 1-sigma usually realized; VIX/sqrt(12) = monthly.** Because of the VRP fewer than one in five SPX days exceed the prior-day VIX-implied 1-sigma (18% vs 32% normal); "bigger move than expected" is rarer than headlines suggest. Use VIX/16 for "how much is priced", VIX/22 for "how much usually happens", and remember the tails are fatter than normal on either scale.
- **Use the straddle, not a fudge factor**: straddle = market-implied expected absolute move; 1.25x straddle = 1-sigma. Compare with the trailing 21-day RV move and the name's historical event moves before pricing an event.
- **Curve as regime gauge (spot indices)**: VIX3M/VIX above ~1.10 = carry regime (short vol has the wind behind it); 1.00-1.05 = transition; below 1.00 = stress (do not add short vol; fade the front only once vol of vol is falling). Curves invert almost always above VIX 40 and almost never below 20. Futures-based gauges (M1/spot, M2/M1) measure forward-starting claims and have their own thresholds; do not transplant the spot-index numbers.
- **Point beta**: expect ~1-1.5 VIX points per 1% SPX in normal regimes with acceleration beyond -2%; size VIX-call hedges with the percentage beta for the current VIX level (higher when VIX is low). For futures or ETP hedges use the futures' beta to spot (roughly half at the front month [practitioner estimate]), estimated by days-to-expiry.
- **Carry is front-loaded**: the VRP is harvested on next-month realized variance; selling 3-6 month vol is a view on the level of vol, and VIX futures premia shrink or reverse as risk rises (Dew-Becker et al. 2017; Cheng 2019).
- **Skew is level-dependent and maturity-dependent**: compare skew per unit of ATM vol, in delta space, and normalized by sqrt(T) across expiries; raw put-minus-call points widen automatically when vol rises. High SKEW in a calm tape means cheap ATM vol and expensive wings, not an imminent crash. For deltas, use SSR ~1.5 x skew slope as the skew adjustment unless the regime says otherwise.
- **Vol crush sizing**: front-expiry IV after earnings falls to roughly the ex-event vol implied by the second expiry; if the front premium over the second expiry is small relative to the historical event move, the event is cheap. Never read annualized front-week IV as an annual forecast.
- **Mean-reversion timing**: positioning/liquidity spikes (2018-02, 2024-08) reverted below 20 within about a week; policy, credit or macro spikes (2008, 2020, 2022, 2025-04) took weeks to a year. That split is hindsight; ex-ante discriminators used on desks [heuristic, untested here]: (1) credit and funding: HY/IG spreads, FRA-OIS and cross-currency basis not widening points to positioning; (2) realized vs implied: positioning spikes show VIX far above subsequent intraday realized within 2-3 sessions; (3) curve shape: deep inversion with VIX6M/VIX1Y also rising signals macro repricing, a front-only spike with a flat back end signals positioning; (4) VVIX/VIX collapsing within days. None is reliable alone.
- **Vol of vol**: VVIX thresholds (~85 cheap, ~120 rich) are regime-dependent folklore: VVIX is a percentage vol of VIX, the dollar price of VIX convexity scales roughly with VIX x VVIX, so "cheap VVIX" at VIX 12 buys little payout per contract, and the calm-regime floor has drifted up as the VIX-option market deepened (since-2010 median 92.8 vs 91.4 from 2008). Compare VVIX with its quantiles conditional on the VIX bucket and with the realized vol of the front future. "VVIX rolls over before VIX" fits 2018-02 and 2024-08 but only loosely 2020-03 (both peaked 03-16). Price and hedge VIX options against the matching future, never spot; expect the call wing to carry far higher IV than the put wing.
- **Tail hedges in vol terms**: OTM SPX puts pay on a crash and bleed theta plus skew premium; VIX calls pay on a vol spike (which can occur without a large SPX drawdown) and bleed roll plus the VIX call premium; put spreads cap payoff but sell back expensive wing vol. Compare instruments per unit of crash payoff and per unit of bleed by regime; VVIX prices the VIX-option smile, not the cost of SPX puts, so a low VVIX says VIX calls are cheap relative to their own history, nothing more [heuristic].
- **Estimator choice**: Yang-Zhang or Garman-Klass for low-variance estimates from few daily bars; close-to-close when comparing with VIX (which prices variance including overnight); 5-minute RV plus overnight for intraday work. Never compare open-to-close RV with an IV that includes overnight variance.
- **Clocks**: price 0-5 DTE options and read VIX1D in business/event time; expect VIX to drift lower into long weekends and holidays and recover after, with no information content; a Friday-afternoon "IV collapse" on a calendar-time screen is the weekend being priced out.
- **IV rank vs percentile**: rank is distorted for a year after a spike (post-April-2025 everything looked "low"); percentile is robust but blind to regime shifts; use both plus the IV/RV ratio.
- **Roll yield**: never hold a long VIX ETP as a static hedge (negative carry of several percent a month plus leverage drag); inverse products earn carry but a 100% futures rise wipes a -1x product; contango is a forecast of convergence, not a guaranteed carry, and is paid back in full when spot rises by more than the curve implied.
- **Events in the 30-day VIX**: one event day among ~21 trading days adds little; read VIX1D or the front weekly for the event premium; expect VIX to fall after elections/FOMC when the outcome is not a shock, even if the index also dips, and subtract the mechanical drop before calling it relief.
- **When these break**: in 2020-03 and 2025-04 the point beta, the contango rule and "VIX reverts in days" failed together; disorderly opens can print VIX values the futures never confirm (2024-08-05).

## Common misconceptions and pitfalls

- "VIX is the average IV of SPX options." It is the square root of a 1/K^2-weighted risk-neutral variance that loads on OTM puts; it sits above ATM IV by the skew contribution (DDKZ term plus put-wing convexity).
- "VIX predicts a crash." VIX is a price; it exceeded subsequent realized vol ~84% of the time in 2016-2026. High VIX after a drop is mostly reaction plus insurance demand.
- "VIX futures = VIX" and "VIX ETPs track VIX." Futures are forwards on VIX at expiry (positive basis in contango, below the forward variance strip by Jensen) and move with roughly half of spot's beta at the front month; ETPs track a rolling futures index with persistent negative carry.
- "Contango is carry in the bank." The slope is the market's forecast of convergence; the long loses only when spot rises by less than the curve implied, and a short loses everything the curve "paid" and more when spot rises by more.
- "Expected move = 0.8 x straddle." The straddle already is ~0.8 of the 1-sigma; multiplying again double counts.
- "Option payoffs are linear in variance." Only variance swaps are; option prices are roughly linear in vol near the money and concave in variance, which is why vol swaps and straddles trade below the square root of the variance-swap rate.
- Annualization and clock errors: sqrt(365) on trading-day data (or vice versa) misstates vol by ~20%; mixing open-to-close RV with close-to-close IV omits overnight variance (overnight share of SPX daily variance ~20-40% [uncertain; needs open prices, which the free sources used here lack]); comparing calendar-time VIX with business-time VIX1D, or 0-5 DTE IVs across a weekend, without converting clocks.
- Over-fine intraday sampling: 1-second RV is dominated by bid-ask bounce; 5-minute (or noise-robust) estimators are the standard; Liu-Patton-Sheppard (2015) found 5-minute RV hard to beat.
- Range estimators are biased in practice: observed highs/lows understate the true range (discrete observation), and jumps/gaps violate Parkinson/GK/RS assumptions; YZ handles gaps, not jumps.
- SKEW as a timing signal: records in Dec 2024-Feb 2025 preceded April 2025 by two months, but SKEW had also sat at 150+ through many calm stretches, and it fell during the spike.
- "Backwardation means vol will fall." It means spot exceeds expected future vol; the VRP is largest after VIX > 30 in the computed sample, but realized vol often stays high for weeks and the loss was taken on the way in.
- Overlapping-window statistics: "positive 84% of days" is ~120 independent windows, not 2,500 observations; conditional buckets with few episodes are anecdotes with decimals.
- Treating the SOQ as the prior close: settlement uses opening-auction trades in a single expiry with a fixed strike range and can differ from surrounding spot prints by a point or more.
- Reading VIX1D like VIX: it is one-day, event-driven, business-time variance (below VIX ~84% of the time); a high print before CPI is an event premium, not a trend, and after ~15:00 it is mostly tomorrow's variance.
- Pricing VIX options off a flat vol or off spot VIX: the smile slopes up in strike and the forward is the future.

## Contested or uncertain claims

- **VIX settlement manipulation**: Griffin-Shams (2018, RFS 31(4):1377-1417) find settlement-time volume spikes in exactly the OTM SPX options entering VIX, larger where influence per dollar is highest, and use put/call comparisons, open-interest patterns and a European volatility contract with a different settlement procedure to reject hedging and coordinated liquidity trading; they argue manipulation. Cboe and Saha-Malkiel-Rinaudo (2019, Journal of Asset Management) argue hedging/unwinding of VIX exposure through the replicating strip, with economically small deviations. Claims against Cboe in the VIX manipulation multidistrict litigation (N.D. Ill.) were dismissed in 2019-2020 while claims against unnamed traders proceeded [uncertain; not verified]. The method was not changed; the pre-announced strike range, zero-bid inclusion and auction prices are Cboe's mitigants. Practical stance: avoid holding VIX derivatives into settlement when the position is large relative to wing liquidity.
- **Was the 2024-08-05 print "real"?** The 65.73 high came at the cash open with very wide far-OTM put quotes; VIX futures and options priced far less, and critics argued the mid-quote index overstated tradable vol. Cboe's methodology does contain an index-level filtering algorithm (section 3.2; 0.50-point/2-minute thresholds) and a last-value republication rule, but whether and how they bound at that open is not public. Cboe defended the calculation. Lesson: VIX is quote-based and can decouple from tradable vol in disorderly opens.
- **Who caused Feb 2018?** BIS attributes the afternoon acceleration to mechanical ETP rebalancing plus anticipatory trading; others stress short-vol fund/retail positioning and dealer gamma hedging. Estimates of ETP rebalance demand (~200-300k contracts [uncertain]) exceed what BIS documents (115,862 in one minute). XIV's exact terminal values and termination timetable, and the ProShares leverage-change dates, were not verified from primary documents [uncertain].
- **Does structural option selling (0DTE, covered-call/put-write ETFs, vol targeting) cap VIX?** Fast reversions in 2024-2026 and a ~18 average VIX through a war and a hiking Fed in 2026 support it; the 52.33 close in April 2025 shows the cap is not binding in a true shock. Not settled (see 11-current-landscape-2025-2026).
- **Size of the forward VRP**: the computed +3.7 mean clusters in calm years and vanishes or reverses in 2018, 2020, 2022; the unconditional average overstates what short vol harvests net of tails and costs; academic estimates vary with sample, RV estimator and horizon, and overlapping windows inflate apparent precision.
- **VIX minus ATM IV**: the file quotes the common "1-3 points" but the linear-skew DDKZ term with typical calm-market slopes reproduces only about half a point to 1.5 points; the remainder, if real, comes from put-wing convexity. Treat the magnitude as [uncertain] until measured from a surface.
- **VIX futures beta to spot** (front ~0.5-0.7; capture ~50% for a 30-day constant-maturity index): practitioner regularity, not computed here; estimate from CFE settlements before relying on it.
- **Leverage-effect cause**: financial leverage (Black 1976; Christie 1982) vs volatility feedback (Campbell-Hentschel 1992) vs positioning flows; feedback/demand channels appear to dominate for indices [uncertain]. Practically, corr(return, dVIX) ~ -0.8 matters more than the cause.
- **Sticky strike vs sticky delta**: Derman (1999) proposed regime-dependent behaviour (sticky strike in ranges, sticky delta in trends, sticky implied tree in sell-offs). The working consensus is Bergomi's SSR ~1.5 for SPX (between sticky strike and "sticky local vol" behaviour, not sticky delta); it drifts in stress, so no single formula is exact.
