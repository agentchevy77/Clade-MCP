## Core concepts

- Leg: one option or stock line. Vertical: same expiry, different strikes. Calendar/horizontal: same strike, different expiries. Diagonal: both differ. Debit position: net premium paid (max loss = debit). Credit position: net premium received.
- Defined risk: worst case known at entry (debits, spreads, iron structures). Undefined risk: loss uncapped (naked calls, puts to zero, ratio front spreads).
- Breakeven (BE): expiry price where P&L = 0. Max profit/loss stated at expiry; before expiry P&L is mark-to-market, not the expiry payoff.
- Greeks as profiles: delta Δ (P&L per $1 move), gamma Γ (Δ change per $1), theta Θ (P&L per day, all else fixed), vega ν (P&L per IV point), rho (per 1% rates). "Short premium" = −Γ, −ν, +Θ; "long premium" the reverse. Book-level Greeks must be normalised before adding: beta-weight Δ to SPX, state Γ as $ per 1% move, Θ as $/day, and weight vega by tenor (ν × √(30/DTE) or a term-structure beta) — raw vega is not additive across expiries (a "flat vega" book long 2-year/short 1-week vol is heavily short front vol).
- IV: volatility implied by the market price. IV rank (IVR): position of current IV between 52-week low/high (0-100). IV percentile: share of look-back days with lower IV. Volatility risk premium (VRP): IV exceeds subsequently realized vol on average; the source of short-premium carry and of its negative skew. IVR is a first filter only; the edge is IV minus expected realized and the term-structure slope (Practitioner heuristics).
- Skew: index OTM puts trade at higher IV than equidistant calls, so put-side credit is rich, call-side poor; drives zero-cost collars and risk reversals. Choose strikes in delta space, not %-OTM: the 16-delta put is farther from spot (in %) than the 16-delta call, and skew steepens as spot falls (vanna), so short-put risk grows faster than model delta implies; compare sides via the 25-delta risk reversal.
- DTE: days to expiration; 0DTE: expires today. POP: probability of profit, a risk-neutral approximation (1 − credit/width for credit spreads), not a real-world probability. Probability of touch ≈ 2 × probability of expiring ITM (reflection principle for driftless Brownian motion; approximately true for lognormal with small drift): a 16-delta short strike is touched about one time in three and either side of a 16-delta strangle roughly half the time, so "roll when tested" rules fire on most trades; size and manage for touches, not expiries (Shreve, Stochastic Calculus for Finance II, §3.6).
- Extrinsic value = price − intrinsic. Early exercise is rational only when extrinsic is below the gain from exercising now (dividend for calls; interest on strike plus, for hard-to-borrow stock, the borrow fee for puts).
- Exercise/assignment: holder exercises; OCC assigns randomly to clearing members, who allocate to customers randomly or FIFO. Equity/ETF options are American and physically settled; SPX/XSP/NDX/RUT/VIX are European and cash-settled (no early assignment).
- Pin risk: at expiry with spot at a short strike, the writer learns of assignment only after the exercise cutoff and may hold an unintended position Monday.
- Buying-power reduction (BPR): capital the broker locks under its margin regime; the real cost of a credit trade. Rolling: close and reopen at another strike (up/down), expiry (out), or both; what matters is the net credit/debit of the two trades, not "avoiding" the loss.
- Margin regimes: Reg T strategy-based (FINRA 4210(f) rule tables), portfolio margin (PM; FINRA 4210(g), OCC TIMS stress of the whole account), SPAN (CME scenario margin for futures/futures options).
- Reg T spread eligibility (FINRA 4210(f)(2)(A)(xxxii)): same underlying; all legs American-style or all European-style; all listed or all OTC; equal aggregate contract value long vs short within each option type; short option(s) expire on or before the long option(s). Requirement on the short = lesser of the naked requirement ((f)(2)(E)) or the maximum potential loss from netting intrinsic values at every strike ((f)(2)(H)(i)); longs paid in full. Consequences: reverse calendars/diagonals (short dated after the long), the unpaired shorts of ratio spreads, and SPX-vs-SPY or SPX-vs-ES hedges (European vs American; listed vs futures) are not spreads and are margined naked under Reg T; PM/TIMS nets them.
- Reg T long options (4210(f)(2)(B)-(D)): options with nine months or less to expiry have no loan value and are paid in full; listed options with more than nine months (LEAPS) carry 75% maintenance loan value — relevant to PMCC/ZEBRA capital use.
- Section 1256 contract: tax category (regulated futures, nonequity options incl. broad-based index options, dealer equity options) taxed 60% long-term / 40% short-term, marked to market at year end.

### Strategy catalogue (construction / payoff / Greek profile and evolution / fit / management / failure modes)

- Long call: max loss = debit; BE = K + debit. +Δ +Γ −Θ +ν; OTM delta decays toward 0 and ATM theta grows ∝ 1/√T as DTE falls. Fit: bullish, IV cheap vs expected realized, move expected inside the window. Manage: take profit or roll up when Δ > ~0.7; buy more DTE than the thesis needs. Fails: right direction, wrong timing; buying after an IV spike; far-OTM "lottos" (negative EV after VRP and spread).
- Long put: BE = K − debit; max profit K − debit. Pays on price, not vol: beats VIX calls in slow grinding declines (2022), lags them in fast spikes.
- Short (naked) call: max profit = credit; loss unbounded above K + credit. −Δ −Γ +Θ −ν. Fit: bearish/neutral, high IV; rarely sensible for retail (takeover/squeeze gaps, call-side IV is cheap). Deep-ITM short calls are assigned the day before ex-dividend when extrinsic < dividend. Highest margin and approval tier.
- Short (naked) put: max profit = credit; max loss K − credit; BE K − credit. +Δ −Γ +Θ −ν. Expiry-equivalent to a covered call at the same strike (parity) but margin-efficient. Fit: neutral-bullish, IV rich vs realized, willing to own at K. Manage: 50% of credit or 21 DTE; pre-committed loss rule (Practitioner heuristics); roll down-and-out for a credit when tested; never roll for a debit unless deliberately converting to stock. Fails: crash (gamma, rising IV, margin expansion forces liquidation at the worst mark); early assignment deep ITM when rates are high or the stock is hard to borrow.
- Cash-secured put: same payoff with K × 100 in cash/T-bills; no leverage. Benchmarked by Cboe PUT.
- Covered call / buy-write (same thing; buy-write is entered as one order): long 100 shares + short call. Max profit (K − S0) + credit; max loss S0 − credit; BE S0 − credit. Net Δ 0.5-0.8, −Γ +Θ −ν. Strike choice: ATM (BXM) maximizes premium, 30-delta (BXMD) or 2% OTM (BXY) keeps upside. Cboe publishes no weekly buy-write benchmark; the monthly-beats-weekly result is for put-writes (PUT vs WPUT, Bondarenko 2016) and carries to covered calls only as a parity corollary [uncertain]. Manage: roll up-and-out when ITM if you want the stock; accept assignment when indifferent. Fails: opportunity cost in melt-ups; writing calls on stock you would not otherwise hold; dividend assignment; an ITM non-qualified call suspends or resets the stock holding period (Taxes).
- Covered-call / put-write ETFs (XYLD, QYLD, JEPI-style), the benchmark a junior is asked to beat: distribution yield is not return; NAV erodes in rallies; monthly ATM vs 30-delta OTM construction sets upside capture; realized performance tracks BXM/BXMD-type indices minus fees and strike choice [uncertain: fund-specific].
- Protective put: long stock + long put. Floor K − debit; upside less premium. +Γ −Θ +ν. Cost: a 5% OTM 1-month put rolled monthly costs several percent of NAV per year [uncertain: 2-5%]. Cboe PPUT benchmarks it. Israelov (AQR) "Pathetic Protection": because of the VRP, rolled puts cut drawdowns unreliably and cost more return than holding less equity at matched volatility.
- Collar: stock + long OTM put + short OTM call, often zero-cost via skew. Payoff bounded [K_put, K_call]; Δ ≈ 0.3-0.6. Cboe's roll page (read 2026-10-14) lists CLL3M ("3-Month Collar 95-110": put ≈ 95%, call ≈ 110%, rolled quarterly) and CLL1M ("Risk Managed Income": put ≈ 95%, call ≈ 100%, monthly); the legacy CLL description (3-month 5% OTM put + 1-month 10% OTM call) was not found on current pages [uncertain: current CLL methodology]. Tax: a straddle (loss deferral, holding-period reset; mixed-straddle rules if hedged with SPX puts, Taxes); a very tight collar on appreciated stock risks a constructive sale (IRC §1259) [uncertain: no bright line; practitioners cite ~15-20% bands].
- Vertical debit spread: buy near, sell farther strike. Max loss = debit; max profit = width − debit; BE long K ± debit. Lower ν/Θ than outright; Δ concentrates between strikes near expiry. Fit: directional view in high IV. Fails: short-leg assignment when ITM near expiry; two spreads to cross.
- Vertical credit spread: sell near, buy farther. Max profit = credit; max loss = width − credit; BE short K ∓ credit. −Γ +Θ −ν, bounded; the Reg T workhorse. Manage: 50%, 21 DTE, roll the whole spread out for a credit if tested and thesis intact, close before expiry if the short strike is near spot. Fails: tiny credits far OTM (risk/reward 10:1), gaps through both strikes, legging.
- Early assignment on verticals/diagonals: once the short leg is assigned you hold stock (or short stock) plus a long option, not a spread; choices are exercise the long (forfeits its extrinsic), trade out of the stock and keep the long, or let the broker auto-liquidate. A Friday assignment can create a Reg T call or a short-stock position carrying borrow and dividend liability over the weekend.
- Calendar: sell front, buy back, same strike (usually ATM). Max loss = debit. Profit peaks with spot at strike at front expiry. +ν (back-month), +Θ and −Γ near the strike, Δ ≈ 0. Fit: low IV expected to rise, or steep front-month event IV expected to collapse while back holds. Manage: 10-50% of debit (tastylive); close/roll the short at front expiry. Fails: large move away from strike; front IV rising relative to back; dividend assignment on an ITM front short; back-month IV also crushing after earnings. A reverse calendar (long front, short back) is not a Reg T spread and is margined naked.
- Diagonal: calendar with different strikes (adds Δ). Double diagonal: put diagonal below + call diagonal above = condor-shaped with +ν instead of −ν; max loss per side ≈ width − net credit.
- Long straddle: ATM call + put; BE K ± debit; loss = debit at K. Δ ≈ 0, +Γ +ν, largest −Θ of common structures. Cost ≈ the market's expected absolute move (≈ 0.8 SD), so profit needs a move beyond consensus. Fails: vol crush after the event; theta while waiting. Long strangle: OTM pair, cheaper, wider BEs.
- Short straddle: max profit = credit at K; BE K ± credit; unbounded. −Γ −ν +Θ. Fit: very high IV, range view, cash-settled index preferred. Manage: 25% of credit (tastylive), 21 DTE; roll the untested side in or invert. Fails: gaps; realized > implied over the life; margin expansion as a side goes ITM. Reg T: greater of the two naked requirements plus the other option's market value ((f)(2)(G)(i), Formulas).
- Short strangle: OTM call + put (the 16-delta "1-SD" 45-DTE convention; the 1-SD label is exact only without skew, and the put side's real-world touch probability in sell-offs exceeds the lognormal figure). Max profit = credit; BE K_call + credit, K_put − credit. Manage: 50%, 21 DTE, roll untested side in for credit; invert only if total credit exceeds the inversion width. Fails: as straddle; single-name earnings/M&A gaps.
- Iron condor: short strangle + long wings. Max profit = credit; max loss = wider wing − credit; BE short strikes ± credit. Smaller −Γ/−ν than a strangle. Cboe CNDR: monthly SPX, sell ~20-delta put and call, buy ~5-delta wings, T-bill collateral [uncertain: deltas; not on current Cboe pages]. Manage: 50%, 21 DTE; close or roll the tested spread, or add an opposing debit spread; do not roll indefinitely. Fails: tight shorts with wide wings (risk/reward ≥ 3:1); 0DTE condors erased by a 1% move.
- Iron butterfly: short ATM straddle + wings. Max profit = credit at K; max loss = width − credit; BE K ± credit; higher credit, narrower zone, Δ builds fast. Cboe BFLY: sell ATM SPX call and put, buy 5% OTM wings, monthly [uncertain: wing rule].
- Long butterfly: buy 1 lower, sell 2 middle, buy 1 upper, equal widths. Max profit = width − debit at middle; max loss = debit. Cheap low-probability pin bet; strongly +Θ/−Γ at the middle near expiry (0DTE flies, precise targets).
- Broken-wing butterfly: unequal wings, usually for a small credit so one side has no risk. Put BWB below spot: risk only beyond the wider far wing = (far width − near width) − credit. Fit: skew harvesting (the skew-rich alternative to a put credit spread), neutral-bullish. Fails: crash through the far wing; large Reg T BPR for the credit.
- Ratio (front) spread 1x2: buy 1 near, sell 2 farther, same expiry, near zero cost. Max profit = width + credit at the short strike; naked beyond it. Fit: high IV and skew (put 1x2 sells rich far puts), drift toward the short strike. Fails: gap through the shorts; the unpaired short is margined naked under Reg T.
- Backspread (sell 1 near, buy 2 farther): +Γ +ν; loss zone at the long strike at expiry (max loss width − credit); open-ended profit beyond. Put backspread = cheap crash hedge that loses in a slow drift to the long strike; call backspread = melt-up convexity.
- Jade lizard: short OTM put + short OTM call vertical with total credit ≥ call-spread width (no upside risk). Max profit = credit; downside K_put − credit. Fit: high IV, rich puts, neutral-bullish. Reverse jade lizard: short call + short put spread, credit ≥ put width; no downside risk, naked upside.
- Risk reversal: long OTM call + short OTM put (bullish) or reverse; near zero-cost via skew; stock-like outside the strikes, flat between. Cboe RXM benchmarks a monthly SPX version. Also a pure skew trade. Fails: short the crash; margined as a naked put.
- Synthetics: long call + short put (same K, T) = long stock less financing; reverse = short stock. Parity: C − P = S − PV(K) − PV(div) − PV(borrow fee); in hard-to-borrow names the synthetic forward trades below spot.
- Conversion/reversal: conversion = long stock + long put + short call (riskless, earns the implied rate); reversal is the mirror. Market-maker tools; risks are pin risk, dividend and rate changes, and in HTB names borrow recall (a reversal there is really a borrow trade).
- Box spread: long call spread + short put spread at the same strikes; payoff exactly K2 − K1, price = PV(K2 − K1). Selling a box borrows at the implied rate, buying lends; done in European SPX/XSP where it is safe, at rates near T-bill/general-collateral and usually below broker margin rates (basis of box-lending ETFs such as BOXX, inception 2022-12-27 per the issuer's fund page, read 2026-10-14). Tax: SPX box P&L is Section 1256 capital gain/loss, not interest — a short box's financing cost is a 60/40 capital loss usable only against capital gains (then $3,000/yr, IRC §1211(b)), so the after-tax cost depends on having gains to shelter; a long box yields 60/40 capital gain instead of ordinary interest, the basis of box-lending ETFs; this is a tax position, not a specific IRS ruling [uncertain]. American-style boxes are not riskless: a deep-ITM short leg can be assigned early. Robinhood, January 2019: a r/wallstreetbets user (handle "1R0NYMAN") sold a ~$300k-notional box on American-style options in a ~$5,000 account; the deep-ITM short puts were exercised early, leaving an unhedged position; loss > $57,000, peak exposure ~$212,500; Robinhood then barred opening box spreads. Wikipedia's "Box spread" article (read 2026-10-14) gives account size, loss and exposure only; the underlying (UVXY, a frequently hard-to-borrow ETF where early put exercise was near-certain) and the handle are from the original Reddit post and press coverage [uncertain].
- ZEBRA (zero extrinsic back ratio): buy 2 ITM (~70-delta) calls, sell 1 ATM call, same expiry; the short's extrinsic roughly cancels the longs', giving ≈ +100 Δ with near-zero net extrinsic and max loss = debit. Stock replacement with a floor (tastylive). Fails: wide ITM markets; worst outcome is spot at the short strike at expiry; the zero-theta property holds only near inception.
- Poor man's covered call (LEAPS diagonal): long deep-ITM call ≥ 1 year (70-85 delta), sell short-dated OTM calls. Max loss = debit. Rule: short strike + credit received must exceed long strike + debit paid, or assignment locks a loss. Fails: dividend assignment on the short; LEAPS extrinsic collapses in a crash.
- The wheel: sell CSPs until assigned, then covered calls until called away. A put-write/buy-write cycle that is path-dependent (holds losers, surrenders winners); gains are short-term unless shares are held > 1 year; calls below cost basis lock losses.
- 0DTE: (a) credit spreads/iron condors at 5-15 delta sold after the opening range, closed intraday or expired; (b) long OTM "lottos" on event/momentum days; (c) 0DTE butterflies at targets. Theta is gone within hours and gamma is extreme, so P&L is dominated by a few large moves. SPX/XSP: cash-settled, no assignment, SPXW settles to the 4:00 pm ET close. SPY/QQQ/IWM: options trade until 4:15 pm ET and holders can exercise on post-close moves until the broker cutoff (OCC's clearing-member deadline on expiration is 5:30 pm ET; retail brokers cut off between roughly 4:30 and 5:30 pm ET [uncertain: OCC page returned 403; broker times from recall]). Retail aggregate 0DTE P&L is negative on average (Empirical magnitudes). A same-day open and close is a day trade for the pattern-day-trader rule.
- Futures options (ES/MES, NQ), the institutional alternative to SPX: SPAN/SPAN 2 margin usually below Reg T and comparable to PM; Section 1256; nearly 23-hour trading; American-style but exercised into futures (no cash/stock delivery surprise); ES options are premium-paid while some CME options are futures-style margined [uncertain: product detail]. SPX Global Trading Hours (8:15 pm-9:25 am ET; Cboe, read 2026-10-14) allow overnight adjustment of SPXW/0DTE positions.
- Earnings: long straddle/strangle (realized jump > implied), short strangle/iron condor (jump < implied, collect the crush), calendar/diagonal (sell event IV in front, own back-month vol that crushes less). Jump-variance math in Formulas; state which "implied move" convention you use (straddle/spot ≈ expected absolute move ≈ 0.8 SD; sqrt(v_j) = 1 SD). Average pre-earnings straddle returns are negative with wide dispersion; retail earnings option trading loses on average [uncertain: magnitudes].
- Dispersion: short index vol (straddles/variance), long constituent vol in index weights; pays when implied correlation exceeds realized (correlation risk premium). Cboe COR1M/COR3M and the Cboe S&P 500 Dispersion Index (DSPX, 2023 [uncertain: month]) track it. Fails: correlation → 1 in crashes while short index vol.
- Variance swap: pays N_var × (realized variance − strike²); replicated by a 1/K²-weighted strip of OTM options (the VIX construction). Pure long/short vol with no delta hedging; convex in vol; single-name swaps capped (commonly 2.5× strike) after 2008 losses; variance strike > vol-swap strike by a convexity adjustment.
- VIX futures/options: VIX options are European, settle Wednesdays to the VRO opening quotation (last trading day the day before; Cboe spec, read 2026-10-14), and price off the matching VIX future, not spot; in contango the future sits above spot, so OTM calls look cheap versus spot but must clear the future. Long VIX calls: convex in spikes; carry cost from contango roll and premium; Cboe VXTH buys 30-delta 1-month VIX calls sized by the VIX futures level [uncertain: bands; not on current Cboe pages]. VIX calls beat SPX puts in fast, persistent crashes where futures follow spot (Feb 2018, Mar 2020). Aug 5, 2024 is the caution: spot VIX printed ~65 intraday and closed ~38.6 with SPX only ~8.5% off its high, while front-month VIX futures peaked far lower (in the 30s [uncertain: exact level]); VIX options mark off the futures, and the spot print was largely an artifact of wide SPX quotes at the open. Hedge value of VIX calls ≈ beta of the relevant future to spot VIX (front month ~0.5-0.7 in contango, higher in backwardation [uncertain]) × the spot move, minus roll. SPX puts win in slow bears (2022: SPX ≈ −25% peak-to-trough, VIX peak 36.45 close / 38.94 high). Short VIX harvests contango with a left tail (Feb 5, 2018: VIX 17.31 → 37.32, +115.6% per Cboe history; Wikipedia states +103.99% [discrepancy noted]; XIV terminated).
- Hedging a short-vol book with VIX products: size by vega notional (SPX option vega vs VIX-futures vega), apply the futures-to-spot beta and the roll cost; in backwardation (typically VIX > ~30) prefer SPX put spreads or VIX call spreads over outright front-month VIX calls, whose futures are already elevated [uncertain: thresholds].
- Tail hedging (Universa/Spitznagel): small allocation to far-OTM SPX puts (often 2-3 months), rolled systematically; a steady bleed for explosive payoffs that let the rest stay fully invested, improving compounded return. Universa-reported figures (via WSJ/Bloomberg/Forbes; not audited public data): >100% in 2008; ~20% in August 2015; an estimated 3,612% return on invested capital in March 2020 (investor letter), with a Forbes headline of 4,144% whose period Wikipedia does not state [uncertain: YTD through March 2020]. Counter-view (AQR: Israelov; Israelov & Nielsen "Still Not Cheap"): systematic puts are negative-alpha because of the VRP, protection is path-dependent, and diversification, trend-following or lower beta control drawdowns more cheaply. CalPERS dropped its Universa program in early 2020 just before the COVID crash.
- Put-write benchmarks: Cboe PUT (monthly ATM SPX put, 1- and 3-month T-bill collateral), WPUT (weekly), PUTR (Russell 2000), PUTY (2% OTM). PUT ≈ BXM by parity; historically PUT had slightly higher return and lower vol (roll timing, put skew) [uncertain: magnitude].

## Mechanisms

- Short-premium carry exists because hedgers and structured-product issuers pay for convexity and market makers demand compensation for gap/inventory risk; every "income" strategy (covered call, CSP, condor, wheel) is the same VRP exposure with a different delta and tail shape.
- Defined risk costs return: the long put wing is bought at higher IV than the short put (skew), while the call wing is usually bought at lower IV than the short call (index IV declines with strike across most of the OTM call range); net, condors earn less per unit of risk than strangles under PM but far more per unit of BPR under Reg T, which charges strangles as naked.
- Theta-gamma identity: Θ ≈ −½ Γ S² σ² (Black-Scholes PDE with r ≈ 0), so the theta/gamma ratio is roughly constant across strikes and tenors (−½ S² σ²); what changes near expiry and near the strike is the absolute level of Γ. Early management (50% / 21 DTE) is justified because (i) the ½ΓS²σ²dt approximation assumes small continuous moves — with large Γ a single gap produces losses far beyond what diffusive theta has paid for, so P&L becomes jump-dominated and left-skewed; (ii) the premium left to collect is small relative to the tail (a 45-DTE strangle at 50% has earned half the credit while Γ keeps rising ~1/√T); (iii) skew and rates break the identity in the direction of less theta per unit gamma for OTM puts. It is variance/tail reduction, not better theta-per-gamma (Hull ch. 19; tastylive "Managing Winners").
- Vol crush: an event adds jump variance to the front expiry; after the event it is removed, so front IV collapses even after a "large" move below implied; longer expiries dilute the jump and crush less (calendars exploit this).
- Assignment chain: holder exercises → clearing member → OCC random assignment → broker allocation → writer learns next morning. OCC auto-exercises equity options ≥ $0.01 ITM at expiry absent contrary instructions.
- SPX settlement: standard (third-Friday) SPX options are AM-settled; trading ceases the Thursday before (Cboe spec, read 2026-10-14) and the exercise-settlement value (SET) is the special opening quotation from each constituent's Friday opening print — not the 9:30 index print — which can gap 1%+ from Thursday's close, so a short strike "safe" Thursday can settle ITM with no way to close and Thursday-night news cannot be hedged. SPXW (Mon-Fri weeklies, end-of-month) and XSP are PM-settled to the 4:00 pm ET close and trade until expiration (1:00 pm ET on half days). Never carry near-the-money standard SPX into the Thursday close unless the SOQ outcome is acceptable.
- Dividend assignment: a call captures the dividend only by exercise before ex-date, rational when the dividend exceeds remaining extrinsic (≈ matching put + interest on strike). Conversion/reversal arbitrageurs exercise everything that qualifies the night before ex-date; ITM short calls on SPY/QQQ/high-yield stocks are assigned en masse. Calendar trap: SPY's quarterly ex-dividend date is the third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec — quarterly expiration day — so every ITM short SPY call in that expiry with extrinsic < dividend is assigned Thursday night; close or roll by Thursday afternoon. QQQ's ex-date is typically the Monday after quarterly expiration and IWM's varies; check the issuer calendar [uncertain: QQQ/IWM patterns from recall].
- Deep-ITM put exercise, two drivers: (1) interest — the holder gains K now; when interest on K over the remaining life exceeds the (tiny) extrinsic, exercise is optimal; the 2023-2025 rate level raised this risk for short deep-ITM puts. (2) Stock borrow — for hard-to-borrow stock (squeezes, meme names, post-IPO, biotech) exercising delivers stock without paying borrow, so deep-ITM American puts are exercised early even at low rates and often trade below intrinsic. Consequences: short deep-ITM puts in HTB names are assigned early; reversals/conversions there earn the borrow but carry recall risk; American-style boxes on HTB names are not riskless (Avellaneda and Lipkin 2009; Hull ch. 11). The market maker's most common early-assignment surprise is the borrow term, not the rate term.
- Pin risk: after-hours moves can flip ITM/OTM status and holders may exercise on them until the broker cutoff; writers hedging to expected assignment get caught.
- Contract adjustments: splits, special cash dividends, mergers and spin-offs change strikes, multipliers or deliverables (ordinary dividends are not adjusted); adjusted series with non-standard deliverables become illiquid and can strand one leg of a multi-leg position; OCC adjustment memos are the source of truth [uncertain: thresholds].
- Margin: Reg T charges each strategy from a rule table (naked options, spreads = max loss, covered calls none beyond the stock). PM reprices the whole account across a grid of underlying moves (TIMS) and charges the worst loss with cross-position netting, so hedged books use far less capital and naked books are charged nearer their true tail. Brokers add house charges above the FINRA/TIMS floor — implied-vol shocks, concentration add-ons, minimum per-position charges — so PM requirements exceed the rule minimum and jump in stress. SPAN scans price and vol shocks across 16 scenarios for futures/options on futures [uncertain: SPAN 2 migration status]. PM deficiencies: 3 business days to cure before new opening orders (other than risk-reducing) are barred and liquidation is required (FINRA 4210(g)(10)); brokers may liquidate sooner under house rules.
- Settlement and account mechanics: equities settle T+1 since 2024-05-28 (options already T+1); in a cash account, selling a position bought with unsettled proceeds before they settle is a good-faith violation (three in 12 months restricts the account); in a margin account four same-day round trips in five business days with < $25k equity triggers pattern-day-trader restrictions — the binding constraint on intraday 0DTE closes in small accounts [uncertain: broker enforcement varies].
- Incentives: brokers earn commissions and payment for order flow and favor volume (0DTE, spreads); market makers earn spread and VRP and hedge gap risk in ways that feed back into spot; FINRA/SEC margin and approval rules protect broker solvency, not strategy outcomes.

## Formulas and quantitative rules

```
Vertical credit spread: credit C, width W, short strike Ks
  max profit = C ; max loss = W - C ; BE = Ks - C (puts) ; approx POP = 1 - C/W
  e.g. W = 5, C = 1.20 -> max loss 3.80, POP ~ 76%
Iron condor: max loss = max(Wp, Wc) - C ; BEs = Kput_short - C, Kcall_short + C
Probability of touch ~ 2 x P(expire ITM) (driftless reflection principle; 16-delta strike -> ~32% touch)

Straddle: BEs = K +/- debit ; ATM straddle ~ 0.8 * S * sigma * sqrt(T)
  expected |move| (lognormal) = S * sigma * sqrt(2T/pi) ~ 0.8 * S * sigma * sqrt(T)  => straddle ~ expected absolute move (~0.8 SD, not 1 SD)

Earnings jump variance from two expiries T1 < T2 (both after the event):
  sigma_d^2 = (sigma2^2 T2 - sigma1^2 T1) / (T2 - T1) ; v_j = (sigma1^2 - sigma_d^2) T1
  jump SD = sqrt(v_j) ; straddle-implied (expected absolute) move ~ 0.8 * sqrt(v_j)
  e.g. 80% IV at 7d, 45% IV at 35d: sigma_d^2 = (0.01942 - 0.01227)/0.0767 = 0.0932 ; v_j = (0.64 - 0.0932)*7/365 = 0.01048
       jump SD ~ 10.2% ; straddle-implied move ~ 8.2% ; P(|move| > straddle-implied) ~ 42% under normality,
       so "the stock moved more than the implied move" is not evidence of mispricing

Box spread (European): price = (K2 - K1) e^{-rT} -> r = -ln(price/(K2 - K1))/T
  e.g. 100-wide 1-year SPX box sold at 95.60 -> r = 4.5%

Parity: C - P = S - K e^{-rT} - PV(D) - PV(borrow fee) ; conversion value = K e^{-rT} + PV(D)
Call early exercise (dividend D): exercise if D > P(K,T) + K(1 - e^{-r(T-t)})
Put early exercise: exercise if K(1 - e^{-r(T-t)}) + PV(borrow fee) > C(K,T) + PV(D)
Covered call = short put: S - C(K) = K e^{-rT} + PV(D) - P(K)

Reg T maintenance for a short uncovered listed option (FINRA 4210(f)(2)(E)):
  equity: premium + 20% of underlying - OTM amount ; floor = premium + 10% of underlying (call) / 10% of strike (put)
  broad-based index: premium + 15% of index - OTM ; floor = premium + 10%
  e.g. stock 100, sell 95 put at 2.00: 2,000 - 500 + 200 = 1,700 ; floor 950 + 200 = 1,150 ; requirement 1,700
  cash-secured put: 9,500 ; spread ((f)(2)(H)(i)): lesser of naked requirement or max loss = width - credit per spread
  short straddle/strangle ((f)(2)(G)(i)): greater of put-side or call-side naked requirement + market value of the other short
  e.g. stock 100, sell 95 put at 2.00 and 105 call at 1.80: put req 1,700 ; call req 2,000 - 500 + 180 = 1,680
       -> requirement 1,700 + 180 = 1,880 (not 3,380) ; recomputed daily, rises as either side goes ITM ; house multipliers on top
  long options: <= 9 months paid in full (no loan value) ; listed > 9 months: 75% maintenance ((f)(2)(B)-(D))

Portfolio margin (TIMS): charge = worst theoretical loss over the stress range (FINRA 4210(g)(2)(F))
  high-cap broad-based index +6%/-8% ; other broad-based +/-10% ;
  any other eligible product that is, or is based on, an equity security or a narrow-based index +/-15%
  (the rule does not name ETFs; products TIMS groups with a high-cap index, e.g. SPY, are stressed at the index range [uncertain: grouping])
  per-contract minimum $0.375 x multiplier ($37.50 per standard contract), capped at market value for longs ((g)(7)(B))
  $5M minimum equity only to carry unlisted derivatives ((g)(4)(C)) ; no FINRA dollar minimum for listed-product PM accounts (house floors apply)
  e.g. the 95 put above at -15% (stock 85): put ~ 10.3 -> loss ~ 830 -> PM charge ~ 0.8-1.0k vs 1.7k Reg T (before house add-ons)

Book aggregation: weighted vega_i = vega_i * sqrt(30/DTE_i) (or x term-structure beta) ; beta-weighted delta = sum_i delta_i * beta_i * S_i / S_SPX
  gamma in $ per 1% move = 1/2 Gamma (0.01 S)^2 * multiplier * contracts ; theta in $/day
Delta-hedged option P&L: dPnL ~ 1/2 Gamma S^2 (sigma_real^2 - sigma_imp^2) dt
  attribution: dV ~ Delta dS + 1/2 Gamma dS^2 + Theta dt + Vega dIV + Vanna dS dIV + 1/2 Volga dIV^2 (+ skew/term residual)

Variance swap: payoff = N_var (sigma_real^2 - K^2), N_var = N_vega / (2K) ; sigma_real^2 = (252/n) sum ln(S_i/S_{i-1})^2
  fair K^2 ~ (2 e^{rT}/T) sum_i (dK_i/K_i^2) Q(K_i) - (1/T)(F/K0 - 1)^2   (VIX formula)
  e.g. N_vega 100k, K 20 -> N_var 2,500 ; realized 25 -> 2,500 x (625 - 400) = 562,500 (vs 500,000 if linear in vol)

Implied correlation: rho_imp ~ (sigma_I^2 - sum w_i^2 sigma_i^2) / ((sum w_i sigma_i)^2 - sum w_i^2 sigma_i^2)
  dispersion P&L ~ N (rho_imp - rho_real) (sum w_i sigma_i)^2 to first order

Section 1256 blended federal rate = 0.6 LTCG + 0.4 ordinary ; e.g. 0.6*20% + 0.4*37% = 26.8% vs 37% short-term equity option gain
  add 3.8% NIIT (IRC 1411) to both where applicable, plus state tax ; net 1256 losses: elective 3-year carryback against prior 1256 gains only (Form 6781)
```

- A 45-DTE credit trade closed at 50% in ~15-20 days [uncertain: the tastylive page gives no day counts] earns similar P&L per day to holding to expiry with far less exposure to the last three weeks, where most large losses occur (tastylive backtests; caveats below).
- A credit spread whose credit is < 1/4 of width has reward/risk worse than 1:3 and needs POP > 75% to break even before costs.
- Delta-hedging practice for short premium: hedge on bands, not a clock — Whalley-Wilmott-type bands widen with transaction cost and shrink with gamma (half-width ∝ (cost × S Γ²)^{1/3}); hedge with the underlying/futures or the untested side, not new naked legs; attribute P&L daily into Δ/Γ/Θ/ν/vanna/volga so a loss can be traced to realized vol, skew or vol level (cross-link: pricing/Greeks domain).

## Practitioner heuristics

- Vol selection: use IVR/IV percentile only as a first filter. Condition on (i) IV minus realized vol (20- and 60-day) and minus a realized-vol forecast — the VRP is the edge; (ii) term-structure slope: in backwardation (typically VIX > ~30) front-month short premium fights rising realized vol and margin expansion, so prefer longer tenors, defined risk and smaller size; in steep contango the front month carries the most roll-down; (iii) skew richness for put-side structures. Caveats: IVR resets with the window (a spike 53 weeks ago drops out); IVR is high precisely when realized is also high (after a long calm IVR 50 can mean IV 12 against realized 8; after a crash IVR 20 can mean IV 25 against realized 35); "high IVR" after a crash is a buy-the-dip-in-vol regime only with capital to survive a second leg. Buy premium at low IV only with a catalyst.
- Enter short premium at 30-60 DTE (the "45 DTE" convention); exit or roll at ~21 DTE; manage winners at 50% (25% straddles; 10-50% calendars/diagonals/iron flies; the tastylive page confirms these ratios but gives no day counts and does not mention 21 DTE [uncertain: 21-DTE provenance]). At VIX > 35 the average IV−RV premium is largest, so holding longer is justified only with (i) size cut to the stressed requirement, (ii) defined risk or longer tenor to avoid the backwardated front month, (iii) acceptance that a second leg (Oct 2008, Mar 2020: VIX closes above 80) can follow. In single names exit before earnings regardless of DTE.
- Loss-side rule (convention, not alpha), pre-committed at entry: close or roll undefined-risk shorts when the loss reaches ~2× the credit received (tastylive convention for strangles/naked puts, i.e. the position marks at 3× initial credit [uncertain: page not retrievable]) or when the short strike is breached and the roll no longer yields a credit; for defined-risk spreads use max loss, or 100% of credit, as the stop. 21-DTE exits and untested-side rolls do not cap losses. A loss rule lowers mean P&L in backtests (it realizes losses that would have recovered) but cuts the tail that drives margin calls and ruin [uncertain: magnitudes].
- Size: BPR per undefined-risk position 1-5% of net liq. Size to the stressed requirement, not the entry requirement: recompute Reg T/PM at spot −10%/−20% with IV +50-100% (naked requirements commonly 2-3× entry in a crash as OTM shrinks and premium rises; brokers also raise house requirements intraday in stress, as in March 2020) and keep enough excess liquidity that the stressed requirement fits; total BPR ≤ 25-50% under Reg T is a starting point, not a guarantee.
- Defend a tested side in order: roll the untested side in (credit, lower Δ); roll the structure out for a credit; accept the loss. Never add risk to a losing undefined-risk trade in a trend; "rolling for a credit" on a deep-ITM short is a bigger position.
- Delta-neutral adjustment: a common desk rule is to act when |position Δ| in dollars exceeds ~2-3× daily theta or beta-weighted Δ drifts past your bias [uncertain: rule of thumb, no published support; calibrate to your own logs]; use the cheapest instrument (underlying/futures or the untested side), not new naked legs.
- Close vs expire: let shorts expire only if far OTM and cash-settled or pin probability is negligible; otherwise buy back for $0.01-0.05. Close ITM short equity options before 3 pm ET on expiry day; never carry ITM short calls through ex-dividend with extrinsic < dividend; for standard SPX, decide by Thursday's close.
- Time clock: market makers price time on a business/event-weighted clock (weekends and holidays low weight, event days high weight), so Friday IV marks typically dip and Monday marks rebound — the "weekend theta" a screen shows on Friday is mostly already in the price; the weekend gap risk is not [uncertain: magnitude varies by name and calendar] (Natenberg, Option Volatility and Pricing).
- Liquidity: penny-wide or ≤ 5% of premium markets, open interest in the hundreds, enter multi-leg positions as one complex order at mid and walk in ticks; "fills ~30-40% of the spread from mid in liquid names" is a desk rule of thumb — calibrate to your own fill logs [uncertain].
- Earnings: the ATM straddle ≈ expected absolute move (≈ 0.8 SD), not 1 SD; defined-risk selling beyond it is the common trade, but one or two misses a year erase many wins.
- Scheduled-event days (FOMC/CPI/NFP): IV crushes intraday after the print and 0DTE straddles reprice in minutes; "sell after the opening range" fails on these days — follow the events domain's microstructure notes rather than generic 0DTE rules (cross-link).
- 0DTE: assume every trade can hit max loss; sell after the opening range (30-60 min) on non-event days; stop selling on trend days; never average down; prefer SPX/XSP (cash settlement, 60/40, PM settlement to the close) over SPY.
- Hedging: slow bear → SPX put spreads or reduce exposure; fast crash → far-OTM puts or VIX calls sized by vega and futures beta (not spot VIX); cheap convexity → backspreads; budget the bleed and pre-commit a monetization rule (sell a fraction after +50-100%, roll strikes down).
- Taxable active traders favor SPX/XSP/NDX/RUT (60/40, mark-to-market, no wash-sale bookkeeping across accounts).

## Taxes (US, trader-relevant form)

- Section 1256: 60/40 blended rate and NIIT as in Formulas; year-end mark-to-market; net 1256 losses may be carried back three years against prior-year 1256 gains only (earliest year first; Form 6781 election; IRS Pub 550, read 2026-10-14). A §475(f) mark-to-market election made for securities does not automatically cover 1256 contracts (a separate commodities election exists), so traders usually keep 60/40 on index options [uncertain: practitioner reading of §475(f)].
- Which options are 1256: "nonequity options" — listed options that are not options on a single stock or a narrow-based stock index (IRC §1256(g)(3), (g)(6)) — so SPX, XSP, NDX, RUT and also VIX options (a volatility index: nonequity by definition, not a "broad-based stock index"), plus futures and futures options; SPY/QQQ/IWM options are equity options under the mainstream reading [uncertain: no IRS ruling specific to ETF options].
- Qualified covered call (IRC §1092(c)(4); Reg. §1.1092(c)-1 to -4; Pub 550): exchange-traded (or qualifying OTC) call on stock you hold; more than 30 days to expiration when written; term not more than 33 months; strike not below the "lowest qualified benchmark" (the highest strike below the prior day's close, adjusted: stock ≤ $25 → strike ≥ 85% of price; stock ≤ $150 → strike ≥ price − $10; calls > 90 days with strike > $150 → second strike below the close); not written by an options dealer. If qualified: no straddle loss deferral, but an ITM QCC on stock held ≤ 1 year suspends the holding period while the call is open, and a loss on the QCC is long-term if the stock gain would be long-term. If not qualified (ITM beyond the benchmark, < 30 days, or index/ETF-style positions): §1092 straddle — losses deferred to the extent of unrecognized gain on the offsetting position, and the stock's holding period (if not already > 1 year) restarts when the straddle ends.
- Straddles with index options: stock or ETF hedged with SPX puts (a collar) is still a §1092 straddle and a "mixed straddle" because one leg is a 1256 contract; absent an election the 1256 leg loses 60/40 (§1256(d)/§1092(b)); the identified-mixed-straddle and mixed-straddle-account elections (Reg. §1.1092(b)-3T/-4T) change netting and character and must be made in advance [uncertain: election mechanics].
- Wash sales (IRC §1091): see Common misconceptions; mark-to-market makes the question largely moot for 1256 contracts and futures are excluded [uncertain].

## Common misconceptions and pitfalls

- "Covered calls are income": they are short vol plus long stock; buy-writes returned about the same as or slightly less than the index with lower volatility over 1986-2015, and well below it in 2009-2024, not more.
- "Defined risk is small risk": a 50-wide condor sold for 5 risks 45; POP is risk-neutral and ignores VRP sign and slippage.
- "I'll just roll it": a roll out for a credit adds duration and often size; the loss is realized and re-bet.
- "Pennies in front of a steamroller" is incomplete: the VRP is real; leverage, not the trade, is the failure.
- "Boxes are risk-free": only European cash-settled (SPX/XSP); the 2019 Robinhood loss was an American-style box on a hard-to-borrow ETF.
- "0DTE is theta scalping": intraday theta is small relative to gamma; most 0DTE P&L is directional, and retail aggregate P&L is negative.
- "SPY options get 60/40": SPY/QQQ/IWM options are equity options under the mainstream reading; only nonequity options — broad-based stock index options (SPX, XSP, NDX, RUT) and other listed non-equity options such as VIX options — and futures/futures options are Section 1256 [uncertain: no IRS ruling specific to ETF options].
- "Wash sales ignore options": buying a call within 30 days before/after selling stock at a loss is a wash sale ("contract or option to acquire"); so is reopening a substantially identical option; losses replaced in an IRA are permanently disallowed (Rev. Rul. 2008-5).
- "My covered call keeps my long-term clock": an ITM non-qualified call makes a straddle and the stock's holding period restarts when the straddle ends (if not already > 1 year); an ITM qualified covered call excludes the days the call is outstanding (Taxes).
- "50% management always helps": it raises win rate and P&L/day in backtests but lowers P&L per trade and raises trade count; it is variance reduction, not alpha.
- "PM is always cheaper": PM beats Reg T for hedged books; concentrated naked small-cap positions can be charged more, requirements rise with IV, and house add-ons sit above the TIMS floor.
- "VIX calls hedge my book": they hedge vol spikes that reach the futures; a grinding 20-25% decline with VIX in the 20s-30s leaves them nearly worthless while SPX puts pay, and a one-day spot spike the futures do not follow (Aug 5, 2024) pays far less than the spot print suggests.
- "A multi-leg position is a spread for margin": only if it meets 4210(f)(2)(A)(xxxii); reverse calendars, ratio-spread excess shorts and SPX-vs-SPY/ES hedges are margined naked under Reg T.
- "It was OTM at Thursday's close": standard AM-settled SPX settles to Friday's opening SOQ, which can gap through the strike.

## Contested or uncertain claims

- Tail hedging: Universa/Spitznagel argue a small convex hedge raises compounded wealth and permits higher equity exposure; AQR (Israelov, Ilmanen, Asness) argue systematic puts are negative-alpha and trend-following, lower beta or diversification deliver similar drawdown control cheaper. Both rely heavily on 2008 and March 2020 and on strike/tenor/monetization choices.
- 50% / 21 DTE rules: tastylive backtests cover a few liquid ETFs/indices, mostly 2005-2020s bull markets, with simplified fills; critics cite in-sample parameter choice; whether 21 beats 14 or 30 DTE is not robust [uncertain].
- 0DTE and stability: Brogaard, Han and Won (2023) find higher intraday underlying vol; Adams, Fontaine and Ornthanalai (2024) "The Market for 0DTEs" find roughly balanced flow and limited net gamma impact; Dim, Eraker and Vilkov (2024) find dealer 0DTE gamma can amplify moves; Bandi, Fusari and Renò (2023) study 0DTE pricing. No consensus [uncertain: authorship/years/findings not re-verified].
- Weekly vs monthly put-writing: Cboe-sponsored work favors monthly (2006-2015); others argue weeklies harvest more premium and the gap is transaction-cost driven. No Cboe weekly buy-write benchmark exists, so the covered-call version of the claim is unsupported.
- BXMD vs BXM outperformance (1986-2015): upside capture in a bull market versus a persistent skew effect; post-2009 figures for all benchmarks are from recall [uncertain].
- Cboe index construction: CLL (legacy description vs current CLL1M/CLL3M), CNDR deltas, BFLY wing rule, VXTH weight bands and 2015 introduction dates could not be re-read (dashboard pages JS-rendered) [uncertain].
- Aug 5, 2024: the direction (VIX futures far below the 65 spot print) is well documented; the exact front-month futures high is not verified [uncertain]. VIX on 2018-02-05: Cboe history gives 17.31 → 37.32 (+115.6%); Wikipedia's VIX article gives +103.99%; the file uses the Cboe figure.
- Expiration cutoffs: 5:30 pm ET is OCC's clearing-member deadline; broker customer cutoffs are earlier and vary (OCC pages returned 403) [uncertain].
- Ex-dividend calendar: the two critiques disagreed (one named SPY only; the other SPY, IWM and QQQ on the third Friday). Resolution: SPY's third-Friday ex-dates are adopted; QQQ's ex-dates fall on the Monday after quarterly expiration and IWM's vary, so those were not adopted [from recall; issuer schedule not retrievable this session].
- Tax edges: whether broad-based-ETF options are 1256; whether wash sales apply to 1256 losses; §475(f) interaction; mixed-straddle election mechanics [uncertain].
