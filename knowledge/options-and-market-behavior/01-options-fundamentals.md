## Core concepts

- **Option contract**: standardized, exchange-listed, centrally cleared (OCC) derivative. **Call** = holder's right, not obligation, to BUY the deliverable at the **strike (exercise) price K** on or before **expiration**. **Put** = right to SELL at K. The **writer (short)** has the mirror-image OBLIGATION if **assigned**. A **series** = one root + expiration + strike + type; a **class** = all series on one underlying.
- **Premium**: price the buyer pays the writer, quoted per share (equity/ETF) or per index point (index). Dollar amount = quoted premium x **multiplier**. Standard multiplier = 100 (100 shares per equity/ETF contract; $100 per index point for SPX, XSP, NDX, RUT, VIX, DJX). Exceptions: Nanos (NANOS) $1 ("One Multiplier Options" in OCC rules); E-mini S&P 500 (ES) options on futures $50 per index point; Micro E-mini (MES) options $5. Contract **notional** = underlying level x multiplier.
- **Long vs short**: long = paid premium, max loss = premium, owns the right, no margin (paid in full). Short = received premium, max gain = premium, owns the obligation, posts margin. Four atomic positions: long call, short call, long put, short put. **Cash-secured put (CSP)** = short put with K x 100 cash reserved. **Covered call** = long 100 shares + short call. **Naked/uncovered** = short option with neither the deliverable nor reserved cash; margined under Reg T or portfolio margin (domain 09).
- **Moneyness**: call **in-the-money (ITM)** if S > K, put ITM if S < K; **at-the-money (ATM)** if S ~= K (nearest strike); otherwise **out-of-the-money (OTM)**. Quant measures: S/K, ln(K/F) with F = forward, standardized ln(K/F)/(sigma*sqrt(T)), or **delta** (|delta| ~= 0.50 ATM-forward; "25-delta" = |delta| = 0.25). The forward, not spot, defines moneyness: F = S*e^(rT) - FV(dividends) for an index; for VIX options F is the matching VIX future (Mechanisms). Rate and dividend inputs (SOFR/term rate vs box-spread-implied rate, SPX dividend stream) belong to domains 02 and 06.
- **Intrinsic value** = value of immediate exercise: call max(S-K, 0), put max(K-S, 0). **Extrinsic (time) value** = premium - intrinsic; bundles volatility value and **carry** (interest on K, expected dividends, stock-borrow cost). American options never trade below intrinsic in a functioning market (buy-and-exercise arbitrage). European options can price below spot intrinsic because exercise is deferred: a deep-ITM European put is bounded by K*e^(-rT) - S; a deep-ITM European call can be worth less than S - K when dividends before expiry are large.
- **Time value shape**: largest ATM, decays toward zero deep ITM and deep OTM. ATM time value ~ 0.4 * S * sigma * sqrt(T) (Brenner-Subrahmanyam approximation). ATM decay accelerates into expiry (rate ~ 1/sqrt(T)); far-OTM/ITM time value is mostly gone earlier, so its decay is front-loaded. Theta surfaces: pricing/Greeks domain.
- **Exercise style**: **American** = exercisable any business day through expiration; **European** = only at expiration (in practice, automatic cash settlement). All US listed equity and ETF options (AAPL, SPY, QQQ, IWM, ...) are American and physically settled. Cash-settled index options are European: SPX/SPXW, XSP, NDX/NDXP, RUT/RUTW, VIX, DJX, MRUT, NANOS. Exception: OEX (S&P 100) is American-style cash-settled; XEO is its European twin. Options on E-mini/Micro E-mini S&P 500 futures: quarterly (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) and serial-month options are American-style; Monday-through-Friday weekly and End-of-Month options are European-style (CME product calendar: "$50 European Weekly/Monthly"; CME Rulebook ch. 358A groups "European Style Weekly ... and End of Month Options"). All exercise into the corresponding futures contract.
- **Settlement type**: **Physical** = shares change hands (equity/ETF options; the stock leg settles T+1 since 2024-05-28). **Cash** = payout (settlement value - K) x multiplier; cash moves the next business day.
- **AM vs PM settlement** (index options): **AM-settled** = settlement value computed from the OPENING price of each index component on expiration morning; last trading day is the PRIOR business day. SPX third-Friday monthlies (settlement symbol **SET**), NDX monthlies (NDS), RUT monthlies (RLS), and VIX (symbol **VRO**, a Special Opening Quotation from SPX option opening prices) are AM-settled. **PM-settled** = official index close on expiration day: SPXW (weekday weeklies, EOM, quarterlys), XSP, NDXP, RUTW, NANOS. The PM value is the official closing index value built from constituent closing-auction prices and published shortly after 4:00 pm ET, not the 4:00:00 continuous print (Mechanisms). **Third Fridays carry BOTH settlement types**: since 2019-10-07 Cboe Rule 4.13 Interp .13 ("P.M.-Settled Third Friday Index Options", SR-CBOE-2019-092) allows PM-settled third-Friday series on SPX, XSP, SPEQF/SPEQX, RUT, MRUT, DJX, CBTX/MBTX and MGTN alongside the AM-settled monthlies; the PM series sit under the weekly roots (SPXW etc.), trade through Friday 4:00 pm and settle to the close, while the AM root stops Thursday and settles to SET. Key SPX-family data on root + date, never date alone. Rule 4.13(e)(1) also permits AM-settled weekly SPX expirations, though listed SPXW weekday series are PM-settled. SPY/QQQ/IWM options are keyed to the 4:00 pm close for auto-exercise but are physically settled American options, not index options.
- **Trading hours by product type** (ET; Cboe Rule 5.1): single-stock options trade the primary market's hours, 9:30 am-4:00 pm; designated ETF/ETN options (SPY, QQQ, IWM, ...) and broad-based index options trade to 4:15 pm (Rule 5.1(b): "beyond 4:00 p.m. but in no case later than 4:15 p.m."). **Curb** 4:15-5:00 pm for index options and 4:00-4:15 pm for designated equity options (Rule 5.1(d)). **Global Trading Hours (GTH)** 8:15 pm-9:25 am for SPX/SPXW, XSP, VIX and (since 2026-02-12) RUT/RUTW. Early-close days end at 1:00 pm for single-stock options and expiring SPXW, 1:15 pm for 4:15-close ETF/index options. Because ex-by-ex is keyed to the 4:00 pm official close, expiring SPY/QQQ/IWM options trade 4:00-4:15 pm against a known reference price (only after-hours exercise/DNE risk remains), whereas single-stock holders outside the 2026 extended-hours list have no option market after the reference print. Expiring SPXW/XSP series stop at 4:00 pm.
- **Expiration calendar terms**: **Monthly/standard** = third Friday (since Feb 2015 the expiration date is the Friday itself; earlier contracts technically expired the following Saturday). **Weeklys** = non-third-Friday expirations. **Daily expirations** = Mon-Fri series (SPXW and DJXW: EOW Friday plus Mon/Tue/Wed/Thu; SPY/QQQ Mon-Fri; NANOS Mon/Wed/Fri; Cboe's Available Weeklys page lists XSP as "EOW WED" [uncertain whether XSP Mon/Tue/Thu series are listed]). **0DTE** = any option traded on its expiration day. **EOM** = last business day of the month (SPXW, XSP). **Quarterlys** = last business day of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec (SPXW). **LEAPS** = long-term series: Cboe Rule 4.5(f) allows equity/ETF LEAPS expiring 12 to 180 months from listing (up to ten additional expiration months for SPY, six for other classes; new January equity LEAPS added no earlier than the Monday before the September expiration, 28 months out); Rule 4.13(b) allows index LEAPS 12 to 180 months (up to 10 expiration months). In practice equity/ETF chains run ~2-3 years and SPX lists December LEAPS roughly 5 years out [uncertain: practice, not rule]. **Holiday rules** (Cboe Rule 4.5 Short Term Option Series; Rule 4.13(e)(1)): a third-Friday or Tue/Wed/Thu/Fri expiration on an exchange holiday moves to the PRIOR business day; a Monday weekly on a holiday moves to the NEXT business day. On a Good Friday third Friday, equity/ETF and PM-settled index series expire Thursday, so AM-settled SPX/NDX/RUT monthlies last-trade Wednesday with SET computed Thursday morning (derived from the rules; check the exchange calendar). VIX holiday rule: Mechanisms. **Traditional cycles**: each equity class sits in one of three quarterly cycles (Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct, Feb/May/Aug/Nov, Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec); the two nearest calendar months are always listed plus the next two months of the class's cycle; weeklies and LEAPS are layered on top.
- **OCC (Options Clearing Corporation)**: founded 1973; SEC-registered clearing agency, CFTC-registered derivatives clearing organization, designated a Systemically Important Financial Market Utility in 2012; sole clearer for all US listed options exchanges (20 as of Oct 2026: Cboe x4, Nasdaq x6, NYSE x2, MIAX x4, BOX, MEMX x2 incl. MX2 Options launched 2026-09-14, IEX Options from 2026-10). OCC **novates** every trade (buyer to every seller, seller to every buyer), so counterparty risk is to OCC, backed by clearing-member margin and the clearing fund.
- **Exercise notice**: holder's instruction to exercise (customer -> broker -> clearing member -> OCC). **Assignment**: OCC's allocation of an exercise to a short position. **Exercise-by-exception (ex-by-ex)**: OCC's automatic exercise of expiring ITM options absent contrary instructions. **Contrary exercise advice (CEA)**, also called Expiring Exercise Declaration (EED): exchange-rule instruction to deviate from ex-by-ex (exercise an OTM option, or **do-not-exercise (DNE)** an ITM one). It exists for physically settled equity/ETF options and, formally, standard third-Friday index expirations; it does NOT exist for nonstandard-expiration index options (OCC Rule 1804(c), Mechanisms).
- **Position and exercise limits** (Cboe Rules 8.30, 8.31, 8.35, 8.42; same-side aggregation: long calls + short puts, short calls + long puts): equity options sit in tiers of 25,000 / 50,000 / 75,000 / 200,000 / 250,000 contracts by the underlying's float and volume (Rule 8.30). ETF exceptions (Rule 8.30 Interp .07): SPY 3,600,000; QQQ 1,800,000; IWM, EEM, FXI, EFA, IBIT 1,000,000; EWZ 500,000; DIA 300,000. Broad-based index options on SPX (incl. SPXW; XSP aggregated 10:1), NDX/XND, RUT, VIX, VXN, VXD, VXST, DJX, OEX, XEO, SPEQF/SPEQX and BXM have NO position limits (Rule 8.31(a)); other broad-based indexes carry exchange-fixed caps. Exercise limits equal position limits; hedge and facilitation exemptions exist. Customers holding 200 or more contracts on the same side of any class are reported to the exchange (Rule 8.43(a), Large Option Position Report). This is why institutional S&P 500 risk sits in SPX/FLEX rather than SPY, whose size has a hard ceiling.
- **FLEX options** (Cboe Rule 4.21; position limits Rule 8.35): exchange-listed, OCC-cleared options whose terms are set at trade time: underlying, put/call, exercise style (American or European; Asian- and Cliquet-settled series European-only and calls only), expiration (any business day up to 15 years out), strike, settlement (AM/PM, cash/physical; index multiplier 1 or 100). Used by defined-outcome ("buffer") ETFs, which roll SPX FLEX collars on fixed monthly/quarterly/annual dates, structured-note hedges and institutional collars. FLEX series do not appear in standard chains or most OI feeds, and nonstandard-expiration FLEX index options auto-exercise with no contrary instruction (OCC Rule 1804(c)). Treat "hidden" SPX positioning from FLEX rolls as a known dealer-hedging flow (domain 04).
- **Open interest (OI)**: contracts outstanding (one long and one short each). **Volume**: contracts traded in a session. **NBBO**: best bid/offer across all options exchanges. **Mark** = usually mid-quote. **Tick** = minimum price increment.
- **OSI symbol** (Options Symbology Initiative, 2010): 21 characters = 6-char root (left-justified, space-padded) + YYMMDD + C/P + 8-digit strike x 1000. Adjusted (non-standard deliverable) classes carry a numeral in the root (e.g., XYZ1).

## Mechanisms

- **Exercise style follows settlement type**: physically settled stock options are American because the deliverable exists continuously and early exercise has real uses (dividends, interest, borrow). Cash-settled index options moved to European style because American-style cash settlement creates a **"wildcard" option**: OEX holders could exercise after the 4:00 pm index value was fixed but before the exercise cutoff, capturing post-close information; Fleming and Whaley (1994) priced the feature and showed it made the writer's position unhedgeable. OEX (American) survives as the historical exception; Cboe rules still allow exercise of American-style cash-settled index options through 4:20 pm during a trading halt at or after 4:00 pm (C1 Rule Book, Exercise of Option Contracts, para (f)(3)).
- **AM settlement and SET**: SPX monthlies cease trading "on the business day (usually a Thursday) preceding the day on which the exercise-settlement value is calculated, 5:00 pm ET" (Cboe spec; i.e., through the curb session). Friday morning SET = S&P 500 computed from each constituent's first regular-session trade on its primary market. Because 500 stocks open at different times over several minutes, SET is neither the 9:30 index print nor Thursday's close; Cboe publishes it mid-morning once all constituents have opened. The expiring series cannot be traded after Thursday 5:00 pm, but the delta CAN be hedged overnight with ES/MES futures on Globex or with non-expiring SPX/XSP series in GTH (8:15 pm-9:25 am); the residual, unhedgeable risk is the **SET basis** (SET vs the 9:30 index/futures print): a few index points in normal conditions, far more on volatile opens (8:30 am macro releases, gap opens). VRO is built the same way from SPX option opening prices in a special opening auction (HOSS) whose rules exist because strategically placed opening orders can move it (Cboe VIX FAQ; RG18-015); Griffin and Shams (2018) document volume spikes in the OTM SPX series used in the SOQ at settlement time (Cboe disputes the manipulation reading).
- **PM settlement and the closing auction**: the SPXW/XSP/NDXP/RUTW settlement value is the official closing index value, built from constituent closing-auction prices (NYSE/Nasdaq MOC/LOC auctions) and published a few minutes after 4:00 pm. It can differ from the last continuous print, most on third Fridays (S&P quarterly rebalances effective after the close of the third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, "triple witching") and at month-ends (EOM series), when MOC imbalances are large. Expiring SPXW/XSP series stop at 4:00 pm, so the auction outcome cannot be traded in the expiring option; ES futures trade until 4:15 pm. PM settlement removes the Thursday-night gap but substitutes closing-auction basis risk (domains 04/05).
- **VIX expiration logic**: VIX options expire on the Wednesday 30 days before the third Friday of the following month, so the SPX options in the SOQ have exactly 30 days left; if that Wednesday or the SPX Friday 30 days later is an exchange holiday, settlement moves to the business day immediately preceding that Wednesday (Cboe Rule 4.13). Last trading day is the preceding Tuesday, with the expiring series trading through the Curb session to 5:00 pm ET (analogous to SPX monthlies on Thursday). Standard monthly VIX expirations settle to SPX monthlies; weekly VIX expirations settle to SPXW Friday weeklies (non-Friday SPX series are never used). Cash is paid the business day after expiration. **VIX options price off the VIX future, not spot VIX**: the only deliverable-equivalent is VRO on the settlement Wednesday, the same-expiration VIX future converges to VRO, and spot VIX is not tradable; hence C - P = (F - K)*e^(-rT) with F the matching future, moneyness is K vs F, and "intrinsic vs spot" is meaningless. In backwardation deep-ITM VIX calls trade below spot-intrinsic; in contango VIX puts price off a higher forward. Hedge VIX options with the matching future (or the replicating SPX strip), never with spot-VIX deltas (domain 03).
- **Weekday expirations and 0DTE**: SPXW lists Monday through Friday expirations; SPX Tuesday expirations launched 2022-04-18 and Thursday 2022-05-11 (Cboe release 2022-04-13); Cboe's November 2022 product update scheduled SPY and QQQ Tuesday weeklies from 2022-11-14 and Thursday weeklies from 2022-11-16 "subject to regulatory review" (SEC approved a parallel Nasdaq ISE filing 2022-11-09), giving a five-day 0DTE calendar in index and ETF form; IWM Tuesday/Thursday listings came later [uncertain date]. Every day an expiring series converges to intrinsic within hours, concentrating gamma intraday; dealer-hedging consequences belong to the microstructure/dealer-positioning domain.
- **Clearing and exercise flow (American equity option, any business day)**: (1) holder submits an exercise instruction by the broker's daily cutoff (often 4:00-5:30 pm ET; verify per broker); (2) the clearing member submits to OCC by OCC's clearing-member deadline (later than the customer cutoff); (3) overnight OCC randomly selects among clearing members with short positions in that series; (4) the clearing member allocates to customers by a FINRA-approved method, first-in-first-out (FIFO) or random (FINRA Rule 2360(b)(23)(C)), disclosed in writing; (5) the assigned customer sees the stock position before the next open; the stock trade is dated the exercise date and settles T+1. The same flow runs on non-expiration days (dividend and interest exercises), so a short option can be assigned any night.
- **Ex-by-ex thresholds (OCC Rules 805 and 1804)**: OCC auto-exercises expiring **equity options** ITM by at least $0.01 per share (Rule 805) unless the clearing member files contrary instructions. **Index options** are auto-exercised when the Exercise Settlement Amount (|settlement value - K| x multiplier) is $1.00 or more per contract (= $0.01 per index point at the 100 multiplier); for One Multiplier Options (Nanos) the threshold is $0.01 per contract (SR-OCC-2022-008, SEC 34-95364). For **standard third-Friday index expirations** (Rule 1804(b)) a clearing member may file contrary instructions. For **all nonstandard expirations**, i.e., weekly, monthly, quarterly, short-term/daily, EOM, FLEX and OTC index options (Rule 1804(c)), exercise at the threshold is automatic "by product design" and "Clearing Members do not have the ability to submit instructions to prevent the exercise"; neither DNE of an ITM contract nor exercise of an OTM one is possible. The after-hours CEA/DNE game therefore exists only for physically settled equity/ETF options (and formally for standard index monthlies). Thresholds are an administrative convenience between OCC and clearing members; brokers may apply their own policies to equity options (e.g., DNE a $0.01-ITM option that would create an unfunded stock position). The equity ITM test uses OCC's official closing price: the regular-session last sale on the primary listing market at 4:00 pm ET (OCC Information Memo 30048 "Underlying Prices for Expiration" [uncertain memo number; OIC says only "closing price"]), not after-hours prints; when the primary market is halted at the close, OCC applies special price procedures [uncertain details]. **Threshold history** (SEC 34-57163, SR-OCC-2007-18): September 2005, customer threshold $0.75 -> $0.25 and firm/market-maker $0.25 -> $0.15 (SR-OCC-2004-04); October 2006, $0.05 for all account types (SR-OCC-2006-05); $0.01 for all account types approved 2008-01-16, implemented by the June 2008 expiration.
- **Exercise cutoffs and after-hours assignment risk (equity/ETF options)**: all cutoffs are set relative to the RTH close in non-cash-settled equity options (Cboe C1 Rule Book, Exercise of Option Contracts, paras (c)-(d); FINRA Rule 2360(b)(23)(A)). Customer **final exercise decision** = close + 1 hour 30 minutes: 5:30 pm ET on a normal day (FINRA 2360(b)(23)(A)(iii); OIC's "4:30 pm CT" is the same deadline in Chicago time), 2:30 pm ET on a 1:00 pm early close. The firm's **CEA or Advice Cancel** must reach the exchange within 3 hours 30 minutes of the close (7:30 pm ET) for customer accounts; for non-customer (firm) accounts the 7:30 pm deadline applies only with electronic time-stamped procedures, otherwise within 1 hour 30 minutes (FINRA 2360(b)(23)(A)(iv)). The exchange may extend cutoffs case by case in unusual circumstances (volatility, wide spreads, system malfunctions) or, with notice by 1:00 pm the prior business day, shorten them. Brokers set earlier internal deadlines. The stock trades until 8:00 pm ET, so a call that closed $0.20 OTM can be exercised after a 5:00 pm rally; the writer learns of assignment after the fact and holds short stock into the next session. Symmetrically, an ITM holder can DNE after an after-hours collapse, leaving a writer who expected assignment with no stock and no hedge. Spread writers are exposed on both legs: the short leg assigned while the long leg (OTM at the close) expires leaves outright stock. None of this applies to SPXW/XSP/weekly index series.
- **Early exercise economics (American options)**: exercise forfeits remaining extrinsic value, so it is rational only when a cash flow from holding the deliverable exceeds that extrinsic value:
  - **Calls before ex-dividend** (Merton 1973): with no dividends an American call on stock is never optimally exercised early. With a dividend D, exercise on the last cum-dividend day (the business day before the ex-date; under T+1 the ex-date normally equals the record date) iff D > remaining extrinsic value, which by parity ~= same-strike put value + interest on K over the remaining life. Deep-ITM calls whose same-strike put is bid near zero get exercised. Writers of ITM calls face **dividend risk**: assignment the night before ex-date converts them to short stock that owes the dividend.
  - **Dividend play**: Hao, Kalay and Mayhew (2010) show a significant fraction of deep-ITM call open interest goes unexercised across ex-dates (a windfall to writers). Market makers exploit it by trading very large offsetting deep-ITM call volume with each other on the last cum-dividend day, exercising all longs; random assignment leaves each with fewer shorts assigned than longs exercised, capturing dividends on the public's unexercised longs. This inflates reported volume on ex-dividend eves. Exchanges accommodate it with per-day strategy fee caps (e.g., "dividend strategy" caps in the Cboe fee schedule), which make the trade cheap rather than limit it; when reading volume on the last cum-dividend day, exclude same-day round-trip deep-ITM call prints [uncertain whether any exchange rule has restricted the practice].
  - **Deep-ITM puts (interest)**: exercising a put converts stock into K cash now, earning interest on K for the remaining life. Exercise iff K*(1-e^(-r*tau)) > put extrinsic + PV(dividends before T) (dividends favor holding, since the stock drops by D). At 2023-2025 policy rates near 4-5% the exercise boundary sits far closer to the money than in the near-zero decade [uncertain: no empirical source on 2023-2025 exercise rates]. Barraclough and Whaley (2012) find more than 3.96 million puts (over 3.7% of outstanding), Jan 1996-Sep 2008, that should have been exercised early were not, costing holders ~$1.9 billion in forgone interest that market makers and proprietary firms systematically captured; Pool, Stoll and Whaley (2008) document the analogous call failures before ex-dividend and the resulting "trading game".
  - **Hard-to-borrow (HTB) stocks**: when stock-loan fees are high, deep-ITM calls held against short stock are exercised early to close the short and stop paying borrow (exercise iff borrow saved over the remaining life > extrinsic). Puts on HTB names trade rich relative to calls (parity embeds the borrow fee), so conversions/reversals look "mispriced" only if borrow is ignored. Avellaneda and Lipkin (2009) model this; Jensen and Pedersen (2016) show funding and short-sale costs can make early exercise of non-dividend calls optimal ("never say never"). Call writers on HTB stocks face early assignment exactly when borrow spikes (2021 meme-stock episodes).
- **Pin risk**: when the underlying closes at or very near K on expiration day, the writer cannot know until the next morning whether they were assigned, and the holder cannot know whether to hedge the stock they may receive. Ni, Pearson and Poteshman (2005) show optionable stocks' expiration-date closes cluster at strikes (return shift of at least 16.5 bp per expiration, ~$9 billion of market cap), attributed to market-maker delta-hedge rebalancing plus some manipulation; Avellaneda and Lipkin (2003) give the hedging-feedback model; Golez and Jackwerth (2012) find pinning in S&P 500 futures at option expirations. Cash-settled index options have no pin risk (nothing to deliver) but carry settlement-value risk.
- **Corporate action adjustments (OCC)**: the OCC Securities Committee issues an Information Memo per event (infomemo.theocc.com), aiming to keep long and short economically whole; every adjustment is decided case by case. Whole-number forward splits (2:1, 3:1): contracts multiplied, strike divided, deliverable stays 100 shares. Non-whole splits (3:2, 5:4): strike divided by the ratio, deliverable becomes 150/125 shares, contract count unchanged (non-standard deliverable; root gets a numeral, e.g., XYZ1). Reverse splits (1:10): strike and count unchanged, deliverable becomes 10 new shares. Ordinary cash dividends (paid under a regular quarterly or other periodic policy): no adjustment (priced in via carry). Non-ordinary/special cash distributions: adjusted (strike reduced by the per-share amount) only if worth at least $12.50 per contract, i.e., $0.125 per share for a 100-share contract (OCC By-Laws Art. VI Sec. 11A, effective for dividends announced on or after 2009-02-01; Nov-2012 supplement added the $0.125/share standard; OCC memo reproduced in SR-OCC-2025-017 Exhibit 5). Cash mergers: deliverable becomes a fixed cash amount, extrinsic value collapses, OTM strikes become worthless, and OCC accelerates expiration of all-cash-deliverable options (OCC Information Memo #30047, cited by OIC) [uncertain: exact accelerated-date rule]. Stock mergers: deliverable becomes N acquirer shares per the ratio (fractions cashed out). Spin-offs: deliverable becomes 100 parent shares plus the spun shares/cash, strikes unchanged. Delisted/bankrupt underlyings: options continue closing-only with the deliverable adjusted to the OTC shares [uncertain details]. Adjusted contracts trade alongside newly listed standard contracts of the same strike and are far less liquid.
- **Open interest update cycle**: OI changes only through opening/closing transactions matched by OCC overnight: open vs open +1, close vs close -1, open vs close unchanged. OCC publishes OI before the next open; every intraday OI figure is the prior close. Hence 0DTE OI understates the day's positioning (most 0DTE contracts are opened and closed the same day and never appear in OI), and volume >> OI signals turnover, not new positioning, until the next morning's print.
- **Quotes and ticks (Cboe Rule 5.4)**: quotes are per share/unit (bid x size / ask x size); the dollar spread per contract is 100x the quoted spread. Non-Penny classes (SPX, standard VIX series, single stocks outside the program): $0.05 below $3.00, $0.10 at/above. **Penny Interval Program** classes: $0.01 below $3.00, $0.05 at/above. SPY, QQQ, IWM, XSP (as long as SPY is in the program) and MRUT (tied to IWM): $0.01 at all prices; VIX series under the Nonstandard Expirations program (VIX weeklys): $0.01 at all prices; DJX and MBTX: $0.01/$0.05 tied to DIA and IBIT. SPX/OEX/XEO complex orders may trade in $0.05 or finer net-price increments set class by class [uncertain: verify Rule 5.33]. Penny Program mechanics (Rule 5.4(d); SEC-approved 2020, successor to the 2007 Penny Pilot): initial membership the 363 most active multiply listed classes (July 2020); each December OCC ranks classes on June-November cleared volume, any non-member in the top 300 (underlying priced below $200) is added on the first trading day of January, any member outside the top 425 is removed on the first trading day of April; newly listed classes can be added if they rank in the top 300 in their first full month. Wider ticks are why nominal SPX spreads look wide versus SPY while spreads per dollar of notional are comparable (heuristics).
- **Strike listing (Cboe Rule 4.5 and OLPP)**: base equity intervals $2.50 for strikes at or below $25, $5 above $25, $10 above $200 (a $5-above-$200 program covers up to five classes). Overlays: **$1 Strike Price Interval Program**, $1 intervals for strikes $1 to $50 in up to 150 designated classes, no additional $1 strikes once the stock closes at or above $50, no $2.50 strikes ($12.50, $17.50) in program classes (Rule 4.5 Interp .01(a), .12); **$0.50 Strike Program**, $0.50 intervals for strikes at or below $5.50 when the stock closed at or below $5, up to 20 classes (Interp .01(b)); SPY/QQQ/IWM/DIA/GLD at $1 or greater. **Short Term Option Series** (weeklies/dailies): $0.50 or greater below $100, $1 or greater from $100 to $150, $2.50 or greater above $150, with a tiered table by share price and ADV for single-stock weeklies more than 21 days from listing (Rule 4.5(d)(5)-(6)). SPX: 5-point intervals near the money (wider farther out); XSP 1-point. Strikes are added as the underlying moves within listing ranges; 2023-2025 rule changes capped far-OTM series to curb quote traffic [uncertain details].
- **Margin logic (summary; details domain 09)**: long options paid in full. Reg T naked short equity option: premium + 20% of underlying - OTM amount, floor 10% of underlying (calls) or of K (puts); broad-based index 15%/10% [uncertain: not re-verified against FINRA 4210 today]. Cash-secured put: 100% of K x 100. Covered call: nothing beyond the stock's Reg T. Defined-risk spreads: max loss. Portfolio margin (OCC TIMS) stresses the account across price scenarios (equities +/-15%, high-cap broad indexes -8%/+6% [uncertain exact ranges]) and requires far less for hedged books. Options on futures (ES, MES) are premium-paid ("stock-style") with SPAN margin on the short side [uncertain: CME pages unreachable].

## Formulas and quantitative rules

```
Payoffs at expiration (per share; x100 per contract):
  long call:  max(S_T - K, 0) - C        breakeven S_T = K + C
  long put:   max(K - S_T, 0) - P        breakeven S_T = K - P
  short call: C - max(S_T - K, 0)        (unlimited loss above K + C if uncovered)
  short put:  P - max(K - S_T, 0)        (max loss K - P if S_T -> 0)

Intrinsic / extrinsic:
  call intrinsic = max(S - K, 0);  put intrinsic = max(K - S, 0)
  extrinsic = premium - intrinsic   (>= 0 for American; can be < 0 for European vs spot intrinsic)

Put-call parity (European; r continuous; PV(D) = PV of dividends before T):
  C - P = S - PV(D) - K * e^(-r*T)
Forward form (index and VIX options; F = forward or matching VIX future):
  C - P = (F - K) * e^(-r*T)
American bounds with dividends:
  S - PV(D) - K  <=  C - P  <=  S - K * e^(-r*T)

Cash settlement payout (index):
  call = max(SV - K, 0) * multiplier ; put = max(K - SV, 0) * multiplier
  SV: SPX monthly = SET (Friday opening prices); SPXW/XSP = Friday official close (closing auctions); VIX = VRO (Wednesday SOQ)
  Auto-exercise: |SV - K| * multiplier >= $1.00 per contract (Nanos: $0.01); no contrary instruction for nonstandard expirations

Exercise cutoffs (equity/ETF options; relative to the RTH close C):
  customer decision = C + 1h30 (5:30 pm ET normal day; 2:30 pm ET on a 1:00 pm close)
  CEA / Advice Cancel to exchange = C + 3h30 (7:30 pm ET) for customer accounts; non-customer accounts C + 3h30 only with electronic time-stamped procedures, else C + 1h30

Early-exercise tests (approximate; tau = remaining years):
  call before ex-div:            exercise iff D > P(K, tau) + K * (1 - e^(-r*tau))
  deep ITM put:                  exercise iff K * (1 - e^(-r*tau)) > extrinsic(P) + PV(D before T)
  HTB call held vs short stock:  exercise iff S * borrow_rate * tau > extrinsic(C)

Open interest per contract traded:
  open vs open: OI + 1 ; close vs close: OI - 1 ; open vs close: unchanged

Spread cost per unit notional:  (quoted spread x multiplier) / (underlying x multiplier) = quoted spread / underlying level

OSI symbol: ROOT(6, space-padded) + YYMMDD + {C|P} + STRIKE*1000 (8 digits)
  "SPXW  261218P06500000" = SPXW 2026-12-18 6500 put ; "SPY   261218C00650000" = SPY 650 call
```

Worked examples (illustrative levels, not market data):

```
Dividend call: S = 100, K = 80, D = 1.00, ex-dividend date tomorrow (under T+1 the ex-date and record date
  normally coincide; the exercise must be filed by the broker cutoff today, the last cum-dividend day), 30 days left, r = 5%.
  interest on K = 80 * (1 - e^(-0.05*30/365)) ~= 0.33 ; same-strike put ~ 0.05
  1.00 > 0.38 -> exercise today; the writer should expect assignment tonight.

Deep ITM put: S = 60, K = 100, 90 days left, r = 5%, no dividend.
  interest on K ~= 1.23 ; put extrinsic ~ 0.10 -> exercise now.
  At r = 0.25% (2020-21): interest ~= 0.06 < 0.10 -> hold. Rates drive this decision.

Notional at an illustrative S&P 500 level of 6,500 (SPY ~ 650):
  SPX 100*6500 = $650,000 ; XSP = SPY = 100*650 = $65,000 ; ES option 50*6500 = $325,000 ;
  MES option $32,500 ; NANOS 1*650 = $650.  1 SPX ~= 10 SPY ~= 10 XSP ~= 2 ES ~= 20 MES ~= 1000 NANOS.

Spread per unit notional: $0.50 SPX spread = $50 per contract on $650,000 = 0.77 bp;
  $0.05 SPY spread = $5 on $65,000 = 0.77 bp (identical); a $0.30 SPX spread (0.46 bp) is tighter.

Ex-by-ex (equity): primary-market close 50.01 -> the 50 call is $0.01 ITM and auto-exercises unless DNE is filed
  by the broker cutoff (<= 5:30 pm ET). A 49.50 print at 5:15 pm lets the holder DNE; the holder who
  does nothing buys 100 shares for $5,000 (worth $5,001 at the close), funded T+1, plus commissions.
  A writer who paid $0.02 to close avoided both branches.
Ex-by-ex (SPXW): settlement value 6500.01 -> the 6500 call's exercise settlement amount is $1.00 per contract
  and it is exercised automatically; no DNE exists. Settlement 6499.99 -> expires worthless; no exercise possible.
```

Section 1256 (broad-based index options, options on futures, futures): positions are marked to market at year end and gains are taxed 60% long-term / 40% short-term regardless of holding period; a $10,000 one-day SPX gain = $6,000 LT + $4,000 ST. Equity options (single stocks and ETFs such as SPY, QQQ, IWM) are NOT Section 1256 contracts: holding-period capital-gain rules, wash-sale and straddle rules apply; a writer's premium is short-term gain when closed or expired; exercise folds the premium into stock basis/proceeds (IRS Form 6781 instructions; Pub 550).

## Practitioner heuristics

- Choose the product by mechanics first: no early assignment, no dividend risk, no position limits, 60/40 tax and overnight liquidity -> SPX/XSP; share delivery or penny ticks on small size -> SPY; futures-style 23-hour hedging and European weeklies -> ES options; SPX/10 with penny ticks -> XSP. Mixing SPX and SPY legs in one "spread" creates style and settlement-time mismatches (SPY legs can be assigned early, SPX cannot).
- Know which expiration regime you are in: SPXW/XSP/weekly index positions settle mechanically on the published value with no DNE and no exercise-OTM; equity/ETF positions are live until the 5:30 pm ET decision cutoff (2:30 pm on half days). Cutoff logic that is correct for SPY is meaningless for SPXW and vice versa.
- AM-settled SPX monthlies held through Thursday's close: hedge the delta overnight with ES/MES futures or GTH SPX/XSP; what you accept is SET basis risk, not an unhedged gap. PM-settled series (SPXW, XSP) remove the Thursday-night gap but carry closing-auction risk; on quarterly rebalance/witching days and month-ends, flatten expiring near-the-money index positions before 4:00 pm or hedge with ES into the close.
- Close or roll short equity/ETF options within about one strike interval of the underlying before the expiration close unless you want the stock. Pin risk is a sizing problem, not a forecasting problem.
- Dividend test for short calls: if the same-strike put's bid is below the upcoming dividend minus a few cents of carry, assume assignment the night before ex-date. Check ETF ex-dates too: SPY goes ex-dividend on the third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, i.e., on quarterly expiration day, so ITM SPY calls expiring that Friday are routinely assigned Thursday night; QQQ and IWM have their own quarterly ex-dates [verify each quarter].
- Deep-ITM American options: compare bid to intrinsic. If bid < intrinsic (common in illiquid names), exercise (or exercise-and-sell-stock) rather than hit the bid; if you cannot fund the shares, file DNE or close early instead of relying on the broker's expiration-day liquidation.
- Treat all intraday OI as yesterday's number; read positioning from volume and net premium intraday, OI only next morning. Never infer 0DTE positioning from OI. FLEX positions (buffer-ETF collars) are not in the chain at all.
- Compare spreads per unit notional and per unit vega, not in cents: a $0.50 SPX spread on $650,000 notional (0.77 bp) costs the same per dollar of notional as a $0.05 SPY spread on $65,000 (0.77 bp); a $0.30 SPX spread would be tighter, a $0.10 SPY spread wider.
- After hours: a short equity/ETF option that closed slightly OTM is not dead until the broker cutoff (~5:30 pm ET), and the firm can still file a CEA until 7:30 pm ET. With scheduled post-close news (earnings, index rebalances), buy back cheap shorts rather than "letting them expire". Single-stock options outside the extended-hours list have no market after 4:00 pm, so the buy-back must happen before the close.
- Rate regime: when short rates exceed ~3-4%, expect deep-ITM put early exercise and price American puts accordingly; near zero rates, early put exercise effectively disappears and dividend-driven call exercise dominates. Borrow regime: fees above ~10-20% annualized bring early assignment on deep-ITM short calls and rich puts; parity "arbitrage" that ignores borrow is a trap.
- VIX options: quote moneyness and parity against the matching VIX future, never spot VIX; a "below intrinsic" VIX call in backwardation is not a data error.
- Symbology: verify root (SPX vs SPXW, which on third Fridays distinguishes AM from PM settlement; adjusted roots with numerals), YYMMDD and strike x 1000 before sending; an adjusted-deliverable contract priced like a standard one is a classic fat-finger.
- These rules break in cash-merger names (extrinsic ~ 0, pinning irrelevant), halted stocks at expiration (OCC special price procedures), early closes (1:00 pm ET; cutoffs shift to 2:30/4:30 pm), and exchange outages near the close.

## Common misconceptions and pitfalls

- "Most options expire worthless (70-90%)": CBOE's 2011 count gives ~30-35% expiring worthless, 55-60% closed early, ~10% exercised; OIC's current figure is 22% worthless, >72% closed, 6% exercised; the 76.5% figure counts only futures options held to expiration. Even the correct count says nothing about writer profitability: contracts closed early can be closed at a loss, and dollar P&L is dominated by tails. Expiration-count statistics are not P&L statistics.
- "SPY options are just small SPX": they differ in exercise style (American vs European), settlement (shares vs cash), dividend risk, tax (equity vs Section 1256), ticks ($0.01 vs $0.05/$0.10), hours (4:15 pm ET close vs near-24x5), position limits (3.6 million contracts vs none), and expiration-day mechanics (close-based ex-by-ex with CEA/DNE vs mechanical index settlement with no contrary instruction).
- "I can DNE my SPXW/XSP 0DTE" or "my OTM SPXW can be exercised after hours": nonstandard-expiration index options are exercised purely on the settlement value at the $1.00/contract threshold (OCC Rule 1804(c)); contrary instructions exist only for equity/ETF options and standard third-Friday index expirations.
- "All single-stock options trade to 4:15 pm like SPY": single-stock options close at 4:00 pm ET (primary-market hours); only ETF/index options run to 4:15, plus the ~20 single-stock classes in the 2026 extended-hours program.
- "European options have no overnight expiration risk": AM-settled SPX/NDX/RUT monthlies and VIX stop trading the day before settlement; the SET/VRO basis is the risk (the delta itself is hedgeable with futures). "SPX monthlies expire at Friday's close": the AM root ends Thursday and Friday's open sets SET, but a PM-settled third-Friday SPXW series with the same date does expire at Friday's close. "VIX options settle to the VIX close": they settle to VRO, which can differ from Tuesday's VIX close by multiple points.
- "ITM at 4:00 pm means assigned, OTM means safe" (equity/ETF): holders may exercise or DNE until ~5:30 pm ET on after-hours moves; ex-by-ex uses the primary-market 4:00 pm last sale, not after-hours prices.
- "The exercise cutoff is always 5:30 pm ET": it is close + 1h30, so 2:30 pm ET on 1:00 pm early closes, and the exchange may extend it in unusual circumstances; the firm-level CEA deadline is 7:30 pm ET.
- "Assignment is random": OCC-to-clearing-member allocation is random; clearing-member-to-customer allocation is FIFO or random per the firm's FINRA-approved method, and under FIFO the oldest short positions go first.
- "The broker will always exercise my ITM option": brokers can DNE, or exercise and immediately liquidate, when the account cannot support the resulting position. "A $0.01-ITM auto-exercise is costless": it creates a leveraged stock position plus commissions over a weekend; many retail blow-ups are unfunded expiration-day exercises.
- "OI is live" / "0DTE OI reveals positioning": OI updates once overnight. "SPX OI shows all SPX positioning": FLEX positions are outside the standard chain.
- "Time value decays linearly" / "theta peaks on the last day for all strikes": only ATM theta accelerates into expiry; far-OTM options lost most of their value earlier.
- "Put-call parity holds exactly for American options": only bounds hold; dividends, early exercise and borrow create gaps. "An option can't trade below intrinsic": European options can (vs spot intrinsic); VIX options routinely do versus spot VIX because they price off the future; American marks (last trade, stale quotes) can show it even though live bids rarely do.
- "Ordinary dividends adjust the strike": only non-ordinary/special distributions of at least $12.50 per contract adjust; regular dividends are priced in via carry.
- "Weeklies are always PM-settled": true for SPXW/XSP/ETF weeklies; VIX weeklies are AM (Wednesday SOQ), and Cboe rules permit AM-settled SPX weeklies.
- "ES options are all American like stock options": weekly and EOM ES options are European; only quarterlies and serials are American.
- "A Monday holiday means the Monday weekly expired Friday": Monday weeklies move to Tuesday; only Tue-Fri expirations move backward.
- Governance note: statistics on how SPY reacts at the prior session's high/low are sealed for the pre-registered experiment and are deliberately absent from this file.

## Contested or uncertain claims

- **Cause of expiration pinning**: Ni-Pearson-Poteshman attribute strike clustering to market-maker delta-hedging feedback (Avellaneda-Lipkin mechanism) plus some manipulation; critics argue hedging effects are small outside heavily optioned names and that "max pain" has little out-of-sample predictive value. Index-futures pinning (Golez-Jackwerth) is accepted; its tradability is not.
- **0DTE and volatility**: Brogaard, Han and Won (2023) report 0DTE trading affects underlying volatility; Cboe and several dealer-desk analyses argue 0DTE flow is two-sided with no systematic effect on realized volatility. Beckmeyer, Branger and Gayda (2023) find retail 0DTE buyers lose on average; Cboe disputes the framing (retail ~47-57% of SPX 0DTE volume across 2025-2026 per Cboe Insights/Concretum). Unresolved.
- **Are early-exercise failures irrational?** Pool-Stoll-Whaley and Barraclough-Whaley read unexercised ITM calls/puts as investor error harvested by professionals; Battalio, Figlewski and Neal (2020, FAJ 76(1)) argue many apparent exercise-boundary violations reflect transaction costs, stale quotes and short-sale constraints. Jensen-Pedersen show frictions make textbook "never exercise early" rules wrong. Review resolution (2026-10-14): the Barraclough-Whaley "over 3.7% of outstanding puts" figure, questioned by one critic, appears in the published abstract (Crossref, DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2012.01752.x) and is retained.
- **Dividend play**: Hao-Kalay-Mayhew see volume inflation that transfers value from public holders; market makers call it liquidity provision that tightens deep-ITM call spreads. The earlier draft's claim that exchanges "limited it with fee caps and rules" was reversed on review: fee caps facilitate the trade; no restricting rule was verified [uncertain].
- **Expiry-outcome statistics**: the 2011 CBOE split (10/55-60/30-35) and OIC's current split (6/72/22) are unsourced aggregates from different eras and possibly different universes; neither publishes a methodology. "20-35% expire worthless" is the defensible range.
- **SET/VRO fairness**: Griffin-Shams (2018) read settlement-time SPX volume spikes as manipulation; Cboe attributes them to hedging/replication and points to the HOSS auction rules (RG18-015). Critics argue AM settlement should be retired; Cboe retains it for index-arbitrage and VIX-replication reasons (VIX needs SPX option prices at one instant). RG18-015 content and the VIX FAQ auction description were not retrievable today [uncertain].
- **Ex-by-ex price source**: whether OCC should incorporate after-hours prices is periodically raised; current rule uses the primary-market 4:00 pm last sale.
- **Section 1256 status of ETF options**: SPY/QQQ/IWM options are generally treated as equity options (not 1256); some practitioners argue broad-based ETF options could qualify, but mainstream practice treats them as equity options [uncertain]. Cboe markets Nanos with 60/40 treatment; confirm with a tax adviser.
- **ES option fine points**: European style of weekly/EOM series confirmed only from search excerpts of the CME product calendar and rulebook headings; 3:00 pm CT termination confirmed for Monday weeklies only; quarterly 8:30 am CT SOQ settlement and the 4:15-4:30 pm ET daily halt unverified (CME pages HTTP 503) [uncertain].
- **XSP weekday expirations**: Cboe's Available Weeklys page lists XSP as "EOW WED" while the spec page is silent; whether Mon/Tue/Thu XSP series exist is [uncertain].
