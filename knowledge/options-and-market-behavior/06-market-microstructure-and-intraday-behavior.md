## Core concepts
- **Limit order book (LOB)**: exchange queue of resting limit orders by price; best bid/ask ("top of book") plus depth. Matching is price priority then time priority. **NBBO** = best bid/offer across protected exchange quotes, consolidated by the **SIPs** (CTA: Tape A NYSE-listed, Tape B NYSE Arca/other-listed incl. most ETFs such as SPY; UTP: Tape C Nasdaq-listed).
- **Tick size**: $0.01 for NMS stocks ≥ $1 (Rule 612). **Round lot** (unit for protected quotes), tiered by price since 2025-11-03: 100 shares ≤ $250; 40 for $250.01–$1,000; 10 for $1,000.01–$10,000; 1 above (definition adopted in the Dec-2020 Market Data Infrastructure rules, Release 34-90610; compliance date set by the 2024 release). **Odd lot**: fewer than a round lot; historically excluded from the SIP NBBO.
- **Spread measures**: quoted = ask − bid; **effective** = 2·|price − midpoint| (what a marketable order pays); **realized** = effective measured vs the midpoint ~5 min later (what the liquidity provider keeps); **price impact** = effective − realized (loss to adverse selection).
- **Spread components** (Stoll 1978; Amihud–Mendelson 1980; Ho–Stoll 1981; Glosten–Milgrom 1985; Huang–Stoll 1997): order-processing cost, inventory risk, adverse selection. **Glosten–Milgrom**: bid/ask are conditional expectations of value given a sell/buy arrives; spread widens with the informed fraction. **Kyle (1985) λ**: price change per unit net order flow in a batch auction with a strategic insider; λ ∝ σ(value)/σ(noise flow).
- **Price impact**: temporary (decays after the order) vs permanent (information). **Square-root law**: metaorder cost ≈ daily vol × sqrt(size/ADV) (Almgren et al. 2005; Tóth et al. 2011). **Order-flow imbalance (OFI)**: net change in best-level bid minus ask depth over short windows; Cont–Kukanov–Stoikov (2014) show mid-price changes are approximately *linear* in OFI at second-to-minute horizons with slope ∝ 1/depth — the practical short-horizon impact model. **VPIN** (Easley–López de Prado–O'Hara 2012): volume-synchronized probability of informed trading, a flow-toxicity proxy; contested as a crash predictor (Andersen–Bondarenko 2014).
- **Execution benchmarks**: **VWAP**, **TWAP**, **implementation shortfall** (Perold 1988: all-in cost vs arrival price), **POV** (percent of volume).
- **Order types**: market; limit; marketable limit; IOC/FOK; day/GTC; stop and stop-limit; pegged (midpoint, primary); hidden/iceberg; post-only; **ISO** (intermarket sweep); **MOO/LOO**; **MOC/LOC**; Nasdaq **IO** (imbalance-only, liquidity-providing, executes only against the imbalance side, re-priced to the 4:00 Nasdaq bid/ask if more aggressive); NYSE **Closing D Order** (limit order that exercises discretion in the Closing Auction to a designated undisplayed price; **may only be entered by a Floor broker**; entry/cancel cutoff shortly before 4:00 [uncertain exact cutoff]); NYSE **Closing Offset (CO) / Closing IO** order (limit only; no validation against any imbalance publication; yields to all other closing interest; executes only against the opposite side of an imbalance, never adds to one; follows MOC/LOC cancellation rules; may be entered on either side until 4:00).
- **Auctions**: single-price call auctions at 9:30 and 4:00 crossing all eligible interest at the volume-maximizing price. NYSE: DMM-facilitated (D-Orders, Floor brokers). Nasdaq: fully electronic Opening/Closing Cross. **NYSE Arca** (primary listing venue for SPY and most ETPs): fully electronic, no DMM, no D-Orders; Early Open Auction 4:00 am, Core Open Auction 9:30, Core Closing Auction 4:00 pm, each preceded by an Imbalance Freeze Period (3:59–4:00 am; 9:29:55–9:30 am; 3:59–4:00 pm); the Arca **Official Closing Price (OCP)** prints with sale condition "M". Also halt and IPO auctions.
- **Volatility controls**: **LULD** per-security bands; **MWCB** keyed to S&P 500 declines; regulatory/news halts; **Reg SHO Rule 201** short-sale circuit breaker (NMS stocks incl. ETFs: a ≥10% decline from the prior close triggers a price test — short sales only above the current national best bid — for the rest of that day and the next).
- **Reg NMS (2005)**: Rule 610 (access, fee cap), 611 (Order Protection: bars *executing* a trade-through of a protected automated top-of-book round-lot quote unless an exception applies — ISO sweeps, benchmark/VWAP prints, flickering quotes, etc.; venues comply by routing away, re-pricing/sliding, or accepting ISOs), 612 (sub-penny), 603 (data). Sept-2024 amendments (Release 34-101070, File S7-30-22): $0.005 tick for tight-spread stocks, access-fee cap 30→10 mils, fee determinability, Rule 605 updates; they also *accelerated* the 2020 MDI round-lot tiers and odd-lot SIP data.
- **PFOF / wholesalers / internalization**: retail brokers route marketable orders to a few wholesalers (Citadel Securities, Virtu, G1/Susquehanna, Two Sigma, Jane Street, Hudson River [uncertain list]) who fill off-exchange at/inside the NBBO and pay for the flow; disclosed via Rule 606 (routing) and 605 (execution quality). Off-exchange trades print to a FINRA **TRF**.
- **ATS / dark pool**: FINRA-regulated off-exchange matching, mostly midpoint, no pre-trade display. **HFT**: latency-sensitive proprietary trading, mostly electronic market making and cross-venue/cross-asset arbitrage.
- **ETF primary market**: **Authorized Participants** create/redeem **creation units** (SPY: 50,000 shares) in kind against the index basket; **NAV**, **iNAV/IOPV** (every 15 s), premium/discount. SPY (1993) is a **unit investment trust**: full replication, no securities lending, dividends received are credited to a non-interest-bearing account and held by the Trustee without interest until the quarterly distribution (ex-date = third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, paid last business day of the following month), expense ratio 0.0945% (Form 497, 2026-01-27).
- **ES**: CME E-mini S&P 500, $50 × index, 0.25-pt tick ($12.50); **MES** $5 × index. **Fair value** = spot adjusted for financing minus dividends; **basis** = futures − spot. Quarterly expiries settle to the **SOQ** (special opening quotation: each constituent's own opening print, third Friday). **Daily settlement**: VWAP of lead-month trades in the 30-second window ending at the 4:15 pm ET futures close (14:59:30–15:00:00 CT, aligned with the 4:00 pm cash close since trade date 2020-10-26; the 15:14:30–15:15:00 CT window applied before that; CFTC filing rule092420cmedcm001). **BTIC** (Basis Trade at Index Close, since 2015) and **TACO** (Trade at Cash Open) are CME trades priced as the official cash-index close (or next open) plus a basis negotiated intraday, so closing-benchmark demand is split between equity closing auctions and BTIC, and the BTIC basis is a live read of close-demand pressure [launch year of TACO uncertain].
- **Sessions (ET)**: premarket 4:00–9:30; regular 9:30–16:00; after-hours 16:00–20:00; overnight ATS 20:00–04:00 (Blue Ocean, Sun–Thu); exchange "23x5" overnight sessions 21:00–04:00 planned from 2026-12-06.
- **Intraday regularities**: **J-shaped volume** (close ≫ open ≫ mid-day) and **U-shaped volatility** (highest in the first 15–30 min, second peak into the close); spread seasonality; **market intraday momentum** (first 30-min return predicts last 30-min); **overnight vs intraday decomposition**; lunchtime lull; 3:30–4:00 flows; intraday **periodicity of realized volatility** (Andersen–Bollerslev 1997) that RV estimators and intraday vol forecasts must de-seasonalize, and the **volatility signature plot** (RV vs sampling frequency) that reveals microstructure noise at sub-minute sampling.
- **Key levels**: support/resistance; prior-day high/low/close (PDH/PDL/PDC); overnight high/low (ONH/ONL, from ES/premarket); **opening range**; VWAP ± k·σ bands; **Market Profile** (Steidlmayer, CBOT 1980s): TPO distribution, **value area** (~70%), **POC**, **initial balance**; floor **pivot points**; round numbers; **option strikes with large open interest** (pinning, below).
- **Predictability by horizon**: intraday reversal; short-term reversal (1 week–1 month; Jegadeesh 1990, Lehmann 1990); momentum (12-1; Jegadeesh–Titman 1993); long-run reversal (3–5 y; De Bondt–Thaler 1985); **variance ratio** (Lo–MacKinlay 1988).
- **MDH** (mixture of distributions hypothesis): returns and volume share a latent information-arrival rate (Clark 1973; Tauchen–Pitts 1983; Andersen 1996).
- **Scheduled macro news (ET)**: 8:30 — CPI, PPI, NFP (first Friday), retail sales, GDP, weekly claims (Thursday); **10:00 — ISM manufacturing (1st business day) / services (3rd), Conference Board confidence, UMich final, JOLTS, new/pending home sales, Fed Chair testimony** (the main scheduled *intraday* vol spike other than FOMC); 11:30 — European cash close (volume bump, cross-asset flows); 1:00 — Treasury auction results (rates-led equity moves); 2:00 — FOMC statement (8 scheduled meetings/year, press conference 2:30) and FOMC minutes (three weeks after each meeting); earnings mostly pre-open or after 4:00. [Standing publisher schedules; not re-fetched this session.]
- **Calendar**: **XNYS** (ISO 10383 MIC for NYSE); ~9–10 full holidays/year plus 1:00 pm early closes; `exchange_calendars` Python library. Bond-market-only holidays (SIFMA closed, equities open: Columbus Day, Veterans Day) and SIFMA early closes are low-volume equity sessions.

## Mechanisms
- **Why spreads exist and vary**: a market maker quotes wide enough that gains from uninformed flow cover losses to informed flow plus inventory risk and fixed costs. Information asymmetry (open, news, fast markets) widens/thins quotes; where competition for passive fills is intense (SPY mid-day) the spread pins at one tick and competition shifts to queue position and rebates.
- **Price impact**: a sliced buy metaorder is partly detected (order-flow autocorrelation); providers raise quotes as they infer persistent demand; concavity (square root) arises because latent liquidity refills as price moves. At second–minute horizons the mid-price responds approximately linearly to OFI with slope ∝ 1/(touch depth) (Cont–Kukanov–Stoikov 2014), so impact per share is highest when the book is thin (open, news, overnight). Practitioners trade impact (faster = more) against timing risk (slower = more variance): the Almgren–Chriss frontier.
- **Opening auction and the stale cash open**: overnight information (futures, foreign markets, premarket news) is impounded in one print. NYSE disseminates opening imbalance/paired-off information from 8:00 am every second; from 9:30 DMMs open each security individually — algorithmically if it can open within 10% of the reference price, manually (minutes) beyond that. Nasdaq disseminates opening-cross NOII from 9:28. NYSE Arca runs a Core Open Auction at 9:30 after a 9:29:55 freeze (MOO/LOO cancel cutoff 9:29). Consequences: the published SPX cash index at 9:30:00–9:35 mixes opened stocks with prior closes of unopened constituents ("stale open") and lags SPY/ES; open-based gap statistics computed from SPX cash opens are biased, so use SPY/ES; the AM-settled SPX/VIX **SOQ** is built from each constituent's actual opening print and can differ materially from any printed 9:30 index level. The open has the day's highest adverse selection: widest effective spreads, highest realized volatility, large share of the day's information in the first 5–30 minutes.
- **Closing auction and why the close grew**: index funds, close-benchmark algos, derivatives hedgers, leveraged ETFs and mutual funds (NAV at 4:00) all want the official close, creating a liquidity externality. Timeline (ET, regular days; early-close days shift all times 3 hours earlier):
  - **NYSE (NYSE-listed stocks, i.e. SPY's constituents; Rule 7.35B)** — Auction Imbalance Information available to Floor brokers from 2:00 pm; DMM-initiated **Manual Closing Imbalance** publications 3:00–3:50 (informational only); MOC/LOC/CO may be entered, modified or cancelled for any reason until 3:50; at 3:50 a **Regulatory Closing Imbalance** is disseminated if ≥ 500 round lots (smaller ones only with Trading Official approval) [uncertain whether the 2024 proposal for a percentage-of-ADV test was approved]; after 3:50 MOC/LOC are accepted only on the contra side of a published Regulatory Closing Imbalance, and **all MOC/LOC are rejected if none was published**; **MOC/LOC/CO cannot be modified, cancelled or reduced after 3:50, even to correct a legitimate error** (sole exception: Rule 7.35B(j)(2)(B), a DMM-requested, Trading-Official-approved suspension after 3:58 when a legitimate error would cause significant price dislocation); CO orders accepted on either side until 4:00; informational imbalance (paired, unpaired, closing-only price, continuous-book clearing price) refreshed every second from 3:50; Closing D-Orders (Floor brokers) keep arriving until just before 4:00, so the 3:50 figure is partial.
  - **Nasdaq (Closing Cross)** — Early Order Imbalance Indicator from 3:50 (10-s updates), full NOII with near/far indicative prices from 3:55 (1-s updates); MOC entry until 3:55; LOC entry until 3:58, and an LOC entered 3:55–3:58 is accepted at its limit unless the limit is more aggressive than either the 3:50 or the 3:55 Reference Price, in which case it is re-priced to the more aggressive of the two; IO orders until 4:00, re-priced to the 4:00 Nasdaq bid/ask if more aggressive; after 3:50 on-close orders may not be cancelled or modified (the 2019 FAQ states this without exception; a legitimate-error cancellation window to 3:58 exists in Nasdaq rules [uncertain whether current]).
  - **NYSE Arca (SPY and most ETPs; Rule 7.35-E)** — no DMM, no D-Orders; MOC/LOC and limit orders accepted up to the **Closing Auction Imbalance Freeze Period 3:59–4:00 pm**, during which [verify in Rule 7.35-E] only interest that offsets the published imbalance is accepted and on-close orders cannot be cancelled; the OCP from the Core Closing Auction prints with condition "M" and will continue after 23x5 launches. ETF closing auctions are small relative to single-stock auctions because APs/arbitrageurs pin the ETF close to the basket close, so the NYSE 3:50 print matters for SPY only through its constituents.
  - The public imbalance predicts part of the final move, but offsetting liquidity (CO/IO, late LOC, D-Orders) arrives within seconds.
- **LULD**: continuous trading pauses when a quote would sit outside the band; reopening is by auction. Bands double in the last 25 minutes because closing flows are lumpy. Adopted after the 2010 Flash Crash (SEC–CFTC 2010 report; Kirilenko et al. 2017).
- **MWCB**: Level 1 (−7%), 2 (−13%), 3 (−20%) vs the prior-day S&P 500 close; Level 1/2 before 3:25 pm → 15-minute halt (no halt at/after 3:25); Level 3 → rest of day (under 23x5, reopen at the next day's 4:00 am session). Triggered four times under the current design (2020-03-09, -12, -16, -18, all Level 1) and once under the old points design (1997-10-27); not triggered in the April 2025 selloff (largest daily drops just under 6%).
- **Reg SHO Rule 201**: once a stock or ETF is down ≥10% intraday, shorts can only post above the NBB for the rest of the day and the next day, forcing short hedgers into passive orders — bids look "stickier" below −10% in single names and leveraged ETFs on crash days (2020-03, 2025-04).
- **Reg NMS and fragmentation**: Rule 611 trade-through protection, maker-taker rebates and fee caps produced venue proliferation (16 operating equity exchanges as of 2025; 24X and TXSE approved 2024–25 [launch status uncertain as of Oct 2026]), speed competition and complex order types. Internalizers match retail off-exchange at or inside an NBBO that excludes odd lots. The SEC proposed rescinding Rule 611 and the Rule 610(d) locked/crossed provisions (Federal Register 2026-06-17); outcome open.
- **PFOF economics**: retail marketable flow is low in adverse selection, so wholesalers fill it inside the spread and still profit, paying brokers for it; segregating retail flow raises adverse selection for remaining exchange flow (the "harms lit markets" argument). The SEC's 2022 Order Competition Rule and Regulation Best Execution proposals were formally withdrawn (Federal Register 2025-06-17).
- **HFT market making and arbitrage**: profits from spread/rebate on many small trades with millisecond inventory control; latency protects against being picked off when the NBBO moves. Cross-asset arbitrage (ES ↔ SPY ↔ sector ETFs ↔ constituents) transmits information from the fastest venue (usually ES) to everything else within milliseconds; Budish–Cramton–Shim (2015) show the ES–SPY correlation collapses at sub-second horizons, leaving a persistent stream of mechanical arbitrage opportunities.
- **ETF arbitrage and SPY–ES linkage**: SPY above NAV → APs buy the basket, create, sell SPY (reverse on discounts). Intraday hedgers use ES (cheaper, 23-hour, deep) and translate via fair value, so during RTH SPY ≈ ES − basis plus noise. Hasbrouck (2003) found the E-mini dominant in price discovery vs the big contract and SPY; the lead has shrunk to milliseconds but ES still typically moves first on macro shocks. **Dividend accrual**: SPY NAV ≈ index/10 **plus** accrued, not-yet-distributed dividends (held as cash/receivables in the UIT: $1.76bn cash and $280m dividends receivable at 2025-09-30), because constituents' ex-dividend drops lower the price index but not the trust's NAV; SPY/(index/10) therefore rises within each quarter and steps down on SPY's own ex-date, while ES fair value already nets expected dividends to expiry — so the SPY/ES ratio drifts within each quarter and naive comparisons mislead around SPY ex-dates.
- **Overnight sessions [as of 2026-10-14]**: Blue Ocean ATS (8 pm–4 am ET, Sun–Thu, only when the next morning's TRF opens) underlies Robinhood/IBKR/Schwab "24-hour" products; thin, retail-dominated, concentrated in megacaps/ETFs. Exchange 23x5: **Nasdaq** approved 2026-04-10 (Release 34-105199, SR-Nasdaq-2025-109), conditioned on market-integrity features (overnight price bands, firm-level controls to block client orders overnight, limit-order price protection, clearly-erroneous review procedures) set out in a Federal Register order of 2026-08-10 (per Jones Day/Nasdaq FAQ), with an overnight-session fee filing still pending; **NYSE Arca**: the SEC approved a 22-hour model (1:30 am–11:30 pm ET) on 2025-02-11 (Release 34-102400, SR-NYSEARCA-2024-89), then Arca filed an amended 9 pm–4 am framework on 2026-05-12 (SR-NYSEArca-2026-53; published as immediately effective in Release 34-105532 on 2026-05-21) whose launch the Aug-2026 NYSE FAQ still describes as conditional on SIP/plan approvals ("subject to SEC approvals"); **24X** describes its Market Session as "pending SEC approval" (FAQ dated 2026-03-01); Cboe EDGX and MEMX overnight proposals [uncertain status]; industry target launch Sunday 2026-12-06, contingent on SIP overnight operation and DTCC modernization; NSCC planned a 24x5 schedule (Sunday 8 pm–Friday 8 pm ET) from 2026-06-28 subject to regulatory approval [go-live not confirmed]; six industry tests 2026-10-02 to 2026-12-04. Design: overnight 9 pm–4 am ET, Sunday evening through Friday 8 pm; one-hour industry pause 8–9 pm (ATSs may stay open); NYSE Arca accepts orders from 8:59 pm; continuous trading only, no auction (the first auction remains the 4:00 am Early Open Auction); static ±20% "Overnight Price Bands" (LULD Plan amendment) computed by the listing market from the adjusted OCP and the adjusted consolidated last round-lot sale as of 7:45 pm ET (formula below; $3 minimum; leverage factor for leveraged ETPs); limit-order price protection, optional double-wide Pillar risk controls; no clearly-erroneous review when bands are in force; overnight prints carry a "T" condition and do not update last sale or the official close but do count in consolidated volume; trades 9 pm–midnight carry the next calendar day's trade date and clear one day later; member broker-dealers only; issuers cannot opt out; complex corporate actions (symbol change, splits, ≥25% dividends, spin-offs, mergers, de-SPACs) are halted from the prior evening until 8:00 am on the effective date (Nasdaq halt code M1); after a weekday holiday the overnight session starts at 9 pm that evening. Consequence: the "overnight gap" becomes a continuous path bounded by price bands rather than a single jump.
- **J-shaped volume, U-shaped volatility** (Wood–McInish–Ord 1985; Harris 1986; Jain–Joh 1988; Admati–Pfleiderer 1988): informed traders concentrate when discretionary liquidity traders concentrate, so activity clusters at the open (information) and close (benchmarking, hedging, rebalancing). The classic 1985–88 papers found a symmetric U; the post-2010 growth of closing-benchmark flow and VWAP/POV scheduling turned the volume profile into a J (see Empirical magnitudes), while realized volatility per unit time still peaks at the open because that is when information, not benchmark flow, is impounded. Spreads: wide at the open, narrowing fast, flat mid-day, slight widening/thinning into 3:30–4:00 in single stocks (McInish–Wood 1992); in SPY the quoted spread is one tick nearly all day, so seasonality shows in depth and effective/realized spreads.
- **Lunchtime lull**: lowest volume/volatility and thinnest depth ~11:30–13:30 ET (Wood–McInish–Ord 1985; Jain–Joh 1988); the *claim* of a higher false-breakout rate is plausible (little information arrival, compressed ranges) but undocumented [folklore; unsourced; test before use].
- **Market intraday momentum** (Gao–Han–Li–Zhou 2018, JFE 129(2):394–414; SPY 1993–2013): the first half-hour return measured from the prior close (so including the overnight gap) positively predicts the last half-hour return; stronger on volatile, high-volume, recession and macro-news days; present in ten other liquid ETFs [magnitudes not re-checked]. Channels: (a) infrequent portfolio rebalancing at fixed times (Bogousslavsky 2016 JF); (b) late-informed trading near the close; (c) **hedging demand** — Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens (2021, JFE 142(1):377–403) show in 60+ equity, bond, commodity and currency futures (1974–2020) that the last-30-minute return is positively predicted by the return from the previous close to 3:30, strongest where short-gamma hedgers (option market makers, leveraged ETFs) must trade with the move, and that it **reverses over subsequent days** — temporary price pressure, not information. Post-2013 persistence: present in futures through 2020 per Baltussen et al.; practitioner replications on SPY report attenuation [uncertain; no peer-reviewed consensus].
- **Overnight vs intraday ("tug of war")**: Lou–Polk–Skouras (2019, JFE 134(1):192–213): firm-level overnight and intraday returns each continue within component but reverse across components; most anomaly profits accrue entirely overnight or entirely intraday with opposite signs (institutional intraday vs retail/other overnight clienteles). At the index level the cumulative close-to-open return since 1993 far exceeds the open-to-close return (Cliff–Cooper–Gulen 2008; Knuteson 2018 arXiv:1811.04994, 2020 arXiv:2010.01727, 2022 arXiv:2201.00223). **Where the overnight return accrues** (Boyarchenko–Larsen–Whelan, "The Overnight Drift", FRBNY Staff Report 917, Feb 2020, rev. Aug 2022; ES 1998–2020): close-to-close ES returns are earned almost entirely between **2:00 and 3:00 am ET** (European open), ~1.48 bps/day ≈ 3.7% annualized, positive in 20 of 23 years (significant in 17), significant on every weekday; Asian hours and post-European-open to noon are flat-to-negative; the drift is larger after large negative US-session order imbalances (reversal after selloffs is robust, after rallies modest), consistent with inventory models in which futures market makers absorb the closing imbalance and unwind when European liquidity arrives; Bondarenko–Muravyev (2020, "Market Return Around the Clock: A Puzzle") replicate the concentration near the European open. Implication: the "overnight premium" is partly a dealer-inventory return with a specific clock time, not a diffuse risk premium, and subsample profiles (1998–2010 vs 2011–2020) differ. Other candidate explanations: (i) more news released outside RTH → overnight risk premium (Hendershott–Livdan–Rösch 2020); (ii) intraday selling pressure from leveraged-ETF/vol-targeting rebalancing and dealer hedging; (iii) opening-auction price pressure from overnight retail demand that reverts intraday; (iv) Knuteson's hypothesis that large funds' systematic "expand overnight, contract intraday" trading manufactures the pattern (not accepted by mainstream literature). Product test: NightShares NSPY (launched 2022, S&P 500 exposure held overnight only) closed July 2023 (last trading day 2023-07-31, Form 497); reported −6.9% vs +22% for the S&P 500 from inception and AUM ~$3.7m at closure [uncertain; performance figures not found in the filing]. Lesson: the average overnight premium is small relative to its variance, regime-dependent, and costly to isolate (two round-trips/day, basis noise).
- **3:30–4:00 flows**: (a) leveraged/inverse ETFs rebalance to target leverage at the close and always trade in the direction of the day's move (Cheng–Madhavan 2009), amplifying momentum into the close on big days — the Baltussen et al. hedging-demand channel; (b) MOC/close-benchmark orders, part of which is transferred into futures via BTIC rather than the equity auctions; (c) option dealers hedging expiring gamma/charm; (d) mutual fund flows at NAV. Bogousslavsky–Muravyev (2023, JFM 66) show closing-auction prices deviate predictably from pre-close prices and partially reverse overnight, and that the closing auction's volume share rose steeply over 2010–2018.
- **Opening gaps and "gap fill"**: a gap is the open vs the prior close (or prior range). Partial-reversal mechanisms: opening-auction price pressure, one-sided overnight retail flow, providers fading it. Continuation mechanisms: information gaps (earnings/macro) and the GHLZ result. The folklore "most gaps fill" depends on gap definition, size and horizon, is dominated by small gaps "filled" by ordinary noise, and has no robust peer-reviewed support; large news gaps fill less often intraday. No SPY statistics here by design.
- **Why key levels can matter mechanically**: (i) order clustering — Osler (2003, JF) shows with bank order data that stop-losses cluster just beyond round numbers and take-profits just before them, mechanically producing bounces at levels and acceleration once breached ("Support for Resistance", Osler 2000, FRBNY EPR); Donaldson–Kim (1993, JFQA) find DJIA price barriers at 100-multiples beyond chance. (ii) PDH/PDL/PDC and ONH/ONL are universally observable reference prices in auction-market theory (outside yesterday's value area = initiative activity, inside = responsive) and are where resting orders, stops and option-hedge triggers concentrate. (iii) VWAP attracts passive institutional flow because desks are benchmarked to it. (iv) Self-fulfilling coordination: Brock–Lakonishok–LeBaron (1992) found moving-average and trading-range-breakout rules on the DJIA 1897–1986 had predictive content; Sullivan–Timmermann–White (1999) show it mostly fails data-snooping adjustment after 1986; Lo–Mamaysky–Wang (2000) find some chart patterns carry marginal information but little economic value. (v) **Option-strike pinning**: Ni, Pearson & Poteshman (2005, JFE 78(1):49–87) show optionable stocks close on or near a strike on expiration Fridays far more often than chance, driven by delta-hedge rebalancing of market makers' net long gamma (short gamma produces anti-pinning/acceleration); Golez & Jackwerth (2012, JFE 106(3):566–585) find pinning in S&P 500 futures. With daily SPX/SPY expiries (PM-settled at 4:00) since 2022 the relevant strikes are those with the largest same-day open interest, the effect is strongest in the last hour when gamma/charm are largest, and its sign is conditional on dealers' net gamma (see options-positioning domain). Expect level edges to be small, regime-dependent and cost-sensitive; test with Reality Check (White 2000) / SPA (Hansen 2005).
- **Mean reversion vs momentum by horizon**: minutes–hours: bid-ask bounce and inventory give negative autocorrelation in transaction prices (Roll 1984) and reversals after liquidity shocks; Heston–Korajczyk–Sadka (2010, JF) find cross-sectional continuation at exact 30-minute multiples (recurring institutional flows). Daily index returns: positive autocorrelation in the 1960s–1990s (nonsynchronous trading; Lo–MacKinlay 1988) has fallen to ~0 or slightly negative since the 2000s as ETF/futures arbitrage removed stale prices and leveraged-ETF/vol-targeting flows added end-of-day reversal [uncertain magnitude]. Weeks–1 month: reversal. 6–12 months: momentum (skip the latest month). 3–5 years: reversal. VR(q) ≈ 1 for the modern index at daily–weekly horizons.
- **Volume–volatility (MDH)**: both load on information arrival, so volume spikes coincide with volatility spikes, volume is persistent and predictable from the time-of-day curve, and VWAP algos scheduled to that curve reinforce the J-shape.
- **Scheduled announcements**: 8:30 releases hit when only futures/premarket trade, so the 9:30 open absorbs much of the reaction; 10:00 releases land in RTH and produce the main scheduled intraday spike outside FOMC days; FOMC at 2:00 pm reprices ES instantly with a second move at the 2:30 press conference; volatility and volume spike for 30–60 minutes then decay. **Pre-FOMC drift** (Lucca–Moench 2015, JF 70(1)): ~49 bps average S&P 500 excess return in the 24 hours before scheduled announcements, 1994–2011, ~80% of the equity premium; Kurov–Wolfe–Gilbert (2021, Finance Research Letters 40) find it absent after ~2015 (sample to 2019); Hu, Pan, Wang & Zhu (2022, JFE 145(3):909–936) show the pre-announcement return is earned when VIX rises into the announcement and then resolves ("premium for heightened uncertainty"), so the unconditional drift is absent in low-uncertainty periods — condition any pre-FOMC prior on the VIX path rather than treating the effect as dead; Bernile–Hu–Tang (2016, JFE) document informed-looking ES flow before FOMC releases; Savor–Wilson (2013 JFQA; 2014 JFE) show announcement days carry a disproportionate share of the equity premium.
- **Calendar effects**: documented in the 1980s (French 1980; Gibbons–Hess 1981; Ariel 1990; Lakonishok–Smidt 1988); the Monday effect reversed/vanished after publication (Kamara 1997; Sullivan–Timmermann–White 2001); turn-of-month (last trading day through first three) is the most robust survivor (McConnell–Xu 2008) but small, flow-driven and not reliably exploitable after costs. **Index-event closes** (closing-auction outliers): S&P quarterly rebalances effective after the close of the third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec (coinciding with quad witching); Russell reconstitution **semi-annual from 2026** (June and December; 2026: effective after the close 2026-06-26 and 2026-12-11, rank day 2026-10-30, preliminary lists from 2026-11-13); MSCI reviews effective end of Feb/May/Aug/Nov.

## Formulas and quantitative rules
```
Spreads (M = midpoint, P = trade price, d = +1 buy / -1 sell):
  quoted_spread    = ask - bid
  effective_spread = 2 * d * (P - M)
  realized_spread  = 2 * d * (P - M_{t+5min})
  price_impact     = effective - realized = 2 * d * (M_{t+5min} - M)
Roll (1984):  s = 2 * sqrt( -Cov(dp_t, dp_{t-1}) )   (undefined if Cov >= 0)
Kyle (1985):  dP = lambda * Q,  lambda = sigma_v / (2 * sigma_u)
  Q = net order flow (shares); sigma_v = std of fundamental value; sigma_u = std of noise flow
Glosten-Milgrom: ask = E[V | buy], bid = E[V | sell]; symmetric prior, informed share pi:
  spread ~ pi * (V_high - V_low)  -> 0 as pi -> 0
Square-root impact (metaorder Q shares, ADV V, daily vol sigma_d):
  impact ~= Y * sigma_d * sqrt(Q / V),  Y ~ 0.5-1.0 (asset-dependent)
  Example: V = 60M, sigma_d = 1.0%, Q = 600k (1% ADV), Y = 0.7 -> 0.7 * 1% * sqrt(0.01) = 7 bps
  (temporary part decays; roughly half to two-thirds permanent [uncertain])
OFI (Cont-Kukanov-Stoikov 2014), per event n at the best levels:
  e_n = 1{b_n >= b_{n-1}} q_n^b - 1{b_n <= b_{n-1}} q_{n-1}^b - 1{a_n <= a_{n-1}} q_n^a + 1{a_n >= a_{n-1}} q_{n-1}^a
  OFI_k = sum of e_n over window k;  dM_k ~= beta * OFI_k,  beta ~ c / (average touch depth)
VPIN (Easley-Lopez de Prado-O'Hara 2012): VPIN = sum_{i=1..n} |V_i^buy - V_i^sell| / (n * V_bucket)
  (volume buckets, buy/sell split by bulk classification; contested predictor)
VWAP / TWAP / bands over window (p_i, v_i):
  VWAP = sum(p_i v_i) / sum(v_i);  TWAP = mean of equal-time slices
  sigma_VWAP = sqrt( sum(v_i (p_i - VWAP)^2) / sum(v_i) );  bands = VWAP +/- k * sigma_VWAP
Implementation shortfall (buy): IS = (sum(P_fill_j Q_j) + fees - P_arrival * Q) / (P_arrival * Q)
Futures fair value:  F = S * exp((r - q) * T)   (desk approximation: S * (1 + (r - q) * T))
  basis = F - S, > 0 when r > q.  Example: S = 6000, r = 4.0%, q = 1.3%, T = 60/365
  F = 6000 * exp(0.027 * 0.1644) ~= 6026.7  -> fair basis ~ +26.7 pts
  Observed basis = fair basis + implied-financing premium (dealer balance sheet, roll demand); see basis domain
BTIC price = official S&P 500 cash close (4:00 pm) + negotiated basis;  TACO price = next cash open + basis
ES daily settlement = VWAP(lead month, 30-s window 14:59:30-15:00:00 CT = 3:59:30-4:00:00 pm ET, since 2020-10-26; previously 15:14:30-15:15:00 CT)
ETF arb: trade when |P_ETF - NAV| > create/redeem cost + hedge cost + spread; SPY in RTH stays within ~1-2 bps of iNAV
SPY NAV ~= index/10 + accrued undistributed dividends/share  (ratio rises within quarter, steps down on SPY ex-date)
Leveraged ETF close rebalance (leverage L, AUM A, underlying day return r):
  notional = (L^2 - L) * A * r   (same sign as r for every L not in {0,1})
  L=+2: 2Ar;  L=+3: 6Ar;  L=-1: 2Ar;  L=-2: 6Ar;  L=-3: 12Ar
  Example: A = $20bn in 3x S&P products, r = -2% -> 6 * 20bn * 0.02 = $2.4bn of selling into the close
  (all US leveraged equity ETFs: ~$5-20bn per 1% move, industry estimates [uncertain])
LULD (Tier 1, > $3): band = ref * [0.95, 1.05]; ref = mean trade price over prior 5 min, updated after 30 s only if
  new ref >= 1% away; 15 s Limit State -> 5 min pause (extendable 5 min); bands doubled 15:35-16:00
Overnight Price Bands (23x5): upper = 1.2 * max(adj OCP, adj last round-lot sale as of 7:45 pm ET); lower = 0.8 * min(...)
MWCB: S&P 500 <= prior close * (1 - 0.07 / 0.13 / 0.20); L1/L2 before 15:25 -> 15 min halt; L3 -> rest of day
Reg SHO 201: trigger if low <= 0.90 * prior close -> short sales only above NBB, rest of day + next day
Variance ratio: VR(q) = Var(r_{t,q}) / (q Var(r_t));  VR(2) = 1 + rho_1;  VR(q) ~ 1 + 2 sum_{k<q} (1 - k/q) rho_k
Intraday momentum test (GHLZ): r_{15:30-16:00} = a + b * r_{prev close -> 10:00} + e;  H0: b = 0
Hedging-demand variant (Baltussen et al.): r_{15:30-16:00} = a + b * r_{prev close -> 15:30} + e, b > 0, reverses over days
Overnight variance share: s_on = Var(r_close->open) / [Var(r_close->open) + Var(r_open->close)]  (estimate in-house)
Intraday RV de-seasonalization: r_{t,i} / s_i, s_i = time-of-day volatility factor (Andersen-Bollerslev 1997)
Floor pivots (prior H, L, C): P = (H+L+C)/3; R1 = 2P - L; S1 = 2P - H; R2 = P + (H-L); S2 = P - (H-L)
Market Profile: POC = modal price (TPOs or volume); value area = narrowest band around POC with ~70%;
  IB = range of the first two 30-min periods (9:30-10:30 ET)
Opening range (n min): OR_high/OR_low over 9:30 .. 9:30+n; common n = 5, 15, 30
MDH: r_t | n_t ~ N(0, sigma^2 n_t), volume_t increasing in n_t -> Corr(|r_t|, volume_t) > 0, excess kurtosis
Session arithmetic (5-min bars, left-labelled, close minute excluded): full day 9:30-16:00 = 78 bars;
  early close 9:30-13:00 = 42 bars; LULD doubling starts at bar 15:35; NYSE MOC cutoff = bar 15:50; Arca freeze = bar 15:55
```

## Practitioner heuristics
- **Time-of-day budget**: 9:30–10:00 = information discovery (widest effective spreads, biggest moves, most false signals; cash index stale for the first minutes); 10:00 = scheduled-data slot (ISM/confidence/JOLTS); 10:00–11:30 = post-open trend/continuation window [folklore; unsourced; no peer-reviewed evidence of continuation specific to this window]; 11:30 = European close; 11:30–13:30 = low-information chop (range compression; "failed breakouts" [folklore]); 14:00 = macro slot on FOMC/minutes days; 15:00–15:30 = positioning; 15:35–16:00 = flow-dominated (LULD bands doubled, imbalances, leveraged-ETF rebalancing, dealer gamma/charm hedging, BTIC). Per-5-minute volatility in the first 15 minutes runs ~2–4× mid-day [uncertain].
- **Imbalance trading**: the 3:50 NYSE Regulatory Closing Imbalance / Nasdaq EOII is directional only when large vs ADV and not quickly offset; price moves immediately on publication and again as offsetting CO/IO/late-LOC and D-Order interest lands near 3:58–4:00; the exact within-window timing varies by name and day [folklore; unsourced]. **For SPY itself watch the NYSE Arca imbalance feed and the 3:59 freeze**; the NYSE 3:50 print is informative for SPY only through its constituents. On quarterly third Fridays, the semi-annual Russell reconstitution (June and December since 2026), S&P rebalances and MSCI reviews, auction volume is multiples of normal and closing-price dislocations partly reverse next morning (Bogousslavsky–Muravyev).
- **Leveraged-ETF rule**: on |index return| ≥ 1.5–2%, expect same-direction flow in the last 20–30 minutes, scaled by aggregate leveraged AUM (grew sharply 2023–26 [uncertain]) and partly pre-hedged by swap counterparties (often via BTIC), so visible impact is smaller than the formula.
- **Futures lead and settlement**: watch ES, not SPY, for the first move on news; ES trades Sun 6 pm–Fri 5 pm ET with a 5–6 pm ET daily break and a 4:15–4:30 pm ET halt; ES daily settlement is set at the 4:15 pm futures close [uncertain window], so "ES close vs SPX close" comparisons must use the settlement window or BTIC basis, not the 4:15 last print. ONH/ONL are conventionally from ES (or SPY premarket) between 4:00 pm and 9:30 am; convert with the live basis. The overnight drift literature says the ES overnight return is clock-time concentrated (2–3 am ET): do not treat the overnight leg as homogeneous.
- **Fair-value sanity**: "ES +0.5% premarket" implies SPY opens ≈ +0.5% only if basis is unchanged; around SPY ex-dividend dates (third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, when SPY steps down by the accrued dividend while ES does not) and ES rolls (volume migrates ~8 days before expiry) naive comparisons mislead; the observed ES basis also carries an implied-financing premium vs SOFR that varies with dealer balance-sheet cost (see futures/basis domain).
- **Open and SOQ hygiene**: use SPY/ES, never SPX cash, for 9:30–9:35 statistics; for AM-settled SPX/VIX expiries the SOQ is built from constituent opening prints and can sit outside the 9:30 printed index range.
- **VWAP use**: price above a rising VWAP = buyers paying up (trend day); repeated VWAP reclaims = balance day. VWAP bands describe dispersion of traded prices, not confidence intervals, so 2σ bands are touched routinely on trend days.
- **Market Profile triage**: small IB + one-directional range extension = trend day (do not fade); large IB with rotation inside = balance day (fade extremes toward POC); open outside yesterday's value area then acceptance back inside = "80% rule" folklore (mechanism plausible, evidence anecdotal).
- **Level selection**: fewer, universally watched levels (PDH/PDL/PDC, ONH/ONL, round numbers, large-OI option strikes on expiry days) attract the most resting orders, stops and hedge flow; the Osler mechanics imply a first touch often pauses price (take-profits) while a clean break accelerates briefly (stops) before reverting absent new information; pinning toward large strikes applies only when dealers are net long gamma. Treat levels as zones a few ticks to a few bps wide scaled by current ATR, and assume any level works only modestly better than chance.
- **Reversal vs continuation**: fade moves without volume/news support (liquidity shocks); respect moves with volume expansion and range widening (information). Use the GHLZ/Baltussen relation only as a weak prior on high-vol/high-volume days and expect multi-day give-back.
- **Halts**: never chase into an LULD pause (reopen is by auction); know MWCB thresholds (−7/−13/−20%) and the 3:25 pm cutoff; options exchanges halt concurrently; after a −10% day Reg SHO 201 constrains short entries for the next session too.
- **Announcement protocol**: for 8:30 data express views in ES before the open and SPY after 9:30; at FOMC, SPY option spreads blow out for ~1–2 minutes at 2:00 pm and a second move at 2:30 is common because the press conference is new information; whether the 2:00 move partially reverses by the close is regime-dependent and not established — do not use as a prior [folklore].
- **Execution**: ETF orders > ~1% ADV should follow the volume curve (VWAP/POV) rather than hit the open; avoid crossing the spread in the first minute; use the closing auction (or BTIC for futures-side exposure) for benchmark-insensitive size but expect auction-price noise on rebalance days.
- **Calendar hygiene / `exchange_calendars`**: `xcals.get_calendar("XNYS")`; `cal.schedule` is indexed by tz-naive session dates with tz-aware UTC columns `open`, `break_start`, `break_end`, `close`; `cal.is_session(d)`, `cal.sessions_in_range(a, b)`, `cal.session_minutes(s)`, `cal.next_open(ts)`, `cal.previous_close(ts)`; `cal.early_closes` (DatetimeIndex of sessions closing earlier than the prevailing normal close) and `cal.late_opens` exist, with `special_closes`/`special_closes_adhoc` as the underlying definitions; minutes are left-closed by default (`side="left"`: 09:30 is a trading minute, 16:00 is not), so start-labelled 5-min bars run 09:30–15:55 (78) or 09:30–12:55 (42); early-close `close` shows as 17:00/18:00 UTC (13:00 ET in EDT/EST); the regular open is 13:30 UTC in EDT and 14:30 UTC in EST. Default bounds are `GLOBAL_DEFAULT_START` = today − 20 years and `GLOBAL_DEFAULT_END` = today + 1 year (narrowed by a calendar's `bound_min`/`bound_max`), so pass explicit `start`/`end` for anything before ~2006 or more than a year ahead. `pandas_market_calendars` maintains its own "NYSE" holiday code (it can also wrap exchange_calendars), so the two can disagree on ad hoc closures — reconcile before mixing.

## Common misconceptions and pitfalls
- "The closing auction is a third of all volume." Only on reconstitution/expiry days; typical days ~9–10% of consolidated volume (2024). The last 30 minutes *including* the auction can approach a third.
- "SPY's close is set by the NYSE closing auction." SPY is NYSE Arca-listed: its OCP comes from the Arca Core Closing Auction (3:59 freeze, no DMM, no D-Orders); NYSE 3:50 mechanics apply to its constituents.
- "The index open is the 9:30 print." Cash indices are stale for the first minutes while DMMs open stocks one by one; SPX AM SOQs come from constituent opening prints.
- "Volume is U-shaped." It is J-shaped (close ≫ open); volatility is the U.
- "Dark pools have taken over." ATS share is roughly flat since 2019; the >50% off-exchange share comes from wholesaler internalization and other bilateral trading.
- "Half-penny ticks are live." Round-lot tiers are (Nov 2025); the $0.005 tick and 10-mil fee cap are deferred to at least Nov 2027.
- "Odd lots are invisible." Direct feeds always showed them; odd-lot *trades* have printed to the SIP since 2013-12-09 with sale condition "I" and do not update last sale/high/low (include them in volume, not in last-price series, unless deliberately chosen); SIP dissemination of odd-lot *quotes* had a May-2026 compliance date [go-live uncertain]; the protected NBBO still uses (tiered) round lots.
- "Rule 611 guarantees best execution." It bars trade-throughs of protected top-of-book quotes (with ISO and other exceptions); best execution is a broker duty (FINRA 5310), and Rule 611 itself is proposed for rescission.
- "The 3:50 imbalance tells you the close." It is a partial, rapidly offset snapshot; CO/IO orders, late LOCs and D-Orders arrive after it, and on NYSE nothing can be cancelled after 3:50 (not even for error), so the offsetting side is the only thing that changes.
- "Most gaps fill." Definition-dependent folklore; small gaps fill by noise, information gaps often do not; the published intraday-momentum evidence points the other way for first→last half hour.
- "Overnight drift is free money." Small premium vs variance, regime-dependent, concentrated in one European-open hour (inventory return); NSPY lost money in a rising market [uncertain figures]; futures implementations pay basis noise and two round-trips a day.
- "Pre-FOMC drift is dead." Unconditionally absent 2015–2019, but conditional on a VIX run-up into the announcement (Hu–Pan–Wang–Zhu 2022); condition, do not discard.
- "Support/resistance works because of psychology alone." The documented channels are order clustering (stops/take-profits) and, at strikes, dealer delta-hedging.
- "VWAP is a magnet." It attracts passive institutional flow; price crosses it repeatedly on balance days and ignores it on trend days.
- "Index daily returns are positively autocorrelated." That was the pre-2000 regime (nonsynchronous trading); modern index autocorrelation is ~0 to slightly negative.
- "HFT = front-running." Legal HFT is latency arbitrage and market making on public data; the welfare debate concerns the arms race and adverse selection.
- "LULD halts the market." LULD is per security; only MWCB halts everything, at −7/−13/−20%.
- "Overnight exchange trades set the official close/open." Overnight prints carry a "T" condition, are not last-sale eligible, and there is no overnight auction.
- "Half-days are just shorter." MOC cutoffs move to 12:50, LULD doubling to 12:35, Arca freeze to 12:59, volume and option liquidity drop, bar counts change (42 vs 78).
- Data pitfalls: SIP vs direct-feed timestamps differ by milliseconds; consolidated "last" includes off-exchange prints; auction prints carry condition codes ("M" for the Arca OCP) and get mis-assigned to bars; SPY dividends must be handled in close-to-open returns (and the within-quarter accrual in SPY/ES ratios); "T" overnight prints must be excluded from RTH statistics; odd-lot "I" prints must be treated consistently; RV estimators must de-seasonalize the intraday periodicity and respect the volatility signature at sub-minute sampling; multi-day gaps (weekends/holidays) inflate the overnight leg; backfilled calendars sometimes miss ad hoc closures.

## Contested or uncertain claims
- **Overnight-return puzzle**: risk/news timing (Hendershott et al.), clientele/flow (Lou–Polk–Skouras), dealer-inventory return at the European open (Boyarchenko–Larsen–Whelan), opening-auction price pressure, vs Knuteson's "manipulation by large funds"; mainstream finance rejects the last; none explains the full magnitude; post-2020 persistence unclear.
- **Intraday momentum persistence**: in-sample through 2013 (GHLZ); futures evidence through 2020 with subsequent-day reversal (Baltussen et al.); practitioner replications on SPY report attenuation after publication [uncertain].
- **NYSE post-3:50 cancellation (critic disagreement, resolved)**: one critique claimed a 3:50–3:58 legitimate-error cancellation window on NYSE; NYSE RM-22-12 (2022-09-20) and the 2024 fact sheet say MOC/LOC/Closing IO "may not be cancelled or reduced in size, even to correct a legitimate error, after 3:50 p.m." (sole relief: DMM-requested Rule 7.35B(j)(2)(B) suspension after 3:58). The legitimate-error window is a Nasdaq (and pre-Pillar NYSE Rule 123C) feature; file follows the primary documents.
- **Tick/fee deferral (critic disagreement, resolved)**: Release 34-105656 (91 FR 36022) was fetched from govinfo this session and confirms the Oct-2025 → Nov-2026 and June-2026 → Nov-2027 chronology.
- **NYSE Arca overnight approval**: Feb-2025 approval was of a 22-hour model (Simpson Thacher memo footnote 2, Release 34-102400); the 9 pm–4 am amended filing (May 2026) is not reported as approved by the Aug-2026 NYSE FAQ or the Sept-2026 Jones Day memo [uncertain as of 2026-10-14].

- **HFT welfare**: liquidity and price-discovery benefits (Hendershott–Jones–Menkveld 2011; Brogaard–Hendershott–Riordan 2014; Menkveld 2013) vs a socially wasteful speed race (Budish–Cramton–Shim 2015) and withdrawal in stress (Kirilenko et al. 2017).
- **PFOF**: the SEC's 2022 Order Competition Rule argued retail could get better prices via auctions; withdrawn June 2025; academics split on whether retail is harmed (price improvement vs NBBO is real; whether the NBBO itself is degraded is the question).
- **Closing-auction concentration**: convenience and low cost vs fragility (single point of failure), rebalance-day distortions and the Bogousslavsky–Muravyev reversal evidence.
- **Circuit breakers**: "magnet effect" vs cooling-off benefits; LULD pauses concentrate in small caps; MWCB evidence rests on four observations.
- **Square-root exponent**: estimates 0.4–0.7; universality and decay shape (power vs exponential) unresolved. **OFI vs square root**: linear OFI impact at seconds–minutes and concave metaorder impact over hours are different objects; do not extrapolate one to the other's horizon.
- **VPIN**: Easley et al. claim it rose before the Flash Crash; Andersen–Bondarenko (2014) find no predictive content beyond volume/volatility.
- **Technical analysis**: BLL (1992) positive vs STW (1999) data snooping vs LMW (2000) marginal; practitioner belief persists because levels partly self-fulfil via order clustering.
- **Calendar effects**: mostly gone after costs; turn-of-month survives in some samples; whether the residual is risk, flow or noise is open.
- **23x5 trading**: global/retail access and continuous discovery vs thin overnight liquidity, operational risk, corporate-action handling and fragmented price references; the 2026-12-06 launch depends on SIP readiness, DTCC modernization and pending approvals (Arca amended filing, Nasdaq fee filing).
- **Tick-size/access-fee reform**: whether half-penny ticks improve or fragment liquidity in tight-spread names is untested until ≥ Nov 2027.
- **Overnight variance share and NSPY performance figures**: order-of-magnitude only; not sourced this session.
