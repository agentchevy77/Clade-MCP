## Core concepts
- **OPRA** (Options Price Reporting Authority): the consolidated SIP for US listed options; disseminates each participant exchange's best bid/offer (BBO), trades and summary messages (48→96 multicast lines, go-live 2024-02-05 per OPRA notices) **and an NBBO**: quote messages carry a BBO Indicator and Best Bid/Offer appendages computed by the SIP (OPRA Pillar Output Specification; "Current BBO Guidelines" in the OPRA document library). Vendors' NBBOs still differ because some pass OPRA's appendage through while others recompute it from per-exchange quotes with their own timestamps and locked/crossed handling (Databento `cmbp-1`/`cbbo`); a dataset must state which. Exchanges: 19 listed participants as of 2026-10 (incl. MX2 Options, launched 2026-09-14, and IEX Options, from 2026-10) (BOX; Cboe C1/C2/BZX/EDGX; MEMX; MIAX/Pearl/Emerald; Nasdaq NOM/PHLX/ISE/GEMX/MRX/Texas; NYSE American/Arca) plus MIAX Sapphire = 20; 16 before MEMX Options (OPRA notices Apr–Jun 2023), 17 before Sapphire (2024-08 [uncertain month]).
- **OPRA sale conditions and multi-leg prints**: every OPRA trade carries a sale condition (regular; cancel/correction; late/out-of-sequence; single leg of a multi-leg (spread) order; stock-option/buy-write leg; auction and price-improvement prints (AIM, PIM, PRISM, BAM); floor). Spread legs are net-priced and reported as separate single-leg prints at allocated prices, so a leg can print outside its own NBBO. Vendor fields: Cboe DataShop "trade condition", Massive/Polygon `conditions`, ThetaData `condition`, Databento OPRA `flags`/condition mapping.
- **Equity SIPs**: CTA (Tape A = NYSE-listed; Tape B = Arca/other-listed incl. SPY) and UTP (Tape C = Nasdaq-listed). **Consolidated** prints = all venues incl. off-exchange TRF; **primary-venue** = listing exchange only; **official open/close** = listing-exchange auction prints (SPY: NYSE Arca), carrying their own condition codes.
- **Condition codes / sale conditions (equities)**: per-trade flags (odd lot, Form T extended hours, average price, prior reference price, sold out of sequence, derivatively priced, contingent/QCT, official open/close). SIP rules define, per condition, whether the trade updates last sale, high/low, and volume. Consequence: a bar's high/low can be set by prints that do not update "last", and vendors disagree on OHLC depending on which flags they honour.
- **Round lot / odd lot**: odd lots were absent from the SIP before 2013-12-09; since then flagged and excluded from last/high/low. SEC round-lot redefinition (compliance 2025-11-03): 100 shares if price ≤ $250; 40 if $250.01–$1,000; 10 if $1,000.01–$10,000; 1 above. Odd-lot quotes enter core SIP data from 2026-05-01 (first business day of May 2026; depth between the NBBO deferred to May 2028 by Rel. 34-104612).
- **OSI symbology** (Options Symbology Initiative, 2010): 21 characters = 6-char root (space-padded) + YYMMDD + C/P + 8-digit strike (5 integer + 3 decimal). Example: `SPY   261218C00600000`. Adjusted contracts get a numbered root (`SPY1`).
- **AM vs PM settlement**: SPX (root `SPX`) standard 3rd-Friday options are AM-settled on the **SET** value from constituents' opening prints; trading ceases the prior business day (usually Thursday) at the end of the curb session, 17:00 ET (Cboe SPX spec, 2026). A PM-settled 3rd-Friday series also lists under root `SPXW` (Cboe SPXW spec), so identify settlement by root/OSI symbol, never by expiry date. SPXW/EOM and all SPY options are PM-settled (SPY physically delivered, American-style). VIX options/futures settle Wednesday mornings on the **VRO** special opening quotation (SOQ).
- **Exercise by exception and the exercise cut-off**: OCC auto-exercises expiring contracts ≥ $0.01 ITM unless instructed otherwise. SPY/QQQ/IWM/DIA options trade to 16:15 ET and holders may submit exercise or do-not-exercise instructions after the close (OCC cut-off 17:30 ET; broker deadlines earlier, often 16:30–17:00 [uncertain: OCC pages returned 403]), so a physically settled contract OTM at the 16:00 print can be exercised on a post-close move; "expired worthless" is known only at the cut-off.
- **Open interest timing**: OCC computes OI overnight from cleared positions; the OI attached to session D is positions as of D−1 close, delivered before D's open (DataShop calls it "start-of-day"; publication time [uncertain]). OI(D) is a legitimate feature at D's open but excludes D's volume; ΔOI from D's trades is known only from the D+1 file; series listed on D (same-day-listed weeklies, new strikes) show OI = 0 as a listing artefact; no intraday OI exists. Same-day positioning comes from volume plus open/close flags.
- **OCC contract adjustments** (Information Memos): whole-ratio splits → strikes divided, contracts multiplied; odd-ratio splits/spin-offs → deliverable changed (e.g., 150 shares + cash), strike unchanged, new root; special cash dividends ≥ $12.50/contract ($0.125/share) → strike reduced; ordinary dividends → nothing.
- **Forward, carry, implied rate**: F = S·e^{(r−q)T} or F = (S − PV(divs))·e^{rT}; parity-implied forward F = K + e^{rT}(C − P). **Box spread** (call spread + put spread at K1<K2) pays K2−K1 with certainty → the options market's own risk-free rate (van Binsbergen, Diamond & Grotteria 2022). **OIS/SOFR** is the default discount curve since USD LIBOR ended 2023-06-30.
- **IV convention set**: (forward, rate, day-count, intraday fraction and **clock** (wall-clock vs variance-time), model: European BS vs American binomial, input: mid vs last, quoting increment); vendors' IVs are not comparable unless conventions match.
- **Variance-time (business-time) clock**: time to expiry measured in expected variance rather than minutes; intraday variance is U-shaped (Wood, McInish & Ord 1985; Andersen & Bollerslev 1997) and the overnight gap carries a large share of close-to-close variance, so wall-clock T misprices short-dated options (formula below). Practitioner default for DTE ≤ 5.
- **Realized variance (RV)**: sum of squared intraday log returns at a sampling frequency coarse enough that microstructure noise does not dominate; 5-minute RV is rarely beaten across ~400 estimators (Liu, Patton & Sheppard 2015); below ~1 min on liquid names use noise-robust estimators (realized kernel, pre-averaging, two-scale).
- **Standardized options / vol surface** (OptionMetrics): IV interpolated at fixed deltas and constant tenors by **kernel smoothing** (weighted average of observed IVs in (log-tenor, call-equivalent delta) space).
- **Open-close data**: per-series daily volume split by origin (customer, professional customer, broker-dealer/firm, market maker), side (buy/sell), and open/close; sold by Cboe (C1/C2/BZX/EDGX) and Nasdaq (ISE etc.). Basis of Pan & Poteshman (2006).
- **COT/TFF**: CFTC Commitments of Traders; VIX futures (CFE, code 1170E1) sit in the Traders in Financial Futures report (Dealer/Intermediary, Asset Manager/Institutional, Leveraged Funds, Other Reportables, Nonreportable); TFF data from 2006-06-13 (CFTC).
- **Short interest vs short volume**: FINRA short interest = positions (Rule 4560, twice monthly); FINRA daily short sale volume = flow of off-exchange (TRF/ADF) shorts incl. market-maker hedging, not positions.
- **Lookahead bias**: using information unavailable at decision time (bar close inside the bar, HTF values before HTF close, prices adjusted with future splits, restated fundamentals, realized dividend amounts before declaration, same-session OI). **Survivorship bias**: universe built from today's constituents.
- **Walk-forward** (Pardo 2008): refit on a rolling or anchored (expanding) window, test on the next block, stitch OOS results. **Purged K-fold / embargo / CPCV** (López de Prado 2018): purging drops training samples whose label windows overlap the test window; embargo drops training samples just after it; CPCV tests every combination of k of N groups and reassembles multiple OOS paths. **PBO** (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu 2017): share of CSCV splits where the in-sample-best configuration is below-median OOS.
- **Multiple testing**: FWER control (Bonferroni, Holm, Romano-Wolf stepdown), FDR (Benjamini-Hochberg), White's Reality Check (2000), Hansen SPA (2005), HLZ t>3 hurdle, Deflated Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & López de Prado 2014). **Pre-registration / frozen protocol**: hypotheses, sample, outcomes, metrics, decision rules and code fixed, hashed and timestamped before sealed data are touched; one holdout opening.
- **Event study** (Kothari & Warner 2007; MacKinlay 1997): abnormal return = realized − expected around an event; CAR; cross-sectional dependence when events cluster. **Climatology / base rate**: unconditional outcome frequency in the same context (session-time bucket, vol regime). **Placebo levels / matched controls**: the identical pipeline run on fake reference levels or matched non-event windows.
- **Proper scoring rule**: expected score is optimized only by the true probability (Gneiting & Raftery 2007). Brier and log loss are strictly proper; hit rate is not. **Brier decomposition** (Murphy 1973): reliability − resolution + uncertainty. **Reliability diagram / calibration curve**; **ECE** (expected calibration error); **RPS** (ranked probability score, Epstein 1969) for ordered categories; **skill score** = 1 − score/score_climatology.
- **Forecast comparison**: Diebold-Mariano (1995) tests whether the mean loss differential between two forecasts is zero with HAC variance; Harvey-Leybourne-Newbold (1997) small-sample correction; Giacomini & White (2006) for conditional predictive ability. Two Brier numbers without a DM test are not a comparison.
- **Clopper-Pearson**: exact binomial CI from Beta quantiles; conservative. Wilson interval: better average coverage (Brown, Cai & DasGupta 2001).
- **Block bootstrap / HAC**: resampling and standard errors that respect serial dependence (Künsch 1989; Politis & Romano 1994; Newey & West 1987).

## Mechanisms
- **Why options quotes are dirty at the open**: market makers quote wide (or not at all) until the opening auction prints and first hedges fill; 09:30–09:45 ET near-the-money spreads are several times midday width and any IV from them is noise. Consistent with this, vendors that snapshot a few times a day (OptionMetrics Intraday: 10:00, 14:00, 15:45 ET) avoid the first and last 15 minutes; the vendor states no rationale [inference].
- **Zero-bid series**: far-OTM options carry bid = 0 at any time of day (nobody pays the minimum tick); mid = ask/2 is meaningless and parity-implied IV from the matching ITM side is unreliable too; drop them or carry ask-only IV as an upper bound. This, not the open, justifies the permanent `bid > 0` filter.
- **Why "stale" quotes exist**: OPRA disseminates only on change, so an illiquid strike's BBO can be hours old; a 1-minute snapshot product carries it regardless of age unless a quote timestamp is supplied. Cboe DataShop's 2026-06-22 change captures sizes at the last *price* change in the interval, not the last size update.
- **Why underlying/option timestamps must be synchronized**: the option quote embeds the underlying at quote time; pairing a 16:00 ET stock close with a 16:15 ET option quote (SPY/SPX/QQQ/IWM/DIA options trade to 16:15; SPY stock's regular session ends 16:00) creates spurious skew/forward noise. Align via backward as-of joins on a common UTC nanosecond clock. **Clock provenance**: OPRA dissemination timestamp ≠ SIP timestamp ≠ vendor receipt (`ts_event` = exchange/SIP time vs `ts_recv` = capture time); CME data sit on CME's clock; offsets run from tens of microseconds to milliseconds, so state which clock each table uses and never join event-time against receipt-time without a tolerance.
- **Underlying during Global Trading Hours**: SPX is not computed 20:15–09:25 ET; the reference is the front ES future (roll-adjusted), S_implied = F_ES·e^{−(r−q)T_ES}, or the parity-implied forward from GTH SPX option pairs. Source ES from Databento `GLBX.MDP3` (`ES.c.0` or explicit contract) or CME DataMine; pair each GTH option quote with the ES mid at the same UTC nanosecond, never with the 16:00 SPX cash close. In RTH, ES leads the cash index by seconds in fast markets because the index is a stale composite (Hasbrouck 2003).
- **Why leg prints break microstructure statistics**: a vertical executed at a net price is reported as two single-leg prints at allocated prices; the allocation is arbitrary within the net, so one leg can print above the ask and the other below the bid. Before effective spread, trade signing (quote rule at the NBBO mid; the Lee-Ready tick rule is unreliable for options, Muravyev 2016) or buy/sell imbalance, tag or exclude multi-leg, auction and cancel conditions and report the share excluded (double-digit percent of SPX volume [uncertain, measure it]).
- **Why condition codes change OHLC**: SIP rules exclude odd lots, average-price and extended-hours (Form T) trades from last/high/low while counting their volume; a vendor aggregating "all prints" gets different highs/lows than a SIP-compliant one. The closing auction print is disseminated after the auction completes, usually within seconds of 16:00:00 but up to tens of seconds on rebalance/expiry days, with sale conditions "M" (market-center official close) / "6" (closing prints); because its timestamp is ≥ 16:00:00, open-labelled minute-bar vendors emit a 391st "16:00" bar and 5-minute aggregation yields 79 bars unless it is folded into the 15:55 bar.
- **Round-lot redefinition mechanism**: after 2025-11-03 an SPY-sized stock (> $250) has a 40-share round lot, so 40–99-share prints become round-lot (last-sale-eligible) prints where before they were odd lots [uncertain: confirm in CTA/UTP output specs]. Bar-level statistics (print counts, high/low extremes) are not stationary across that date.
- **Corporate actions and the forward**: discrete dividends lower the forward; for American-style equity/ETF options early exercise of short calls is optimal the day before ex-dividend when the dividend exceeds the remaining extrinsic value (≈ matching put price + carry); SPY ex-dividend dates fall on the third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, i.e., quarterly expiration day, so ITM short calls are routinely assigned the Thursday before. Deep ITM short puts are exercised when the interest earned on K exceeds the put's extrinsic value. **Dividend lookahead**: SPY's quarterly amount is declared only days before the ex-date, so a historical IV/forward built with the realized amount uses unavailable information; use the dividend known at the time (last paid amount or a vendor projection, as OptionMetrics' "dividend projections" do) or the parity-implied forward, and record the source.
- **AM-settlement mechanism**: SET = constituents' first prints on expiration Friday; effective expiry is the open, so T for a Friday-AM contract on Thursday afternoon includes the Thursday curb hour (to 17:00 ET) and the overnight; AM-settled series treated as PM mislabel 0DTE statistics.
- **Early-close days** (2026: Nov 27, Dec 24): cash equities and 16:00-close options end at 13:00 ET; "eligible options" that normally close 16:15 (SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA, SPX, XSP, VIX, RUT) end at 13:15 ET (NYSE: "1:00 p.m. (1:15 p.m. for eligible options)"; Cboe DataShop: "1:00 p.m. or 1:15 p.m., depending on the symbol"); an SPXW expiring that day stops at 13:00 (Cboe SPX spec). The Cboe hours page's "09:30–13:00" listing is not product-specific and must not truncate SPY/SPX option sessions. A "16:00 EOD snapshot" is then three hours stale; session minutes are 210 (equities, expiring SPXW) or 225 (16:15-close options).
- **Why wall-clock T biases 0DTE IV**: T = minutes/390 assumes constant variance per remaining minute, but the first and last 30 minutes carry a multiple of the midday per-minute variance and scheduled releases (FOMC, CPI) add lumps; for a fixed option price the wall-clock IV drifts mechanically through the day even if the market's variance forecast is unchanged, and the drift gets misread as "market behaviour". 0DTE IV series are comparable across the session only on a variance-time clock; log `clock` in the IV metadata.
- **Vendor incentives → artefacts**: free sources (Yahoo) cover live tickers, so delisted names vanish and corrections are silent; brokerage APIs (IB, Tradier) exist to execute, so history is pace-limited and expired options absent; exchange shops (Cboe DataShop) license for internal use only; aggregators (Kibot) do not document venue/condition filtering, so OHLC extremes cannot be audited.
- **Why backtests overfit**: the researcher picks the best of many variants; the maximum of N noisy Sharpe estimates grows like sqrt(2·ln N) with zero true skill. Selection + non-stationarity + leakage inflate in-sample metrics. DSR/PBO/Romano-Wolf/HLZ quantify selection; purging/embargo remove leakage; pre-registration removes researcher degrees of freedom; a sealed holdout opened once gives the unbiased estimate.
- **Why purging/embargo**: with forward-window labels ("does price revisit X within 30 bars"), a training sample whose window reaches into the test block shares the test outcome (purging); serially correlated features leak test information into training samples just after the block (embargo of h bars after each test fold; AFML's default h ≈ 0.01·T with T the full sample length, Snippet 7.2 `step = int(len(times)*pctEmbargo)`). Purging covers label-window overlap, embargo covers dependence beyond it, so set h from the longest feature lookback/dependence horizon, not the default.
- **Why CPCV**: one walk-forward path is one draw; CPCV gives a distribution of OOS performance from the same data, at the cost of training on non-contiguous history.
- **Event-study mechanics for intraday levels**: event = first touch of a reference level; outcomes measured in bars after touch; events cluster by session/regime → cross-sectional correlation inflates t-stats; remedies: cluster SEs by session or one observation per session, plus placebo levels at the same distance from price to measure the reaction to "any" level.

## Formulas and quantitative rules
```
Forward and parity (European, per expiry):
  F = K + exp(r*T) * (C_mid - P_mid)         # use the ATM pair; r continuous, T in years
  implied carry q: F = S*exp((r - q)*T)  ->  q = r - ln(F/S)/T
Box-implied rate (strikes K1<K2, box price B = (C1-C2) + (P2-P1), T years):
  r_box = -ln( B / (K2-K1) ) / T
  example: K2-K1 = 100, B = 97.80, T = 0.5 -> r_box = -ln(0.978)/0.5 = 4.45%/yr
Time to expiry conventions:
  ACT/365:   T = (calendar days + intraday fraction)/365
  trading:   T = (trading days + intraday fraction)/252
  wall-clock intraday fraction = minutes remaining to the contract's expiry reference / 390
    expiry reference: SPXW 16:00 print; SPY 16:15 last trade (assignment risk to the 17:30 cut-off)
    early-close days: denominator 210 (16:00-close products, expiring SPXW) or 225 (16:15-close options)
  sigma_365 * sqrt(T_365) = sigma_252 * sqrt(T_252)   (price fixed -> IV changes with convention)
  example Fri 16:00 -> Mon 16:00: T_365 = 3/365, T_252 = 1/252, sigma_252/sigma_365 = sqrt((3/365)/(1/252)) = 1.44
Variance-time clock (practitioner default for DTE <= 5):
  T_var(t) = sum_{remaining intraday buckets b} w_b + w_overnight * (#overnights) + w_event * 1{scheduled event remaining}
  weights from the historical intraday RV profile (5-min RV by time-of-day bucket, same vol regime, >= 60 sessions),
  normalised so one full regular day = 1/252; typical shape: first and last 30 min each ~2-3x a midday 30-min bucket,
  overnight ~20-40% of SPY close-to-close variance [uncertain, period-dependent: estimate on your own window and state it]
Realized variance (regular session, NBBO-mid log returns r_j at 5 min):
  RV_oc = sum_j r_j^2 ;  RV_cc = RV_oc + r_overnight^2 (close-to-open) when comparing with IV, which prices close-to-close variance
  annualise x252; report both; sampling finer than ~1 min on SPY mid is noise-dominated unless realized-kernel / pre-averaging /
  two-scale estimators are used (Barndorff-Nielsen, Hansen, Lunde & Shephard 2008)
```
```
Brier score (binary, forecasts f_i, outcomes o_i in {0,1}):
  BS = (1/N) * sum_i (f_i - o_i)^2            # 0 best, 1 worst
Murphy (1973) decomposition over K probability bins (n_k obs, mean forecast f_k, event freq obar_k, overall obar):
  BS = REL - RES + UNC
  REL = (1/N) sum_k n_k (f_k - obar_k)^2   # calibration error (want 0)
  RES = (1/N) sum_k n_k (obar_k - obar)^2  # discrimination (want large)
  UNC = obar (1 - obar)                    # climatology Brier = base-rate variance
Brier skill score: BSS = 1 - BS / BS_clim,  BS_clim = obar(1-obar)
  example: constant forecast 0.60, base rate 0.55 -> BS = 0.55*0.16 + 0.45*0.36 = 0.250,
           BS_clim = 0.2475, BSS = -0.0101 (negative skill despite "majority-class-correct")
Log loss: LL = -(1/N) sum_i [ o_i ln f_i + (1-o_i) ln(1-f_i) ]; clip f to [1e-6, 1-1e-6]
  same example: LL = 0.6933 vs climatology 0.6881 -> worse than base rate
ECE = sum_k (n_k/N) * | obar_k - f_k |     # bin-dependent; report bin scheme (10 equal-width or quantile bins)
RPS (K ordered classes, cumulative forecast F_k, cumulative outcome O_k):
  RPS = (1/(K-1)) sum_{k=1}^{K} (F_k - O_k)^2
Forecast comparison (Diebold & Mariano 1995), proper loss L (Brier or log), forecasts A and B:
  d_i = L(f_A,i, o_i) - L(f_B,i, o_i);  DM = mean(d) / sqrt( HAC_var(d) / N ) ~ N(0,1) under H0: E[d] = 0
  HAC lag per the Newey-West rule below, or cluster by session for intraday touches
  Harvey-Leybourne-Newbold (1997) small-sample factor: DM* = DM * sqrt( (N + 1 - 2h + h(h-1)/N) / N ), h = forecast horizon
  run model vs climatology, model vs placebo level, model vs model; report the CI of mean(d), not two Brier numbers
  conditional (regime-dependent) superiority: Giacomini & White (2006)
```
```
Hit-rate uncertainty (x successes of n):
  SE = sqrt(p(1-p)/n);  n=400, p=0.55 -> SE = 0.0249, normal 95% CI +/- 0.049
Clopper-Pearson 95%: lower = BetaQuantile(0.025; x, n-x+1), upper = BetaQuantile(0.975; x+1, n-x)
  x=60,n=100 -> [0.497, 0.697]   x=55,n=100 -> [0.447, 0.650]
  x=220,n=400 -> [0.500, 0.600]  x=330,n=600 -> [0.509, 0.590]  x=120,n=200 -> [0.529, 0.669]
Sample size, one proportion, two-sided alpha=0.05, power 0.80 (z=1.960, 0.842):
  n = ( z_a*sqrt(p0(1-p0)) + z_b*sqrt(p1(1-p1)) )^2 / (p1-p0)^2
  0.50 vs 0.55 -> 783;  0.50 vs 0.52 -> 4,903;  0.50 vs 0.60 -> 194;  0.60 vs 0.65 -> 742
Design effect (cluster size m, intra-cluster corr rho): n_eff = n / (1 + (m-1) rho)
```
```
Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio (Bailey & Lopez de Prado 2012), SR per-period, T obs, skew g3, kurtosis g4 (non-excess):
  PSR(SR*) = Phi( (SR - SR*) * sqrt(T-1) / sqrt(1 - g3*SR + ((g4-1)/4)*SR^2) )
Deflated SR (Bailey & Lopez de Prado 2014): SR* = expected max SR of N trials with cross-trial SR variance V:
  SR* = sqrt(V) * [ (1-gamma) * Phi^-1(1 - 1/N) + gamma * Phi^-1(1 - 1/(N*e)) ],  gamma = 0.5772 (Euler-Mascheroni)
  DSR = PSR(SR*).  Units of SR, V and T must match (all per-period or all annualised consistently).
  example (sd of annualised SR across trials 0.5): N=10 -> SR* = 0.79; N=100 -> 1.27; N=1000 -> 1.63
  reported SR 1.0/yr, 3y daily (T=756), g3=-0.5, g4=5, N=100: convert to daily units first,
    SR_d = 1.0/sqrt(252) = 0.0630, SR*_d = 1.265/sqrt(252) = 0.0797, denominator sqrt(1 + 0.0315 + 0.0040) = 1.0176
    PSR(0) = Phi(0.0630*sqrt(755)/1.0176) = Phi(1.70) = 0.956;  DSR = Phi((0.0630-0.0797)*sqrt(755)/1.0176) = Phi(-0.45) = 0.33
Minimum track record length (vs benchmark SR_b, confidence 1-a):
  MinTRL = 1 + (1 - g3*SR + ((g4-1)/4)*SR^2) * ( Z_{1-a} / (SR - SR_b) )^2     # per-period obs
  SR 1.0/yr (g3=-0.5, g4=5, a=0.05, vs 0) -> 707 daily obs = 2.8 y;  SR 0.5/yr -> 11.0 y
```
```
Multiple testing:
  Bonferroni: alpha_adj = alpha/m   (m=20 -> 0.0025);  Holm: step-down alpha/(m-j+1)
  Benjamini-Hochberg FDR q: reject p_(i) <= (i/m) q for largest such i
  HLZ (2016): require |t| > 3.0 for a new "factor"; equivalently p < ~0.0027 single-test
  Romano-Wolf: bootstrap the joint max-t distribution, step down; controls FWER using dependence
Walk-forward efficiency (Pardo): WFE = annualised OOS return / annualised IS return
Purged K-fold: drop train i if [t0_i, t1_i] overlaps [test_start, test_end]; embargo: also drop t0_i in (test_end, test_end + h],
  h = int(pctEmbargo * T_total) bars, AFML default pctEmbargo = 0.01 (Snippet 7.2); raise h to the longest dependence horizon
CPCV: N groups, k test groups -> C(N,k) splits, number of OOS paths = (k/N) * C(N,k);  N=6,k=2 -> 15 splits, 5 paths
PBO (CSCV): split S even blocks, C(S,S/2) train/test combos; lambda_c = logit(rank_OOS of IS-best / (n+1)); PBO = P(lambda_c < 0)
```
```
Serial dependence:
  Newey-West HAC with Bartlett weights w_j = 1 - j/(L+1); lag rules: L = floor(4 (T/100)^(2/9)) (NW 1994) or floor(0.75 T^(1/3))
    T=500 -> 5; 750 -> 6; 1000 -> 6/7; 20,000 -> 12/20
  Block bootstrap length ~ T^(1/3) or Politis-White (2004) automatic; stationary bootstrap: geometric blocks, mean 1/p
  Cluster bootstrap for intraday events: resample sessions (days), not events
Transaction cost per option leg (model):
  cost = phi * half_spread + commission + fees,  phi in [0.25, 1.0] (0.5 = "mid plus a quarter of the spread each way"),
  half_spread from the NBBO at decision timestamp (not the bar close); add slippage growing with |delta| distance from ATM, DTE<2, and 09:30-09:45 / 15:50-16:00 windows
  fees per contract, itemised (reset annually, verify for your sample): OCC clearing fee; Options Regulatory Fee (ORF); SEC Section 31 (sells,
  dollar-value based); FINRA TAF (sells); exchange maker/taker or marketing fees routed by the broker. All-in ~$0.03-0.10/contract excluding
  commission [uncertain, period-specific; OCC fee page returned 403] = 60-200% of a $0.05 premium, so model fees explicitly for wings
Reg T short (uncovered) listed equity option (FINRA Rule 4210(f)(2)(E)):
  calls: premium + max(20% S - OTM amount, 10% S) x 100;  puts: premium + max(20% S - OTM amount, 10% K) x 100
  broad-based index: 15% of underlying, 10% minimum (same call/put basis); spreads: max loss
  Portfolio margin (OCC TIMS): shocks about +/-15% equities, -8%/+6% broad-based indices [uncertain current ranges] - see risk/margin domain
Quoting increments (tick): SPY/QQQ/IWM $0.01 at all premiums (Penny Interval Program); other penny classes $0.01 below $3 / $0.05 at or above;
  non-penny classes incl. SPX $0.05 below $3 / $0.10 at or above [uncertain: from Cboe Rule 5.4 / Penny Interval Program, not re-fetched]
Session arithmetic: 09:30-16:00 ET = 390 min = 78 five-minute bars = 390 one-minute bars (+1 if vendor emits a 16:00 bar);
  09:30-16:15 options = 405 min = 81 five-minute bars; early close 09:30-13:00 = 210 min = 42 bars; 09:30-13:15 = 225 min = 45 bars
UTC mapping: 09:30 ET = 13:30 UTC (EST, UTC-5) or 14:30 UTC (EDT, UTC-4); DST starts 2nd Sunday March, ends 1st Sunday November
```

## Practitioner heuristics
- **Source by question**: EOD academic cross-sections → OptionMetrics (intraday cuts only from 2018); 1-minute NBBO snapshots 2013+ → Databento `cbbo-1m` or Cboe DataShop 1-min intervals (2012+, with vendor Greeks/OI); tick-level NBBO and trade-quote matching → Databento `cmbp-1`/`tcbbo` (2023-03-28+); budget tick options → ThetaData (mind the SPY underlying 2020 cap); overnight SPX → ES from Databento `GLBX.MDP3`; live chains → Tradier (recompute Greeks yourself) / Massive; never IB for history.
- **Quote filters before any IV calc**: bid > 0; ask > bid; spread ≤ max(2 ticks, 20% of mid) where tick is the class's quoting increment ($0.01 SPY/QQQ/IWM; $0.05/$0.10 SPX) [team-specific]; quote age ≤ 60 s for intraday snapshots; drop locked/crossed NBBO; drop 09:30–09:35; on early-close days drop snapshots after 13:15 for 16:15-close options and after 13:00 for equities/expiring SPXW. The tick floor means IV uncertainty for an SPX option quoted $0.05/$0.15 is enormous regardless of the filter: carry the bid-IV/ask-IV band, not a point mid-IV, for premiums under ~5 ticks, and record the increment in the IV metadata.
- **Trade filters before any flow/microstructure statistic**: exclude or tag multi-leg legs, stock-option legs, auction/price-improvement prints, cancels/corrections and out-of-sequence prints; sign the rest by the quote rule against the NBBO at the trade timestamp (`tcbbo`-style); report the excluded share (order-flow domain; Muravyev 2016; Hu 2014).
- **IV convention lock**: state in metadata `{model: BS-European|CRR-American, forward: parity|carry, rate: SOFR-OIS|box, daycount: ACT/365, clock: wallclock|variance-time, intraday_fraction: minutes/390|T_var, tick: 0.01|0.05, price: mid|bid|ask}` and recompute everything yourself rather than mixing vendor Greeks with your own.
- **Underlying for ETF options**: SIP NBBO mid at the option quote timestamp, not last trade (which may be an odd lot or off-exchange print, and lags); for GTH index options, the ES mid at the same nanosecond.
- **Store in UTC, analyse in ET**: parquet column `timestamp[ns, tz=UTC]`; derive `session_date` and `et_time` with `zoneinfo("America/New_York")`; never store naive ET. Layout: parquet partitioned `underlying=.../session_date=...`, sorted by (symbol, ts), ~1e6-row row groups, zstd, OSI symbol dictionary-encoded; query with DuckDB/polars lazy scans rather than loading whole days into pandas.
- **As-of join rules**: always `backward` (last quote at or before the event timestamp) with an explicit tolerance and a staleness column: `pandas.merge_asof(left, right, on="ts", by="symbol", direction="backward", tolerance=pd.Timedelta("60s"))`; `polars.join_asof(..., strategy="backward", tolerance="60s")`; DuckDB `ASOF JOIN ... ON l.ts >= r.ts`. Never `nearest`/`forward` in research joins: they pull a future quote.
- **Enumerate expected bars**: build the session grid from `exchange_calendars.get_calendar("XNYS")` or `pandas_market_calendars.get_calendar("NYSE").schedule()` (early closes included), then left-join bars; count misses per session; a 5-minute equity session with ≠ 78 (or 42) bars, or a 16:15-close option session with ≠ 81 (or 45), is a data problem, not a market fact.
- **Bar label convention test**: the bar labelled 09:30 must contain the opening auction print and its open must equal the official open; if the 09:30 bar is empty and 09:35 holds the open, the vendor labels by close.
- **Adjustment policy**: keep unadjusted intraday prices (strikes are unadjusted) and apply split factors on demand; dividend-"adjusted close" is for daily total-return series only; SPY has no split history, so SPY intraday levels need no adjustment.
- **Fill realism**: fill at next bar open or at the NBBO seen at decision time + latency; options at mid ± 25–50% of half-spread for liquid series, at the touch for anything with spread > 10% of mid; charge commissions ($0–$0.65/contract typical retail as of 2026 [uncertain]) plus itemised fees (above); simulate assignment on ex-dividend eves and pin risk at expiry. **Expiry handling for SPY/ETF options**: trading ends 16:15 ET and exercise instructions run to the OCC cut-off (17:30 ET [uncertain]) on the post-16:00 extended-hours price, so a strike within the typical post-close move (~0.1–0.3% of spot [uncertain, measure from extended-hours prints]) of the 16:00 close is uncertain assignment, not "expired"; mark expiring positions at the 16:15 option close or the 17:30 extended-hours underlying, never at the 16:00 print alone; for SPXW (cash, European) use the 16:00 SPX close exactly.
- **Pre-register, then freeze**: write hypotheses, exact level definitions, outcome windows, filters, metrics, alpha, and the analysis script; commit, tag, and record the SHA-256 of protocol + code; open the sealed window once; report everything, including failed pre-specified tests.
- **Minimum evidence**: a claimed 5-point edge in a binary hit rate needs ~800 independent events at 80% power; for intraday events independence is by session, so count sessions, not touches.
- **Baselines first**: compute climatology (same time-of-day bucket, same vol regime) and placebo levels before the real level; test model vs climatology and vs placebo with Diebold-Mariano on the per-event Brier/log-loss differential (session-clustered); a result that does not beat placebo levels is a property of the price process, not the level.
- **Simple beats complex** when events < ~2,000 or signal-to-noise is low: regularized logistic regression with 3–8 hand-crafted features, isotonic/Platt-calibrated, usually matches gradient boosting OOS; use GBM with monotone constraints only when nonlinearity is hypothesized a priori.
- **Calibrate on a separate slice**: Platt for small samples (< ~1,000), isotonic when ≥ ~1,000 (Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana 2005); never calibrate on the training fold.
- **Report Brier + decomposition + reliability diagram (state the binning; ECE is bin-dependent) + CP interval on hit rate + DM test vs baselines**; a single AUC or accuracy hides calibration.
- **Reproducibility checklist**: `PYTHONHASHSEED=0`, `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1`, `numpy.random.default_rng(seed)`, deterministic single-threaded GBM, lock file with hashes (`uv lock` / `pip-compile --generate-hashes`), SHA-256 manifest of inputs, outputs keyed by hash(code SHA + input hashes + params), DVC-style content addressing, parquet not CSV.

## Common misconceptions and pitfalls
- "Mid is the fair price": with bid = 0 or ask/bid > 2 the mid is undefined/garbage; use parity-consistent OTM options and drop the other side; under ~5 ticks carry the bid/ask IV band.
- "Vendor IV is comparable across vendors": OptionMetrics (binomial, dividend-projected, own rate curve), Cboe DataShop calcs, ORATS smoothed vols and py_vollib European IV differ by convention; differences of several vol points are routine at short DTE and for ITM American options.
- "Trading days/252 and calendar/365 give the same IV": true on average at 30 days, false across weekends and holidays (44% difference Friday→Monday).
- "Minutes/390 is a neutral T for 0DTE": it assumes flat intraday variance; the U-shaped profile and event lumps make wall-clock 0DTE IV drift through the day for a fixed price.
- "Monthly SPX expires at the close": root `SPX` is AM-settled, trading ends Thursday 17:00 ET; root `SPXW` (incl. its 3rd-Friday series) and SPY expire PM.
- "Half-day close is 13:00 for everything": 13:15 for 16:15-close options (SPY/QQQ/IWM/DIA, SPX/XSP/VIX/RUT), 13:00 for equities and expiring SPXW.
- "OI is a live positioning signal": it is D−1 close positions; zero OI on a listing day is an artefact; same-day flow needs volume and open/close flags.
- "A leg printing at the ask is aggressive buying": spread legs are net-priced; sign only single-leg regular prints.
- "High/low in my bars = highest/lowest trade": depends on condition-code handling; odd lots, average-price and extended-hours prints are excluded by SIP rules; vendors deviate; the Nov-2025 round-lot change moves the boundary for stocks > $250.
- "TradingView free data is the consolidated tape": non-upgraded US-stock real-time comes from Cboe One (Cboe's four equity exchanges), not the SIP, so volume and extremes differ [uncertain plan details, 2026]; no historical options chains → options strategies cannot be backtested there.
- "`request.security()` with `lookahead_on` is fine": without a `[1]` offset it returns future HTF values on historical bars ("dangerously misleading", Pine manual); `varip`, `timenow`, `calc_on_every_tick` also repaint; bar replay does not fix lookahead [uncertain: not stated on the Repainting page].
- "Kibot bars are consolidated and SIP-compliant": adjustment choice is documented (adjusted vs unadjusted files), venue and condition filtering are not; audit against Databento/Massive for sample sessions [uncertain].
- "Yahoo/yfinance is good enough for universes": no delisted tickers (survivorship), silent restatements, 1-min history limited to recent weeks [uncertain limit], ToS forbids redistribution; sanity checks only.
- "Short sale volume = short interest": daily Reg SHO short-volume files are flow incl. market-maker hedges; FINRA short interest is positions, twice monthly, with a lag.
- "Open-close data is the whole market": Cboe files cover Cboe exchanges only, Nasdaq files Nasdaq's; coverage of the remaining exchanges varies and must be checked [uncertain].
- "Walk-forward alone prevents overfitting": it prevents lookahead, not selection; DSR/PBO adjust for the number of variants tried, walk-forward ones included.
- "Hit rate is a proper score": a forecaster can maximize hit rate by always predicting the majority class; Brier/log loss are proper, hit rate is not; BSS can be negative while accuracy exceeds 50% (example above).
- "A lower Brier means a better model": without a DM test on the loss differential two point scores are a coin flip; report the CI of the differential.
- "n = number of touches": touches within a session are dependent; effective n is nearer the number of sessions; use cluster bootstrap by session and HAC errors.
- "Deterministic code = reproducible": BLAS/GBM thread scheduling, unpinned dependencies and vendor restatements all change results; pin, hash, snapshot raw data.

## Contested or uncertain claims
- **Hurdle size for multiple testing**: HLZ's t > 3.0 is widely cited; Chen (2022, arXiv:2206.15365) argues most cross-sectional predictability claims are "likely true" and the hurdle is too strict; Harvey (2017) proposes Bayesianized p-values instead. Practitioners in options research mostly adopt DSR/PBO rather than fixed t-hurdles.
- **CPCV vs walk-forward**: López de Prado argues walk-forward is one path and historically biased; critics note CPCV trains on non-contiguous, future-containing blocks and can hide regime drift; many desks use walk-forward for deployment decisions and CPCV for model selection.
- **Embargo size**: López de Prado (2018, §7.4.3, Snippet 7.2) offers h ≈ 0.01·T (T = full sample length, not the test set) as a value that "often suffices", without derivation; the correct h is the longest dependence horizon of features/labels. (Earlier draft misattributed the 1% rule to practitioner write-ups and to test-set size; corrected 2026-10-14 from the book's snippet.)
- **Early-close time for options** (resolved 2026-10-14): the earlier draft and one critique read the Cboe hours page's "09:30–13:00" as applying to all options; NYSE, Cboe DataShop and the SPX spec establish 13:15 for 16:15-close options and 13:00 for equities, 16:00-close options and expiring SPXW. Whether any historical rule change moved index options between 13:15 and 13:00 was not verified [uncertain]; for older half-day data check the last quote time per product.
- **Which risk-free rate for options**: OIS/SOFR (standard), box-implied (van Binsbergen, Diamond & Grotteria 2022: Treasury yields average about 40 bp below box rates, more under 3 months and ~4x in the 2008 crisis, over their S&P 500 option sample (2004–2018 [uncertain end date]); the gap is time-varying, so use a contemporaneous box or OIS rate, not the average), or broker funding rates (retail reality); the choice moves parity-implied dividends and ITM IVs.
- **Variance-time weights**: no published standard; bucket weights, overnight share (20–40%) and event weights are period- and regime-dependent and must be estimated on the study's own window [uncertain magnitudes].
- **OCC clock times**: OI publication time and the 17:30 ET exercise cut-off are standard industry facts but OCC pages returned HTTP 403 this round [uncertain exact times].
- **Kernel-smoothed surfaces**: OptionMetrics' 3-D smoother vs 1-D per-tenor smoothing (Ulrich, Zimmer & Merbecks 2023): the former is the academic default, the latter is argued to be more accurate; SVI/SSVI parametric fits are the practitioner default and are not in IvyDB.
- **Consolidated vs primary data for intraday levels**: some practitioners insist on primary-exchange prints for highs/lows (auction integrity); most use consolidated with SIP-rule filtering; no published consensus.
- **Share of SPX prints that are multi-leg/auction legs**: material (double digits) by practitioner experience, not measured here [uncertain].
