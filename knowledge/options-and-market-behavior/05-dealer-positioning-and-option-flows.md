# Dealer Positioning, Gamma Exposure, Vanna/Charm Flows, Expiration Effects and Pinning

## Core concepts
- **Option market maker (OMM) / dealer**: liquidity provider quoting two-sided markets for spread plus an inventory risk premium; delta-hedges in the underlying (SPX dealers mostly in E-mini S&P futures, ES; single-stock dealers in the stock). "Dealer" below = aggregate OMM capacity.
- **Greeks (per contract unless scaled)**: Delta (Δ) = dOption/dS, the hedge ratio; Gamma (Γ) = dΔ/dS; Vega = dOption/dσ; Vanna = dΔ/dσ = dVega/dS; Charm = dΔ/dt; Theta = dOption/dt.
- **Dealer inventory**: ≈ the negative of aggregate customer net positioning per series (customer long call ⇒ dealer short call); OMM-to-OMM trades, customer crosses, firm/pro-customer/broker-dealer capacities and cross-product offsets (ES options, SPY, VIX, OTC) make it an approximation, not an identity.
- **Hedgers vs non-hedgers**: price impact comes from the net gamma of delta-hedgers (dealers plus hedging customers: vol-arb, dispersion, bank delta-one desks) versus unhedged holders (retail, asset owners, collar/overwrite/buffer funds held to expiry); a hedged customer's gamma cancels the dealer's rather than feeding back into price.
- **Dealer long gamma**: position delta rises with S, so the dealer sells rallies and buys dips: volatility-dampening, intraday mean reversion. **Dealer short gamma**: delta falls as S rises, so the dealer buys rallies and sells dips: volatility-amplifying, trend ("chase") days.
- **Dollar gamma / Gamma exposure (GEX)**: aggregate dealer Γ as dollars of underlying to trade per 1% move. Popularized by SqueezeMetrics (white paper March 2016, revised Dec 2017).
- **Naive GEX sign convention**: calls +, puts −: dealers assumed long every call (customers overwrite) and short every put (customers buy protection). A modeling assumption; by put-call parity a long call and a short put at one strike have identical gamma, so the sign describes who holds the position and how they traded, not option type.
- **Gamma flip / zero gamma**: modeled spot level where aggregate dealer gamma changes sign: above it dealers long gamma (vol suppressed), below it short (vol amplified). SpotGamma's "Volatility Trigger" is reportedly a proprietary variant [uncertain].
- **Call wall / put wall**: strikes with the largest modeled call gamma / put gamma; practitioner resistance/support proxies.
- **Dealer vanna exposure (VEX) / dealer vega**: aggregate dΔ/dσ (and dP/dσ) of the dealer book; drives hedge changes when implied vol moves with spot unchanged.
- **Charm flow**: predictable hedge adjustment from delta decay of OTM/ITM options as time passes; concentrated into expiration and the closing hour.
- **Pinning**: tendency of the underlying to close at/near a high-open-interest strike on expiration. **Max pain**: strike minimizing total intrinsic value of expiring options; folk theory that price is pulled there.
- **Dealer pin risk**: expiring options within ticks of the strike have unknown assignment until after the OCC exercise cutoff (≈5:30 pm ET), so a closing hedge can be wrong by a full delta per contract over the weekend; dealers trade the close hard around big strikes and clean up "inherited" delta next open.
- **OPEX**: monthly expiration, third Friday. **Triple/quad witching**: third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec: index futures, index options and single-stock options expire and the S&P quarterly rebalance trades at the close.
- **SPX settlement**: third-Friday SPX options are AM-settled to the Special Opening Quotation (SOQ, ticker SET) from each constituent's Friday opening print (last trade Thursday), so SET is usually known mid-morning and expiring hedges unwind in the opening auction; SPXW weeklies/dailies and PM third-Friday series settle to the close.
- **VIX expiration**: Wednesday 30 days before the SPX expiration used in the VIX calculation (normally 30 days before the next month's third Friday); settles to a SOQ from the opening prices of those SPX options; last trading day the Tuesday before.
- **0DTE**: options traded on their expiration day. SPX has had an expiration every weekday since Tuesday/Thursday SPXW listings in April–May 2022.
- **Systematic option flows**: JPMorgan Hedged Equity (JHEQX) put-spread collar roll; covered-call/"derivative income" ETFs (JEPI, JEPQ, XYLD); buffer/defined-outcome ETFs (FLEX options, fixed reset dates); autocallables/structured notes; vol-targeting funds and CTAs (non-option but vol-dependent); dispersion books; leveraged ETF (LETF) daily rebalancing.
- **Positioning data**: OCC daily volume by origin (customer/firm/market maker), aggregate only; Cboe Open-Close Volume Summary (customer / professional customer / broker-dealer / market maker × buy/sell × open/close; customer size buckets <100/100-199/>199; end-of-day and 1-/10-minute intraday files for BZX, C1, C2, EDGX; licensed via Cboe DataShop, C1 history to 2005) and Nasdaq/ISE/PHLX equivalents; trade-level capacity-coded records (Cboe research, Amaya et al. 2025) stay proprietary. Files are per exchange: SPX (C1-only) is complete, multi-listed options need every exchange. OI has no side; without licensed data, side comes from aggressor heuristics, the practical limit of public inference. Whether OCC still publishes series-level account-type breakdowns is [uncertain].

## Mechanisms
- **Dealer business model**: quote → get hit → hold inventory → hedge delta → earn spread plus theta/vol risk premium, minus hedging slippage; delta ≈ 0, gamma/vega within limits, risk laid off across expiries and products. Hedging frequency is firm-specific: high-frequency index OMMs re-hedge in ES near-continuously after netting the book; bank exotics/structured-product desks use delta bands or scheduled re-hedges, often into the close. Closing-hour clustering comes less from bands than from charm and PM settlement, LETF/index rebalancing, and hedgers' discretion to wait for depth, which also makes option-hedging flows harder to front-run than fully predictable LETF flows (Barbon-Beckmeyer-Buraschi-Moerke).
- **Two channels by which option trading moves the underlying**: (1) informational: informed option trades are delta-hedged at inception, transmitting information into the stock (Pan-Poteshman 2006; Hu 2014); (2) non-informational: later hedge rebalancing from gamma/charm/vanna moves prices and volatility independent of information (Ni-Pearson-Poteshman-White 2021). Dealer-positioning analysis is channel (2); "unusual options activity" commentary conflates the two.
- **Why long gamma dampens / short gamma amplifies**: Γ > 0: net delta grows as S rises, so the dealer sells rallies and buys dips ⇒ realized vol lower than it would otherwise be (implied−realized spread widens; distinct from the unconditional variance risk premium, present in all regimes), ranges compress, prices revert toward large-OI strikes; long-gamma P&L per re-hedge ≈ ½ Γ S² (r² − σ_impl² dt), theta earned when realized < implied and lost on large moves, which pays for the premium charged. Γ < 0 (e.g., short calls): net delta falls as S rises, so the dealer buys rallies and sells dips; when large relative to liquidity this extends trends. Short gamma peaks near the strike and near expiry (ATM Γ ∝ 1/(S σ √T)).
- **Feedback-effect theory and 1987**: portfolio insurance = synthetic put via dynamic replication, i.e., selling into declines exactly like short-gamma hedging; the Brady Commission (Jan 1988) and Grossman (1988) blamed it for the Oct 19, 1987 dynamics (≈$70bn insured, up to 24% of futures short volume that day: secondary figures [uncertain]). Gennotte-Leland (1990): unobserved hedging supply makes prices discontinuous; Frey-Stremme (1997), Platen-Schweizer (1998), Sircar-Papanicolaou (1998), Schönbucher-Wilmott (2000): hedging demand in an illiquid market raises realized vol (short-gamma hedgers) or lowers it (long-gamma), with endogenous crashes near short-gamma concentrations; Avellaneda-Lipkin (2003) is the pinning special case. Every GEX narrative applies these models.
- **Sign of aggregate dealer gamma by product**: Index: end users are net long index options, especially OTM puts (Garleanu-Pedersen-Poteshman 2009), with net buying pressure in index puts (Bollen-Whaley 2004); that customers are also net short index calls (overwriting) is a practitioner/SqueezeMetrics modeling assumption, not a GPP result. Under it dealers are long calls/short puts: net Γ positive with spot between big put strikes below and call strikes above, negative after a selloff into the puts. Amaya-Garcia-Ares-Pearson-Vasquez (2025), capacity-coded SPX/SPXW trades Jul 2020–Jun 2023: OMM gamma "typically positive" but negative at some point on ≥25% of days (≥50% after May 2022), all day on ≈1%. Single names with speculative call buying (GME/AMC Jan 2021, TSLA, AI names): dealers short calls ⇒ short gamma at upside strikes ⇒ gamma-squeeze mechanics; the naive sign is exactly wrong there.
- **Naive GEX estimation and weaknesses**: GEX_strike = Γ × OI × multiplier × S² × 0.01, sign +1 calls / −1 puts. Weaknesses: (1) OI is a prior-night snapshot with no side; (2) the sign fails whenever customers buy calls or sell puts in size (retail call buying, put-write funds, buffer-ETF structures, the JPM collar's short 20%-OTM put, which makes dealers long that put), and by put-call parity calls+/puts− describes customer position, not option type; (3) ignores hedges in other expiries/products and OTC; (4) Black-Scholes Γ at one IV per contract; (5) dollar magnitudes differ across vendors; (6) 0DTE positions build and expire intraday, so OI-based levels miss the largest gamma on the day it matters; (7) the relevant quantity is net gamma of hedgers vs non-hedgers, which neither OI nor capacity codes reveal. Better: infer side from trade data (aggressor tagging) or license exchange open-close data; caveat: SEC DERA (Fu-Li-Musto-Pearson 2025) find much 0DTE customer volume executes via non-marketable limit orders, so aggressor-based inference misclassifies customer flow.
- **Aggregating the dealer book across products**: SPX, SPXW, XSP (1/10 SPX, cash-settled), SPY (1/10 SPX notional, physically settled), ES/MES options (CME), NDX/QQQ, VIX and OTC variance all hedge into the same ES/cash basket; dealers net delta-equivalents across them before hedging. SPY rivals or exceeds SPX in contracts but is roughly a quarter or less of SPX notional; ES options smaller again [uncertain: varies by year]. SPX-only or SPY-only GEX misses the offsets; coverage differences are one reason vendor levels disagree.
- **Vanna flows (precise)**: OTM put: Δ_put < 0, |Δ_put| rises with σ (vanna = −e^{−qT} φ(d1) d2/σ < 0 as d2 > 0 for low strikes). Dealers short OTM puts carry position delta −Δ_put > 0 hedged with short futures: IV down (spot up, calm days) ⇒ |Δ_put| shrinks ⇒ hedge too large ⇒ dealers buy futures ("vanna rally" fuel); IV up ⇒ dealers sell more, amplifying selloffs. Strongly negative index spot-vol correlation makes this a positive feedback loop both ways and one reason index realized vol rises faster on the way down. Dealer-long OTM calls (overwriting) have vanna > 0: IV up ⇒ sell futures, IV down ⇒ buy; under the standard index assumption both legs give "IV down ⇒ dealers buy". Where it lives: |vanna| peaks for ≈15-35-delta options with 1-3 months to expiry and is small ATM (d2 ≈ 0) and very short-dated, where gamma/charm dominate: vanna is a monthly/quarterly-OI phenomenon, gamma/charm a 0DTE-weekly one. Event use: post-FOMC/CPI/earnings IV crush with spot unchanged makes dealers short OTM puts buy back hedges ("vol-crush" drift): a one-off delta shift, reversed if IV re-rises; tradeable-drift claims are practitioner folklore without peer-reviewed support [uncertain].
- **Charm flows (precise)**: with spot unchanged, OTM |Δ| decays toward 0 and ITM |Δ| toward 1 at a rate ∝ 1/T near the money: tiny for monthlies, very large in the last hours of 0DTE/weekly life. Under the standard index assumption both dealer legs lose delta ⇒ dealers buy back short-futures hedges: a slow bid while spot stays between the big strikes, strongest at the open of expiration day (overnight decay booked) and in the last hour; if dealer-short puts have gone ITM, charm reverses (|Δ|→1 ⇒ dealers short more). "Vanna + charm" OPEX narrative (Cem Karsan/Kai Volatility, SpotGamma): into OPEX decaying/vol-crushed OTM puts force steady dealer buying ("window of strength"); after OPEX those hedges and the pinning gamma vanish ("window of weakness"). Expiration hedge unwinds are documented (pinning papers; Golez-Jackwerth 2012); a tradeable pre/post-OPEX drift is practitioner folklore without peer-reviewed support [uncertain].
- **Pinning**: Avellaneda-Lipkin (2003): dealers net long options near expiry (large OI, market makers long the strike) sell above and buy below it, adding a drift toward the strike with coupling β = nE/(√(2π) σ² T) (n = OI, E = price elasticity): pin probability rises with OI and elasticity, falls with σ²T; repulsion when dealers are net short ("anti-pinning") follows from reversing the sign and is discussed in NPP 2005 and Golez-Jackwerth 2012, not by A-L. Ni-Pearson-Poteshman (2005, JFE): optionable stocks' closes cluster at strikes on expiration dates (avg 16.5 bp, ≈$9bn market cap per expiration, 1996-2002 [uncertain: not re-verified]), from OMM hedge rebalancing when net long plus manipulation by firm (proprietary) traders. Golez-Jackwerth (2012, JFE 106(3):566-585): S&P 500 futures are pulled toward the ATM strike when serial (non-quarterly) options on the futures expire but pushed AWAY from the carry-adjusted ATM strike just before S&P 500 index (AM-settled SPX) options expire ("anti-cross-pinning"), via delta-hedge rebalancing under time decay and individuals reselling/early-exercising ITM options; notional futures shift ≥$115m per expiration day. Do not assume ES pins into an SPX monthly: the documented index effect is repulsion. Max pain relates to pinning only if dealers are net long near that strike; evidence supports pinning at high-OI strikes in small/illiquid stocks, not a general pull to max pain in liquid index products.
- **Expiration-calendar mechanics**: (a) Monthly OPEX: largest OI; AM-settled SPX/NDX/RUT monthlies last trade Thursday, so their gamma is gone at Thursday's close and PM-settled SPXW/SPY/stock OI drives Friday. (b) Quarterly witching: S&P 500 share/float rebalance (and Nasdaq-100 December reconstitution) trades at the close, the year's largest closing auctions; FTSE Russell reconstitution is semi-annual from 2026: June 2026 effective after the close June 26 (rank day April 30); December 2026 rank day Oct 30, preliminary lists from Nov 13, effective after the close Dec 11 (LSEG). (c) VIX Wednesday: the SOQ uses SPX option opening prices, so SOQ-directed flow moves opening prints (Griffin-Shams 2018, "Manipulation in the VIX?"). (d) Dilution: with daily expirations monthly OI is a smaller share of total gamma; monthly "OPEX effects" have weakened while intraday 0DTE gamma has grown. 2026 quirk: Juneteenth (Fri June 19, 2026) closed markets (NYSE); the exchange rule moves a Friday-holiday expiration to Thursday June 18 and Cboe's "VIX exception" moves the related May VIX settlement to Tuesday May 19 [uncertain: rule and closure confirmed, dates derived, not read from the Cboe calendar].
- **Expiration-day unwinds**: (i) AM-settled index monthlies: the overnight residual is a delta bet on Friday's SOQ, hedged out in the opening auction, hence large MOO imbalances in S&P constituents and ES on third Fridays and a SET that often prints away from Thursday's close. (ii) PM- and physically-settled options (SPY, QQQ, single stocks) become stock via exercise/assignment after the close; dealers unwind this inherited delta Monday morning, a second mechanical flow after OPEX ("post-OPEX hangover" is partly inventory clean-up, not information). (iii) Pin risk makes late-day volume near a big strike two-sided; dealers lean on the closing print.
- **JPM collar (JHEQX)**: fund ($18.52bn, June 30, 2026 fact sheet) holds S&P 500 stocks plus a quarterly SPX put-spread collar (long put ≈5% OTM, short put ≈20% OTM, short call ≈3-5% OTM, near zero cost) rolled on the last trading day of each quarter; Hedged Equity 2 and 3 reportedly roll at other month-ends and legs are ≈40-45k contracts [uncertain: practitioner estimates]. Dealers hold the other side (short 5% put, long 20% put, long call): long gamma around the call strike (a lid/"magnet" into expiry with spot nearby), short gamma around the 5% put (acceleration if spot falls into it late in the quarter); once the call is deep ITM its gamma is small and the lid fades. Roll-day delta flow has no fixed sign; decompose it. Old call deep ITM (Δ ≈ 1), old puts worthless: closing removes ≈+1 delta per contract, so the dealer buys back ≈1 ES-equivalent; the new collar adds ≈+0.45 (long ≈0.35-delta call, short 5% put ≈+0.15-0.25, long 20% put ≈−0.04), which it sells ⇒ net dealer BUYING ≈0.5 × contracts × multiplier × S. Old call OTM with the old 5% put ITM after a selloff ⇒ net dealer selling. Desks pre-hedge over preceding days, so the roll-day print is often small [uncertain]. Mechanical, not informational; check "bullish/bearish roll" commentary against this arithmetic.
- **Covered-call ETFs**: JEPI (≈$46bn, July 2026 [uncertain]) and JEPQ (≈$36-45bn, sources conflict [uncertain]) sell ~1-month calls ≈2-4% OTM via equity-linked notes (ELNs) in laddered weekly tranches, spreading vol supply through the month. The issuing bank is long the embedded calls and typically lays off part of the risk in listed SPX/NDX calls or OTC [uncertain in size], so the footprint is persistent call OI a few percent above spot across several weekly expiries, and whoever hedges those calls supplies long gamma that leans against rallies. Listed overwriters (XYLD, QYLD, single-stock income ETFs) create visible OI at written strikes.
- **Buffer / defined-outcome ETFs** ($78bn across ~420 ETFs at end-2025, Morningstar; First Trust ≈$40bn, Innovator ≈$27.5bn [uncertain: not re-fetched]): each series holds FLEX options (long deep-ITM call as synthetic index, long ATM put, short put at the buffer, e.g., 90%, short call at the cap) with annual/quarterly resets on fixed dates. Dealer side: short ATM put, long buffer put, long cap call ⇒ long gamma at cap and buffer, short gamma near the reset-date ATM; reset days create predictable roll flow.
- **Autocallables / structured notes**: investor long digital coupons, short a down-and-in put (DIP); dealer long the DIP, short the autocall/coupon strip. Above the barrier the long DIP is long vega (hence structural supply of long-dated Nikkei/KOSPI/HSCEI/SX5E vol and skew), and its vega and |delta| rise as spot falls toward the barrier, so dealers sell more vega and add long-delta hedges into the decline (a bid, not trend-following). At knock-in the DIP becomes a vanilla put with lower vega and |delta| than the DIP had just above the barrier: dealers buy back vol (vol spike) and sell underlying abruptly (the "air pocket" is at/below the barrier); a failed autocall also extends maturity. Episodes: HSCEI 2015-16 and 2024 Korean ELS knock-in losses; Nikkei around Aug 5, 2024. Footprint: dealer vega selling depresses long-dated vol and steepens skew in those markets; US worst-of notes on mega-cap stocks do the same locally. Scale [uncertain: SRP figures not re-verified]: US structured-note issuance >$195bn in 2025, autocalls >43%; Korea ELS ≈KRW2.8tn in April 2026 (+46% y/y); US autocallable ETFs ≈$1.2bn across ~12 funds. Hedging practice is desk-specific; flow size and timing [uncertain].
- **Vol-targeting funds, risk parity, CTAs**: exposure ≈ min(cap, target σ / realized σ) with 1-3 month lookbacks, so a realized-vol spike forces selling proportional to (current exposure − new target), spread over 1-10 days depending on the lookback; exposure is rebuilt gradually after calm weeks. Size depends on starting exposure (small if already de-risked) and on AUM estimates that vary several-fold across bank desks [uncertain]; CTAs add multi-week trend-following in the same direction. Daily, lagged, path-dependent: not dealer gamma, though routinely conflated with it.
- **Dispersion**: sell index vol/correlation, buy single-name vol; desks are long single-stock gamma and short index gamma, adding to structural short index gamma near popular strikes and to single-stock pinning. Cboe launched the S&P 500 Dispersion Index (DSPX) in September 2023 [uncertain exact date; Sept 27 per article URL].
- **Leveraged ETF rebalancing** (Cheng-Madhavan 2009): an L× LETF trades (L² − L) × AUM × r_day at the close in the direction of the day's move (long and inverse alike): late-day momentum, extra close volatility. Barbon-Beckmeyer-Buraschi-Moerke (2021/22): LETF and option-hedging flows both produce end-of-day momentum and next-day reversal; LETF effects decayed because perfectly predictable flows attract liquidity provision, option-gamma effects persisted. "Volmageddon" (Feb 5, 2018): VIX closed 37.32, roughly doubling; inverse and 2× VIX ETPs (XIV, SVXY, TVIX, UVXY) had to buy ≈2 × AUM × r_day of VIX futures into the 4:15 pm settlement, estimated at well over 200k contracts, about a day's volume [uncertain]; futures spiked in the final minutes, XIV lost ≈96% and was terminated, SVXY ≈90%: the template for any daily-reset product (known formula, known time, flow grows with the move it chases). Single-stock LETFs (2022-) concentrate this in TSLA/NVDA-type names; Nomura estimated ≈$10bn of trading per 1% move at the June 2026 peak (Bloomberg) [uncertain]. Predictable flows (LETF closes, reconstitutions, known collar rolls) get pre-positioned and partly front-run, so "known flow" alpha decays while discretionary dealer hedging stays impactful; backtests of OPEX/roll-day effects should expect regime decay.
- **0DTE hedging**: ATM gamma scales as 1/√T: ≈4-5× a 30-day option's at the open of expiration day, ≈10-12× with an hour left, so the hedge trade per contract per 1% grows through the day and exceeds a monthly's by an order of magnitude only in the final hours; small net inventories still imply large hedge trades, but inventories reset daily and customer flow is two-sided. Dealers hedge largely in ES and with offsetting 0DTE/1DTE options; hedging clusters at strike crossings and in the last 30-60 minutes (charm). Practitioner pattern: "0DTE pins the day inside the straddle until a big strike breaks, then flows chase": long-gamma mean reversion turning into short-gamma acceleration once spot crosses where dealers are short (often the day's heavily bought OTM calls/puts).
- **Gamma squeeze**: customer call buying ⇒ dealer short calls ⇒ dealer buys stock ⇒ price rises ⇒ call deltas rise ⇒ more buying; needs dealer net short calls, thin liquidity, OI near spot. Detection: near-dated OI concentrated above spot, call volume ≫ put volume with aggressor-buy tagging, high option notional/ADV, call-skew inversion (upside IV rising with price), expensive borrow; counter-signal: IV falling as price rises = call selling into strength (dealers getting longer gamma). GME Jan 2021: SEC staff report (Oct 18, 2021) p.29: options growth "mostly driven by an increase in the buying of put, rather than call, options", market makers buying rather than writing calls, "not consistent with a gamma squeeze"; p.41: by dollar volume trading "concentrated heavily in call options". Feb 2022 Columbia critique (Mitts, Battalio, Brogaard, Cain, Glosten, Kochuba): put/call framing is irrelevant by put-call parity, much GME volume looks like hedging, staff short-interest data start too late; concedes it cannot measure the squeeze. SpotGamma's two-stage account and a later academic paper argue a squeeze occurred [uncertain: "2026 AEA paper" and "began in fall 2020" are practitioner-sourced]. SoftBank "Nasdaq whale" Aug-Sep 2020 (≈$4bn call premium, $30-50bn notional [uncertain]) plus record retail call buying (≈$40bn premium in four weeks, FT-cited [uncertain]); TSLA repeatedly 2020-21.
- **Cross-asset feedback flows**: (i) MBS convexity hedging: the prepayment option leaves MBS holders short convexity; as rates fall durations shorten and hedgers (servicers, GSEs, REITs) buy duration, as rates rise they sell, amplifying rate moves (Perli-Sack 2003, FEDS 2003-49: swaption-implied vol rises with prepayment risk; amplification "substantial" but months-long); 1994 and summer 2003 are the usual examples [uncertain attribution]. (ii) FX: large vanilla expiries at the 10:00 NY cut pull spot toward big strikes beforehand; barrier defense/knock-out flows see dealers push spot through or hold it away from a barrier; sizes from DTCC-reported OTC expiries. (iii) Commodities: Mexico's annual sovereign oil hedge (Asian puts on Maya crude from a few banks, ≈$1bn premium, payouts ≈$5bn in 2015 and ≈$2.5bn in 2020 [uncertain]) leaves dealers short puts hedged by selling futures into declines. Pattern: a large, concentrated, option-like exposure hedged in a market thinner than the exposure.

## Formulas and quantitative rules
```
Variables: S = spot; K = strike; T = years to expiry; sigma = implied vol (decimal); q = dividend yield; r = rate;
M = multiplier (100); OI = open interest; N_i = dealer net position in series i (contracts, +long/-short);
phi = standard normal pdf; N() = cdf; d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r - q + sigma^2/2)T]/(sigma sqrt(T)); d2 = d1 - sigma sqrt(T)

Gamma  = e^{-qT} phi(d1) / (S sigma sqrt(T))                 (calls = puts)
Vanna  = -e^{-qT} phi(d1) d2 / sigma                          (dDelta/dsigma; calls = puts at a strike)
Charm_call = -e^{-qT} phi(d1) [2(r-q)T - d2 sigma sqrt(T)] / (2T sigma sqrt(T)) + q e^{-qT} N(d1)
Charm_put  = Charm_call - q e^{-qT}                            (OTM |delta| decays, ITM |delta| grows, rate ~ 1/T)

Hedge_units            = - sum_i N_i Delta_i M
Dealer $gamma per 1%   = sum_i N_i Gamma_i M S^2 0.01          ($ of underlying per 1% move, linearized)
Naive GEX_strike       = Gamma OI M S^2 0.01 s,  s = +1 calls, -1 puts   (SqueezeMetrics 2017; dealers long calls/short puts, hedged exactly)
Vanna flow (1 vol pt)  = - sum_i N_i Vanna_i 0.01 M S           ($)
Charm flow (1 day)     = - sum_i N_i Charm_i (1/365) M S        ($)
Gamma-theta P&L/step   = 0.5 Gamma S^2 (r^2 - sigma_impl^2 dt) per contract (long gamma earns when realized > implied)
LETF close rebalance   = (L^2 - L) AUM r_day                    (Cheng-Madhavan 2009; -1x => 2 AUM r_day, same direction as move)
Pinning (Avellaneda-Lipkin 2003): drift toward the strike with coupling beta = n E / (sqrt(2 pi) sigma^2 T),
                         n = OI held long by hedgers, E = price elasticity; pin probability rises with beta.
Price impact (square-root law): dP/P ~ Y sigma_daily sqrt(Q / V),  Q = hedge $ to execute, V = $ volume of the execution window,
                         Y ~ 0.5-1 (Almgren et al. 2005; Toth et al. 2011). Compare hedge demand to window volume/depth, not full-day notional.
```
- **Worked example (hypothetical SPX = 6000, IV 15%; trading-day convention)**: 0DTE ATM, 1 trading day left: σ√T = 0.15√(1/252) ≈ 0.00945; Γ ≈ 0.3989/(6000 × 0.00945) ≈ 0.0070/pt ⇒ a 1% (60-pt) move changes delta by ≈0.42 linearized; exact ≈0.35 (Δ(6000) = 0.502, Δ(6060) = 0.855) because gamma falls away from the strike, so the linear estimate overstates the hedge for large moves and understates it for a move ending near a strike ⇒ ≈$210-250k of futures per contract per 1%. Dealer net short 10,000 ⇒ ≈$2.1-2.5bn per 1% move. 30-calendar-day (≈21 trading day) ATM: σ√T ≈ 0.043, Γ ≈ 0.00155 ⇒ ≈$56k per contract per 1% (≈4.6× smaller, √21).
- **Sizing impact**: the ≈$400bn/day ES notional (Cboe, 2023) bounds liquidity from above; it is not the yardstick. Square-root law: $2.5bn over a $400bn day (Q/V = 0.6%) ⇒ ≈0.04-0.08 × σ_daily(≈1%) ≈ 4-8 bp; the same $2.5bn in the last 30 minutes, when ES trades perhaps $40-60bn (Q/V ≈ 4-6%) ⇒ ≈10-25 bp, more when top-of-book depth has thinned (depth, not daily notional, binds in a fast market).
- **Vanna example**: 25-delta 30-day put: d1 ≈ 0.674, d2 ≈ 0.631, φ(d1) ≈ 0.318 ⇒ vanna ≈ −1.34 per unit σ = −0.0134 per vol point. A 2-point IV decline moves delta from −0.250 to ≈−0.223. Dealer short 10,000 ⇒ long delta shrinks by ≈268 contract-equivalents ⇒ buys ≈ 268 × 100 × 6000 ≈ $160m of futures.
- **Charm example**: that 25-delta put decays ≈0.3 delta points/day with 30 days left; a 25-delta put with 2 days left decays to ≈17 delta the next day (≈8 points/day); in the final hours the rate is far higher. Charm is an expiration-day/last-hour phenomenon.
- **Rules**: ATM Γ ∝ 1/√T; near-the-money |charm| ∝ 1/T; |vanna| peaks 15-35 delta at 1-3 months; dollar gamma ∝ S²; hedge trade per 1% ≈ dollar gamma (linearized); realized-vol dampening ∝ dealer long dollar gamma / liquidity in the hedging window.

## Practitioner heuristics
- Positive dealer-gamma regime (spot above flip/trigger, between put wall and call wall): fade intraday extremes, expect realized < implied and moves stalling near the biggest-OI strikes. Negative regime (below flip, after a selloff into put OI): expect trend persistence, wider ranges, gap risk; do not fade. Effect size: SPX median gamma effect −0.08 vol pts/day, worst cases +3.3 (daily) and +6.4 (30-min) vol points vs a 4.5-pt SD of daily vol changes (Amaya et al.): a prior on range/behaviour, not a standalone signal; materially larger in illiquid single names (Barbon-Buraschi). The "usually long gamma" base rate is sample-specific: mean OMM gamma fell ≈40% after May 2022 and intraday negative gamma became a majority-of-days event.
- Call wall ≈ upside lid for the cycle, put wall ≈ floor; more reliable the closer to expiry and the larger the concentration relative to ADV; recompute after every large expiration because walls move with OI.
- Levels break when: the positioning sign is wrong (retail call-buying names, event flows, hedged customers); macro/news shocks overwhelm dealer flow; the gamma sits in AM-settled monthlies that die at Thursday's close; 0DTE flow dominates and OI-based levels are stale; vendors disagree (model risk).
- OPEX week: gamma concentration peaks; expect pins in heavily-optioned names and compressed index ranges, but the documented SPX futures effect into the AM expiry is repulsion from the ATM strike, not pinning; the Monday-Tuesday after monthly OPEX often shows larger moves once pinning gamma is gone and inherited delta is unwound: a conditional prior, not a signal.
- Quarter-end: locate the JPM collar strikes; the short call tends to cap rallies in the final weeks while spot is near it; roll-day flows (last trading day of the quarter) are mechanical, sign-dependent on spot versus the old strikes (net dealer buying if the old call is ITM), partly pre-hedged, usually reversed within days.
- VIX Wednesday: SPX option opening prints are noisy and VIX-related hedges roll off; do not read SOQ-driven moves as information.
- 0DTE intraday: build a real-time gamma map from the day's own flow, not overnight OI; expect mean reversion inside the day's straddle breakeven until a big strike breaks; 3:00-4:00 pm charm and hedge unwinds increase closing drift in the day's direction; 0DTE positions carry no overnight risk, so a "0DTE crash" needs intraday fuel.
- Witching close: record closing-auction volume; imbalances are mostly index-rebalance driven; option effects come earlier (AM index unwinds at the open, PM pin dynamics in the last hour, assignment clean-up Monday).
- Vol-control/CTA: after a 2-3 SD realized-vol shock expect multi-day mechanical selling regardless of dealer gamma, sized by (current exposure − target/realized) over the lookback horizon, small if funds are already de-risked; after 2-4 calm weeks expect a gradual re-leveraging bid.
- LETF: last-30-minute momentum scales with (L²−L) × AUM × |r_day|; strongest in single-stock LETF names on big days; liquidity providers now front-run much of it, so the edge has decayed.
- Autocallables: when a referenced index nears a cluster of knock-in barriers (Korea/HK/Japan) expect dealer vol buying and abrupt delta selling at/below the barriers, not before them; in the US watch concentrated single-stock autocall underliers (mega-cap tech).
- Always ask: who is on the other side and do they hedge; how big is net (not gross) exposure versus the liquidity of the execution window (square-root impact, depth); when do they hedge (continuous, bands, close)?

## Common misconceptions and pitfalls
- "Open interest reveals dealer positioning": OI has no side; naive GEX is an assumption. Customer 0DTE buying and selling are balanced on average (17.2% vs 16.0% of volume) but single-day imbalances are large (daily OMM gamma −5,427 to +16,744 vs mean 341), so real-time inference of the day's net inventory is still needed.
- "Dealers are always short (or always long) gamma": the sign varies with spot vs OI and by product; SPX OMM gamma is positive on average but negative at some point on ≥25% of days (≥50% after May 2022) and all day on ≈1%: intraday flips through heavily bought 0DTE strikes are common, all-day short gamma rare.
- "Max pain is manipulation": pinning is mostly hedge rebalancing; manipulation evidence is limited (NPP 2005 firm-category trades); max pain also depends on which expirations are counted.
- "GEX dollar levels are comparable across vendors or over time": sign inference, IV source and product coverage differ; compare regimes, not numbers.
- "The gamma flip is a precise line": it is a model estimate; treat as a zone.
- "Hedge flow is X% of daily volume, so harmless": impact depends on participation in the execution window and on depth (square-root law); 0.6% of the day can be 5% of the last half-hour.
- "0DTE will cause Volmageddon 2.0": inventories expire daily and measured net exposures are small relative to ES liquidity; the credible risk is intraday acceleration (+3-6 vol pts in worst estimated cases), not an overnight blowup, though those analyses are single-product and sample-limited; the original Volmageddon (Feb 2018) was a daily-reset-product flow, the LETF formula on VIX futures.
- "Vanna is always bullish": the sign depends on positioning and the direction of the IV change; in a rising-IV selloff vanna adds selling.
- "Charm is a Friday thing": charm ∝ 1/T; with daily expirations there is a charm cycle every day, strongest in the last hours.
- "Customer = retail": exchange "customer" capacity includes institutions and hedging funds; retail-share estimates (≈57% of SPX 0DTE volume in June 2026 per a secondary source [uncertain]) rely on heuristics.
- "SPX pins like a stock": AM-settled SPX monthlies have no Friday gamma; PM-settled SPXW/SPY pin to the close if dealers are net long; Golez-Jackwerth document futures pinning at serial futures-option expiries and anti-pinning at SPX index-option expiries.
- "Side/capacity data are only for academics": trade-level capacity-coded records are proprietary, but exchange open-close summaries are commercially licensed (Cboe DataShop, Nasdaq).
- Attributing every late-day move to gamma: LETF rebalancing, index rebalances, closing-auction imbalances and vol-control flows are concurrent and often larger.
- Overfitting levels: strike OI is public, so many vendors publish similar levels; reactions at round strikes are confounded by ordinary round-number clustering and self-fulfilling order placement.

## Contested or uncertain claims
- **Does 0DTE trading raise or lower volatility?** Brogaard-Han-Won: raises (≈+9% per SD, retail-driven, beyond gamma hedging) [uncertain]. Adams-Fontaine-Ornthanalai: lower on 0DTE-available days [uncertain]. Dim-Eraker-Vilkov: no propagation. Amaya et al. and Cboe: average effect dampening (dealers long), maximum amplification modest. Disagreement stems from identification (instrument validity), data (public vs capacity-coded) and whether retail speculation or dealer hedging is the channel.
- **GME January 2021 gamma squeeze**: SEC staff: "not consistent with a gamma squeeze" (put-driven growth, dealers net call buyers) yet call-concentrated dollar volume; Feb 2022 Columbia critique: put/call framing beside the point, much volume was hedging, magnitude unmeasurable; SpotGamma (two-stage: retail call buying, then institutional put selling) and a later academic paper [uncertain: "2026 AEA paper" unverified] argue it occurred; "began in fall 2020" is practitioner-sourced [uncertain]. Unresolved.
- **August 5, 2024 selloff**: competing strategist theories on whether dealer gamma amplified or dampened (Kilburn, Risk.net, Aug 12, 2024; epigraph of Amaya et al. 2025); no consensus.
- **Vanna/charm "flows" as a tradeable calendar edge**: marketed by practitioners (Kai Volatility, SpotGamma, Nomura/McElligott commentary); no peer-reviewed confirmation of reliable pre/post-OPEX or post-event drift [uncertain]. Expiration hedge-unwind effects are established; directional-drift claims are not.
- **Max pain**: a 2020s study ("No Max Pain, No Max Gain", authors/venue unverified [uncertain]) reports a 0.4%/week long-short effect concentrated in small illiquid stocks; not independently replicated.
- **Vendor levels** (SpotGamma, SqueezeMetrics/Prior Analytics, Menthor Q, Tier1Alpha, GammaEdge, bank desks such as Nomura's McElligott): flip/trigger, walls, dollar gamma, vanna/charm maps, real-time hedging-impact estimates (SpotGamma "HIRO"); proprietary methodologies, differing sign inference, no independent out-of-sample audit: regime indicators, not precise levels.
- **JPM collar leg size** (~40-45k contracts) and price effect: practitioner estimates; roll-day direction is derivable (above), actual pre-hedging is not.
- **Whether LETF rebalancing still moves closes**: Barbon et al. find decay after liquidity providers adapted; 2026 single-stock LETF growth and Nomura's $10bn/1% estimate [uncertain] suggest renewed scale.
- **Index-call positioning assumption**: net-short index calls by end users is a SqueezeMetrics modeling assumption, not a GPP/Bollen-Whaley finding; covered-call ETF growth makes it more plausible for upside strikes but it is unmeasured in public data.
- **Review resolution notes**: both critiques agreed on the Golez-Jackwerth correction (confirmed against the JFE abstract) and the JPM roll-day sign (delta accounting). On side/capacity data, "exchanges and academics only" and "Cboe sells open-close summaries" both hold at different granularities (DataShop page confirms the licensed summary; trade-level capacity records stay proprietary).
- **Expiration-product expansions (2026)**: SEC Release 34-105196 (Apr 10, 2026; SR-CBOE-2026-005) approves PM-settled DJX options with Mon-Fri weekly, end-of-month and quarterly expirations; reported Nasdaq Mon/Wed single-stock and ISE SPY/QQQ/IWM Mon-Thu expirations rest on their own SEC orders: ISE Mon/Wed expirations on Qualifying Securities were approved 2026-01-16 (Rel. 34-104624, SR-ISE-2025-15) and ISE Tue/Thu ETF expirations 2026-08-12 (Rel. 34-106100).
