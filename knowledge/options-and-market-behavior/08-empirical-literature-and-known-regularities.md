# Empirical Literature: Option Returns, Information in Options, Anomalies and Their Decay
domain_id: 08-empirical-literature-and-known-regularities | last_updated: 2026-10-14 | status: reviewed
scope: What peer-reviewed and widely-cited working-paper evidence says about (i) average option returns and the premia embedded in them, (ii) what option prices/flows predict about stocks, (iii) retail/0DTE/dealer-hedging effect sizes, (iv) equity anomalies relevant to behaviour, and (v) how robust any of this is (decay, multiple testing, replication, calendar/FOMC effects).
confidence_notes: Well-established: negative variance risk premium, negative average index put/straddle returns, the day/night sign flip in delta-hedged option returns, options-implied signals predicting stock returns in 1996-2010 samples, post-publication decay. Contested: whether put returns are "too" negative vs rational jump/peso compensation; whether 0DTE raises or dampens underlying volatility; whether replication is a "crisis". Recency-sensitive: 0DTE statistics (2023-2025), pre-FOMC drift (gone since ~2015), retail volume shares. ## Core concepts
- Option return puzzle: average returns on S&P 500 index options (especially puts and straddles) are far more negative than CAPM/Black-Scholes/basic stochastic-vol models imply; index options are "expensive" (Coval-Shumway 2001; Bondarenko 2003/2014; Jones 2006).
- Zero-beta straddle: ATM call + ATM put weighted so market beta = 0; isolates volatility compensation. Coval-Shumway's lost ≈3%/week (abstract-confirmed).
- Delta-hedged gain (Bakshi-Kapadia 2003): P&L of a long option continuously delta-hedged; mean ≈ vega × volatility risk premium, so a negative mean implies a negative vol premium. Its sign flips between sessions (Muravyev-Ni 2020: negative overnight, positive intraday) and is especially negative across nontrading periods (Jones-Shemesh 2018).
- Variance risk premium (VRP): risk-neutral expected variance (variance-swap rate / VIX²) minus physical variance. Conventions differ: Carr-Wu use RP = RV − SW (negative; annualized variance of a 30-day contract); Bollerslev-Tauchen-Zhou (BTZ) use VRP = IV − RV (positive), IV = squared VIX, RV = past-month realized variance, both in MONTHLY percent-squared units (≈ annualized/12). Check convention and units before comparing papers.
- Log variance risk premium (Carr-Wu): ln(RV/SW), the continuously-compounded excess return to a long variance swap; ≈ −0.59/month for SPX (≈ −45% simple).
- Correlation risk premium (Driessen-Maenhout-Vilkov 2009): index variance ≈ average single-name variance × average pairwise correlation, so the gap between index and single-name VRPs is a priced premium on correlation shocks; the mechanism behind "index vol expensive, single-name vol not" and behind dispersion trades.
- Skew (risk) premium (Kozhan-Neuberger-Schneider 2013): expected payoff of a "skew swap" exchanging realized for implied third moment; the part of the smirk slope that is a premium rather than a forecast of return-vol correlation.
- Tail/jump risk premium (Bollerslev-Todorov 2011): model-free compensation for rare events from the gap between risk-neutral (short-dated OTM options) and physical (high-frequency extreme-value) jump tails; a large fraction of both equity and variance premia.
- VIX premium (Cheng 2019): ex-ante expected return to a long VIX future (futures price minus the statistical VIX forecast); the carry in short-VIX-futures/vol-ETP strategies.
- Demand-based option pricing (Garleanu-Pedersen-Poteshman 2009): risk-averse intermediaries cannot fully hedge options, so end-user net demand raises an option's price in proportion to the variance of its unhedgeable part (and related options via covariances).
- PNBO (Chen-Joslin-Ni 2019): public net buy-to-open volume of deep-OTM (K/S ≤ 0.85) SPX puts; proxy for crash-insurance demand and, in supply-shock regimes, intermediary capacity.
- Put-call IV spread (CPIV, Cremers-Weinbaum 2010): IV(call) − IV(put) for matched strike/maturity pairs; positive ⇒ expensive calls ⇒ bullish. Volatility smirk (Xing-Zhang-Zhao 2010): IV(OTM put) − IV(ATM call); steep ⇒ bearish. O/S ratio (Johnson-So 2012): option ÷ stock volume; high ⇒ lower future returns. Open-buy put-call ratio (Pan-Poteshman 2006): customer buy-to-open volume only (proprietary CBOE data); low ⇒ bullish.
- Effective spread (conventional): 2 × |trade price − quoted midpoint|. Because option prices are predictable at high frequency and many traders time executions, it overstates the cost actually paid: ≈25% on average and >60% for execution-timing traders (Muravyev-Pearson 2020).
- Option momentum (Heston-Jones-Khorram-Li-Mo 2023): stocks whose ATM straddles had high past returns keep delivering higher straddle returns at 6-36-month horizons, with no long-run reversal.
- Earnings announcement premium (Frazzini-Lamont 2007): expected announcers outperform non-announcers in the announcement month. PEAD (Bernard-Thomas 1989): post-announcement drift in the direction of the earnings surprise (SUE) for ~60 trading days. Pre-earnings straddle window (Gao-Xing-Zhang 2018): ATM straddles bought 3 days before and sold on the announcement date earn positive returns on average.
- Equity behavioural factors: momentum (Jegadeesh-Titman 1993); short-term reversal (Jegadeesh 1990; Lehmann 1990); IVOL anomaly (Ang-Hodrick-Xing-Zhang 2006); betting-against-beta (Frazzini-Pedersen 2014); MAX/lottery demand (Bali-Cakici-Whitelaw 2011; Boyer-Vorkink 2014 for high-skew options).
- Retail options trading / PFOF: three wholesalers receive ≈90% of options PFOF (Bryzgalova-Pavlova-Sikorskaya 2023; naming them Citadel Securities, Susquehanna, Wolverine is [uncertain; not in the abstract]); retail concentrates in cheap, short-dated, wide-spread contracts.
- 0DTE: options expiring the same day; SPX has had an expiration every weekday since May 2022 (Tuesday/Thursday Weeklys added April/May 2022 [dates uncertain]; Neudata attributes this to Cboe filing SR-CBOE-2020-051 [attribution uncertain]).
- Dealer gamma hedging: market makers short gamma must buy as prices rise and sell as they fall (momentum-inducing); long gamma does the opposite (dampening, pinning).
- Anomaly decay (McLean-Pontiff 2016): drop in a predictor's return out-of-sample (data-mining component) and post-publication (arbitrage component). Multiple testing (Harvey-Liu-Zhu 2016): with hundreds of tested factors a t-stat of 2.0 no longer controls false discoveries; hurdle ≈ 3.0.
- Pre-FOMC announcement drift (Lucca-Moench 2015): large average equity return in the 24 hours before scheduled FOMC statements. FOMC cycle (Cieslak-Morse-Vissing-Jorgensen 2019): equity premium concentrated in even weeks from the meeting. Calendar effects (day-of-week, turn-of-month, January, "sell in May") mostly fail data-snooping-adjusted tests (Sullivan-Timmermann-White 2001).

## Mechanisms
- Why index options are expensive (consensus chain): (1) pensions, structured-product hedgers and asset managers are net buyers of index puts (GPP 2009; Chen-Joslin-Ni 2019); (2) sellers are a small set of dealers who cannot hedge jump/vol risk and face margin/capital constraints; (3) they charge a premium proportional to unhedgeable variance that rises when balance sheets are constrained; (4) it appears as negative returns to long puts, straddles and variance swaps and as a steep smirk. Rational counter-chain: jump/crash-averse representative agent (Broadie-Chernov-Johannes 2009) or peso-problem samples. Between the camps, Bollerslev-Todorov (2011) show model-free that rare-event compensation is a large fraction of both equity and variance premia, i.e. much of the index VRP is tail insurance, not diffusive-vol compensation.
- Margin / limits to arbitrage (Santa-Clara-Saretto 2009): margin requirements cap the notional deployable in put writing and can force positions to be closed at a loss in exactly the states where premia are highest; the friction grows with how aggressively OTM puts are written; margins reduce counterparty risk but restrict liquidity supply. This bridges Bondarenko's 39-95%/month returns on premium and single-digit returns on margin capital, and explains why the premium survives.
- Correlation risk premium (Driessen-Maenhout-Vilkov 2009): with S&P 100 options and options on all components, the index/single-name VRP gap is a priced correlation premium; short-index-variance / long-component-variance (dispersion) earns a high alpha frictionlessly but "cannot be exploited with realistic trading frictions"; correlation exposure explains the cross-section of index and single-name option returns.
- Why the VRP predicts equity returns (BTZ 2009): with time-varying vol-of-vol, VRP ∝ vol-of-vol × risk aversion, so a high VRP flags uncertainty premia realized over the next quarter, not at long horizons where valuation ratios dominate. Dew-Becker et al. (2017, 1996-2014): hedging news about future variance at 1-quarter to 14-year horizons was on average costless; only unexpected, transitory realized variance is priced — the premium sits at the very front of the term structure.
- Why skew and variance premia are hard to separate (Kozhan et al. 2013): both compensate the same crash-type state; earning the skew premium while hedging variance earns nothing, so "skew premium" is not a distinct harvestable factor.
- Day/night and nontrading periods (Muravyev-Ni 2020; Jones-Shemesh 2018): stock volatility is much higher intraday than overnight but option prices do not fully reflect it, so delta-hedged option returns are strongly negative close-to-open and positive open-to-close; returns are also significantly lower across weekends/holidays, attributed to persistent mispricing of closed-market variance rather than risk.
- VIX-futures carry (Cheng 2019): the ex-ante VIX premium forecasts realized VIX-futures returns roughly one-for-one but falls or stays flat when ex-ante risk rises because hedging demand falls; falling premia predict rising market and investment risk, so short-VIX carry is largest when least needed and vanishes before the shock.
- How information enters options first: informed traders use options for embedded leverage and to evade short-sale constraints (Easley-O'Hara-Srinivas 1998; Pan-Poteshman 2006; Ge-Lin-Pearson 2016: leverage dominates). Market makers move quotes (hence IVs) on order flow before the stock fully reacts, creating predictive content in CPIV, smirks, O/S and IV changes; strongest where informed trading is concentrated and stock frictions are large; decays as capital arbitrages it (Cremers-Weinbaum). The inventory-risk component of option price impact exceeds the asymmetric-information component (Muravyev 2016).
- Earnings-event mechanics: a scheduled announcement carries an anticipated jump whose variance is embedded in IVs spanning the date and is "large, time-varying and informative about future realized vol" (Dubinsky et al. 2019); earnings-day variance >5× a normal day (Dubinsky-Johannes 2006 WP [uncertain]). The sign of straddle P&L depends on the window: IV rises into the event, so straddles entered 3 days before and exited on the announcement date are positive on average (Gao-Xing-Zhang 2018: investors underestimate announcement uncertainty), whereas holding through and after the announcement is negative on average; retail does the latter, overpays vs realized moves and pays wide spreads (de Silva-Smith-So), and market makers capture the vol crush. Equity side: pre-announcement run-up and post-announcement drift (Frazzini-Lamont; Bernard-Thomas): attention-driven small-investor buying and underreaction. Options spanning elections/summits are more expensive and hedge price, variance and tail risk, more so when the economy is weak (Kelly-Pástor-Veronesi 2016).
- Dealer gamma and intraday dynamics: dealers net short gamma trade with the trend when rebalancing deltas, producing intraday momentum strongest into the close (Baltussen et al. 2021: last-30-minute return predicted by the earlier intraday return across 60+ futures, reversing over subsequent days); net long gamma gives contrarian hedging, lower volatility and strike-pinning on expiration days (Ni-Pearson-Poteshman 2005; Golez-Jackwerth 2012; Ni-Pearson-Poteshman-White 2021: a non-informational hedge-rebalancing channel affecting volatility and the likelihood of large moves [magnitudes uncertain]). In 0DTE SPX, market makers' net gamma is on average positive and stabilizing (Dim-Eraker-Vilkov 2024; Adams et al. 2025). Retail option flow raises single-stock volatility through dealer hedging because retail trades are more often dealer-intermediated and in higher-gamma contracts (Lipson-Tomio-Zhang 2023).
- Retail flow economics: retail options orders are internalized by wholesalers paying PFOF; retail prefers cheap weeklies with ~12.6% average relative spreads (BPS 2023); losses accrue via spreads, overpaying for volatility, and exercise mistakes (unexercised ITM calls before ex-dividend, harvested by "dividend play" arbitrage). Herding by inexperienced retail creates inventory risk for intermediaries and worsens liquidity (Eaton et al. 2022).
- Anomaly decay: publication informs arbitrageurs ⇒ capital flows ⇒ returns fall (McLean-Pontiff attribute ~32 percentage points of the 58% decline to this); the remaining ~26% is data mining / selection on in-sample noise. Any pre-registered test should treat the in-sample effect as an upper bound.
- Pre-FOMC drift mechanisms (unsettled): pre-statement resolution of uncertainty (Lucca-Moench reject several variants; Hu-Pan-Wang-Zhu 2022: a "heightened uncertainty" premium, also before NFP/ISM/GDP, for uncertainty about the announcement's impact that accumulates and resolves just before release, with the VIX as the gauge); informal Fed communication over the cycle (Cieslak et al.); concentration in press-conference meetings (Boguth-Grégoire-Martineau 2019). Drift disappeared after 2015 (Kurov-Wolfe-Gilbert 2021), consistent with lower policy uncertainty and/or post-publication arbitrage.

## Formulas and quantitative rules
```
Zero-beta straddle (Coval-Shumway): w_c*beta_c + w_p*beta_p = 0, w_c + w_p = 1
  beta_c = (dC/dS)*(S/C), beta_p = (dP/dS)*(S/P)   (BS betas; units: elasticities)
  Weekly return = w_c*R_call + w_p*R_put;  CS report mean ≈ -3%/week for ATM S&P index straddles.

Delta-hedged gain over [t, t+tau] (Bakshi-Kapadia):
  Pi = C(t+tau) - C(t) - sum_n Delta(t_n)*(S(t_{n+1}) - S(t_n)) - sum_n r*(C(t_n) - Delta(t_n)*S(t_n))*dt
  E[Pi] ≈ integral of vega * (lambda_vol) ; lambda_vol < 0  =>  E[Pi] < 0.  Report Pi/S or Pi/C.
  Day/night split (Muravyev-Ni): SPX delta-hedged ≈ -0.7%/day = ≈ -1%/day close-to-open + ≈ +0.3%/day open-to-close.

Variance risk premium, two conventions:
  Carr-Wu:  RP = 100*(RV - SW);  LRP = ln(RV/SW).   SPX mean RP = -2.28, LRP = -0.594 (t = -9.5); Jan 1996 - Feb 2003,
            RV, SW = annualized variance (decimal) of a 30-day contract. e^(-0.594) - 1 ≈ -45% simple; LRP is a mean of
            logs, so the arithmetic-mean simple return is less negative.
  BTZ:      VRP_t ≡ IV_t - RV_t,  IV_t = squared VIX at end of month t, RV_t = sum of 5-min squared returns over month t
            (both observable at t; MONTHLY %^2 units ≈ annualized/12).  Means Jan 1990 - Dec 2007: IV 33.23, RV 14.93,
            VRP 18.30 monthly %^2 (≈ 220 annualized %^2; sqrt(12*IV) ≈ 20.0 vs sqrt(12*RV) ≈ 13.4 vol points).
            Robustness variant EVRP_t ≡ IV_t - E_t[RV_{t+1}] (reduced-form RV forecast); corr(VRP, EVRP) = 0.85, t-stat lower.
  Variance-swap P&L:  payoff = N_var*(RV - K^2),  N_var = N_vega/(2K);  expected short-swap P&L per $1 vega ≈ (K^2 - RV)/(2K).
  Carr-Wu SPX: mean sqrt(RV) 18.82%, mean sqrt(SW) 24.41% (means of vols: 24.41^2 - 18.82^2 = 242 only approximates the
            mean variance gap; exact mean 100*(SW - RV) = 2.28 ≈ 228 annualized %^2 per 30-day contract);
            per $1 vega ≈ 228/(2*24.41) ≈ 4.7 vol points (≈ 5.6 points to first order from 24.41 - 18.82).
  Variance beta cross-section (Carr-Wu eq. 54): LRP_j = 0.0201 + 0.2675*beta_V,j (t = 2.72), R^2 = 15.9%.

VRP predictive regression (BTZ 2009):  r_{t->t+h} = a + b*VRP_t + e;  quarterly adj.R^2 = 6.82% (VRP alone, t = 2.86),
  ≈16.8% with P/E added; monthly adj.R^2 = 1.07%. Sample 1990-2007.

Goyal-Saretto sort:  x_i = ln(HV_i / IV_i), HV = 12-month realized vol, IV = ATM 1-month IV [definitions uncertain]; long decile 10, short decile 1.
CPIV spread (Cremers-Weinbaum):  CPIV_i = mean over matched pairs of [IV_call - IV_put], open-interest weighted [uncertain].
  Long top / short bottom quintile => ~50 bp/week (1996-2005).
Smirk (Xing-Zhang-Zhao):  SKEW_i = IV(OTM put, moneyness ≈0.80-0.95) - IV(ATM call, ≈0.95-1.05)  [bands uncertain]
  Steepest minus flattest quintile => -10.9%/yr risk-adjusted; lasts >= 6 months.
O/S (Johnson-So):  O/S_i = option contract volume (x100 shares) / stock share volume, weekly; higher => lower next-week returns.
PNBO (Chen-Joslin-Ni):  monthly public buy-to-open minus sell-to-open volume of DOTM (K/S <= 0.85) SPX puts; in months with a
  negative price-quantity relation, 1 sd decrease => +4.3% (normalized PNBO: +3%) subsequent 3-month log market excess
  return, R^2 24.7% (12.3%); weak in positive price-quantity months. (NBER WP 25573 figures.)

SUE (PEAD):  SUE_i,q = (EPS_i,q - EPS_i,q-4) / sigma(seasonal changes over prior 8 quarters)
MAX (BCW):   MAX_i,t = max daily return of stock i in month t.
BAB:  BAB_t = (1/beta_L)*(r_L - r_f) - (1/beta_H)*(r_H - r_f),  beta-sorted portfolios each levered/delevered to beta 1.

Multiple-testing hurdles (HLZ): Bonferroni p* = alpha/M (M≈300, alpha=0.05 => |t|≈3.8); Holm; BHY (FDR). HLZ: |t| > 3.0.
Anomaly decay arithmetic (McLean-Pontiff): in-sample 0.58%/mo -> out-of-sample 0.40% (-26%) -> post-publication 0.26% (-58%).
  Rule: expected live return of a published cross-sectional signal ≈ 0.4-0.5 x in-sample, before costs.

Worked example (short variance carry, $1 variance notional): IV 20%, expected RV 16% => expected gain = 0.04 - 0.0256 = 0.0144.
  RV 30%: loss = 0.09 - 0.04 = 0.05 (3.5x the expected gain).
  RV 80% (March 2020: VIX record close 82.69 on 16 Mar 2020, 1-month SPX realized vol of the same order [exact RV uncertain];
  Oct 1987 and Oct-Nov 2008 comparable, VIX close 80.86 on 20 Nov 2008, intraday 89.53 on 24 Oct 2008):
  loss = 0.64 - 0.04 = 0.60, ≈ 42x the expected gain. Variance payoffs are quadratic in vol: size a short-variance book
  to the 80%-realized month, or use capped variance (dealers typically cap at 2.5x strike) or vol swaps (loss linear in vol).
  That asymmetry IS the premium.
```

## Empirical magnitudes
- Coval-Shumway (2001, JF 56(3):983-1009): zero-beta ATM S&P index straddles lose ≈3%/week; call returns rise with strike; put returns strongly negative; sample period [uncertain; believed Jan 1990-Oct 1995]. Survival: negative straddle/put returns re-confirmed in every later sample through the 2010s (CJS 2013) and in VRP series into the 2020s; magnitude regime-dependent.
- Bakshi-Kapadia (2003, RFS 16(2):527-566): delta-hedged SPX option gains negative on average, less so for OTM, more so when volatility is high ⇒ negative market volatility risk premium; single-stock delta-hedged gains much less negative [uncertain].
- Muravyev-Ni (2020, JFE 136(1):219-238): delta-hedged SPX option returns average −0.7%/day: ≈−1%/day overnight (close-to-open) and ≈+0.3%/day intraday (open-to-close); the sign flip holds across maturity, moneyness and for equity options. Jones-Shemesh (2018, JF 73(2):861-900): option returns significantly lower over nontrading periods (mostly weekends); not risk; mispricing of closed-market variance [magnitudes not in abstract].
- Bondarenko (2003 RFS method; 2014 QJF 4(3)): S&P 500 futures puts Aug 1987-Dec 2000; selling unhedged 1-month ATM puts returned ≈39%/month and deep-OTM ≈95%/month on premium (not on margin capital); ATM Jensen alpha ≈23%/month; worst subperiod (8/87-6/90, incl. Oct-1987) still 12-27%/month; ATM buyers break even only with 1987-size crashes 1.3×/year; QJF: no model in a broad class (any beliefs, peso adjustment) explains it. Per unit of margin these are single-digit monthly returns with crash tails (Santa-Clara-Saretto 2009).
- Jones (2006, JF 61(5):2325-2363): all S&P 500 futures options 1986-2000; nonlinear factor models with priced vol/jump factors "contribute but are insufficient to explain the magnitudes", worst for short-term OTM puts.
- Broadie-Chernov-Johannes (2009, RFS 22(11):4493-4529): raw put returns (1987-2005 [uncertain]) are statistically indistinguishable from Black-Scholes/Heston because of sampling noise; straddles and delta-hedged returns are informative and consistent with jump risk premia plus estimation risk. Never cite raw OTM put averages as mispricing without a simulated benchmark. Johannes-Kaeck-Seeger-Shah (2024 WP, SSRN 4729757) extend the model benchmark to 1DTE options [findings uncertain; abstract not retrievable].
- Bollerslev-Todorov (2011, JF 66(6):2165-2211): jump tails under P from high-frequency data and under Q from short-maturity OTM options; rare-event compensation is a "large fraction" of both equity and variance risk premia; Investor Fears index large and time-varying. Kelly-Pástor-Veronesi (2016, JF 71(5):2417-2480): options spanning national elections/global summits are more expensive and hedge price, variance and tail risk; protection worth more when the economy is weak and political uncertainty high.
- Constantinides-Jackwerth-Savov (2013, RAPS 3(2):229-257): 1986-2012 [uncertain] panel of unit-beta SPX call/put portfolios; market plus crisis factors (price jumps, vol jumps, liquidity) not rejected even with premia constrained to equity values ⇒ index-option returns not anomalous relative to equities once crisis factors enter.
- Goyal-Saretto (2009, JFE 94(2):310-326): 1996-2006 [uncertain], OptionMetrics equity options; sort on ln(HV/IV); long-short decile straddles ≈22-23%/month gross [uncertain; secondary 22.7%], delta-hedged long-short ≈1-2%/month [uncertain]; robust to factors, industries, liquidity. Costs: Do-Foster-Gray (2016, J. Futures Markets 36(2):107-126) test it on ASX (Australian) equity options with tick-level quotes: transaction costs erode much of the profit, ignoring initial margins dramatically overstates short-option returns, profitability hinges on executing well inside quoted spreads — indirect evidence, not a US replication; for US names rely on Goyal-Saretto's own cost section and Muravyev-Pearson's effective-spread estimates; US net-of-cost verdict [uncertain]. Survival: HV−IV remains among the most robust option cross-section signals in 2010-2020 samples (Zhan et al. 2022; Bali et al. 2023).
- Cross-section beyond HV−IV: Cao-Han (2013, JFE 108(1):231-249): delta-hedged equity option returns decrease monotonically in idiosyncratic volatility; limits-to-arbitrage proxies weaken the relation ≈40%. Vasquez (2017, JFQA 52(6):2727-2754): straddles on stocks with steep IV term-structure slopes outperform flat-slope straddles, unexplained by risk or jump factors [magnitudes uncertain]. Heston-Jones-Khorram-Li-Mo (2023, JF 78(6):3141-3192): ATM straddles on stocks with high past option returns significantly beat low-past-return straddles at 6-36-month horizons; no long-run reversal; robust to delta hedging, OTM options, factor adjustment and IV controls; trading costs unrelated to profits. Zhan-Han-Cao-Tong (2022, RFS 35(3):1394-1442): delta-hedged call-writing returns predicted by price, profit margin, profitability (negative) and cash holdings, cash-flow variance, issuance, external financing, distress, analyst dispersion (positive); annual Sharpe ratios above 2, profitable after transaction costs; two option-based factors explain them, equity factors do not. Bali-Beckmeyer-Mörke-Weigert (2023, RFS 36(9):3548-3602): 1996-2020, >12M observations; nonlinear ML long-short equity-option portfolios profitable after transaction costs; option-based characteristics dominate; attributed to informational frictions and mispricing.
- Carr-Wu (2009, RFS 22(3):1311-1341; figures from the 2004 WP [uncertain vs published]): Jan 1996-Feb 2003, 30-day horizon; SPX mean sqrt(RV)=18.82% (sd 7.23) vs synthetic swap rate sqrt(SW)=24.41% (sd 6.21); mean 100×(RV−SW)=−2.28; mean ln(RV/SW)=−0.594 (t=−9.5), the continuously-compounded excess return to a long 30-day variance swap ("over −50 percent per month"; ≈−45% simple) (OEX −0.51, DJX −0.53, NDX −0.21); 21 of 35 single stocks have significantly negative log VRP but RP insignificant for all but 3; cross-section of log VRP positively related to "variance beta" (covariance with SPX realized variance): slope 0.27 (t 2.72), R² ≈16% — significant but weak.
- Bollerslev-Tauchen-Zhou (2009, RFS 22(11):4463-4492): Jan 1990-Dec 2007; VRP = IV − RV with lagged realized variance, monthly %²; means IV 33.23, RV 14.93, VRP 18.30 (≈220 annualized %², ≈20.0 vs 13.4 vol points); quarterly adj R² 6.82% alone, ≈16.8% with P/E, ≈20% with more regressors; monthly 1.07%; dominates P/E, default spread and CAY at the quarterly horizon. Survival: Bollerslev-Marrone-Xu-Zhou (2014, JFQA 49(3):633-661): finite-sample biases cannot explain it; similar patterns in France, Germany, Japan, Switzerland, the Netherlands, Belgium and the UK; a "global" VRP in panel regressions predicts even more strongly; strongest around the quarterly (≈2-4-month) horizon [horizon uncertain]. Regime-dependent and weak monthly.
- Kozhan-Neuberger-Schneider (2013, RFS 26(9):2174-2203): skew premium >40% of the SPX IV-slope; skew and variance premia co-move; hedged skew strategy earns nothing significant. Sample 1996-2012 [uncertain].
- Garleanu-Pedersen-Poteshman (2009, RFS 22(10):4259-4299): index options expensive (IV minus expected vol) with positive end-user demand, esp. OTM puts; equity options not expensive on average with small negative end-user demand; demand explains index expensiveness/smirk over time and single-stock expensiveness cross-sectionally. CBOE position data, 1996-2001 [uncertain].
- Chen-Joslin-Ni (2019, RFS 32(1):228-265; NBER WP 25573): Jan 1991-Dec 2012; PNBO negatively related to option expensiveness, more so when jump risk is high; in months with a negative price-quantity relation (supply shocks), a 1 sd fall in PNBO ⇒ +4.3% (+3% normalized) subsequent 3-month log market excess return, R² 24.7% (12.3%), stronger when jump risk is above median, much weaker in positive price-quantity months; low PNBO also predicts higher excess returns on high-yield bonds, hedge funds, carry and commodities and lower on long Treasuries and pay-fixed SPX variance swaps; low PNBO ⇔ tighter intermediary constraints ⇔ broker-dealer deleveraging. Published-version coefficient [unverified].
- Pan-Poteshman (2006, RFS 19(3):871-908): CBOE open-buy put/call ratio; low-ratio stocks beat high-ratio by >40 bp next day and >1% next week (1990-2001 [uncertain]); stronger with concentrated informed trading and higher leverage; private information. Requires signed open/close data; public total-volume ratios are far weaker.
- Cremers-Weinbaum (2010, JFQA 45(2):335-367): 1996-2005; high-CPIV stocks beat low-CPIV by ≈50 bp/week; not a hard-to-borrow effect; predictability declines within the sample. A 2024-extended replication (thesis, secondary [uncertain]) still finds 1- and 4-week significance.
- Xing-Zhang-Zhao (2010, JFQA 45(3):641-662): 1996-2005; steepest-smirk quintile underperforms flattest by 10.9%/yr risk-adjusted; persists ≥6 months; steep-smirk firms have the worst next-quarter earnings surprises. A 2026 preprint on 2015-2017 data reportedly finds the same sign at ~half the magnitude (secondary [uncertain]).
- An-Ang-Bali-Cakici (2014, JF 69(5):2279-2338): sample period [uncertain]; decile spread on past-month call-IV change ≈ +1%/month (put-IV change predicts negatively), persisting up to 6 months; stock returns also predict IV changes; slow information diffusion.
- Bali-Hovakimian (2009, Management Science 55(11):1797-1812): 1996-2005; RV−IV spread negatively and call−put IV spread positively predict stock returns (volatility-risk and jump-risk proxies); magnitudes of order 0.5-1%/month across quintiles [uncertain].
- Johnson-So (2012, JFE 106(2)): high O/S ⇒ lower next-week/month returns, stronger under short-sale constraints and before bad news. Ge-Lin-Pearson (2016, JFE 120(3):601-622): with signed volume, open-buy calls are the strongest predictor, then close-sell calls; synthetic shorts carry no extra information; channel = embedded leverage, not short-sale costs.
- Augustin-Brenner-Subrahmanyam (2019, Management Science 65(12):5697-5720): 1,859 US takeovers 1996-2012; ≈25% show abnormal pre-announcement option volume, concentrated in short-dated OTM calls; >half unexplained by rumours, disclosed insider trades, stock leakage, deal predictability or 13D filings; SEC litigates only ≈8% of sample deals.
- Dubinsky-Johannes-Kaeck-Seeger (2019, RFS 32(2):646-687): earnings-date jump uncertainty is large, time-varying and forecasts realized vol; single-name SV models must include it. Gao-Xing-Zhang (2018, JFQA 53(6):2587-2617): individual-stock straddles earn negative returns in general, but ATM straddles held from 3 trading days before an earnings announcement through the announcement date earn +3.34% on average (highly significant; sample period [uncertain]); larger for smaller, higher-vol, higher-kurtosis, more volatile-surprise, lower-volume/higher-cost names.
- Bernard-Thomas (1989, JAR 27 Supplement:1-36): 1974-1986 NYSE/AMEX; top-minus-bottom SUE decile earns ≈4% abnormal over the 60 trading days after announcement (≈8% for small firms) [magnitudes uncertain], with a disproportionate share around the next announcement. Survival: PEAD weakened substantially after 2000 in large caps; persists in small/illiquid names [uncertain].
- Frazzini-Lamont (2007 NBER WP 13090): 1973-2004; value-weighted expected-announcers minus non-announcers 61 bp/month (≈7%/yr, t>5; PDF-confirmed); refined strategies 7-18%/yr; visible since 1927; strongest for stocks with high past announcement-window volume; linked to imputed small-investor buying. Survival post-2004 positive but cost-sensitive [uncertain].
- Jegadeesh-Titman (1993, JF 48(1):65-91): 1965-1989; 6-month/6-month winner-minus-loser ≈1%/month. Survival: positive but with crashes (2009; Daniel-Moskowitz 2016) and large post-publication decay in US large caps [magnitudes uncertain]. Short-term reversal (Jegadeesh 1990; Lehmann 1990): 1-month/1-week losers beat winners by ≈1-2%/month gross [uncertain]; mostly a liquidity-provision return eaten by costs.
- Ang-Hodrick-Xing-Zhang (2006, JF 61(1):259-299): 1963-2000; highest-IVOL quintile earns ≈1.06%/month less than lowest (value-weighted). Frazzini-Pedersen (2014, JFE 111(1):1-25): US BAB 1926-2012 alpha ≈0.7%/month, Sharpe ≈0.78 [uncertain]; holds across countries and asset classes. Bali-Cakici-Whitelaw (2011, JFE 99(2):427-446): 1962-2005; high-minus-low MAX decile ≈ −1.03%/month raw, −1.18% alpha. Boyer-Vorkink (2014, JF 69(4):1485-1527): option portfolios sorted on ex-ante skewness differ by 10-50%/week; compensation to intermediaries supplying unhedgeable lottery-like options.
- Cheng (2019, RFS 32(1):180-227): ex-ante VIX-futures premia predict ex-post VIX-futures returns with a coefficient near one; premia fall or stay flat when ex-ante risk rises (hedging demand and trader exposures fall together), so falling premia predict higher subsequent market and investment risk. Case study of crowded short-vol carry: 5 Feb 2018 ("Volmageddon"), VIX closed 37.32, +115.6% from 17.31% on the day (verified); the XIV inverse-VIX ETN lost most of its value and was terminated by its issuer [details uncertain]. Investable short-index-vol benchmarks: Cboe BXM (covered call) and PUT (put-write) indices, history back to 1986; Israelov-Nielsen (2015, FAJ 71(6):44-57) decompose covered-call returns into passive equity, active (reversal-like) equity timing and short volatility; long-run BXM/PUT return, vol and drawdown statistics [not verified].
- Bryzgalova-Pavlova-Sikorskaya (2023, JF 78(6):3465-3514): retail >60% of options volume around 2020-21 [as of 2021], measured with a retail proxy built from wholesaler flags in transaction data (PFOF-internalized single-leg flow), validated against equity retail proxies and brokerage-outage dips — not the same definition as Cboe's customer/size flags in 0DTE studies, so shares are not directly comparable; ≈90% of options PFOF from three wholesalers; retail prefers weeklies with 12.6% average relative spread; loses on average; retail inflows coincide with more suboptimally unexercised calls.
- de Silva-Smith-So (2023 WP "Losing is Optional..."): retail buys options before earnings, especially where expected abnormal vol is high; average losses 5-9%, 10-14% for high-expected-vol events [uncertain; secondary]; sources: overpaying vs realized vol, spreads, holding weeks after the event.
- Eaton-Green-Roseman-Wu (2022, JFE 146(2):502-528): Robinhood outages ⇒ smaller imbalances, better liquidity, lower volatility in high-retail-interest stocks; traditional-broker outages ⇒ opposite. Lipson-Tomio-Zhang (2023 WP, SSRN 4383463): Robinhood's commission-free option launch (two difference-in-differences designs) raises underlying-stock volatility via dealer hedging of retail flow (more often dealer-intermediated, higher-gamma contracts); because the flow is uninformed, liquidity improves and price discovery falls [effect sizes not in abstract].
- 0DTE literature (as of 2025): (a) Beckmeyer-Branger-Gayda (2023 WP, SSRN 4404704): retail identified via the Automated Price Improvement Mechanism; >75% of retail TRADES in SPX options involve 0DTE contracts (2022-23); retail gets lower effective spreads via price improvement but still loses ≈$350k/day, ≈$125M cumulative (Neudata summary); share of losses from transaction costs and "first-10-minute trades profitable" [uncertain; not in abstract or Neudata]. (b) Vilkov (Frankfurt School, 2025 WP, title [uncertain], Sep 2016-Jan 2024): 0DTE VRP 10:00-to-close ≈0.001% of index (economically nil); realized skewness, not variance, drives daily P&L; static hold-to-expiry structures have Sharpe ≈0 [secondary summary only]. (c) Brogaard-Han-Won (2023 WP, SSRN 4426358): index 0DTE volume 0.08M contracts/month (Jan 2011) → 34.4M (Aug 2023), ≈48% of index option trading; instrumenting with the staggered introduction of index weekly options, a 1-sd rise in 0DTE trading raises volatility by 9.10% of its mean (15.91% of its sd); positive after controlling for market-maker gamma hedging; attributed to speculative retail. (d) Dim-Eraker-Vilkov (2024 WP, SSRN 4692190): market makers' net 0DTE gamma is on average positive and negatively related to future intraday volatility; positive (negative) MM gamma strengthens intraday reversal (momentum); consistent with delta hedging, not informed trading. Adams-Dim-Eraker-Fontaine-Ornthanalai-Vilkov (2025 WP, SSRN 5641974): 0DTEs dampen market volatility through a shift in MM hedging needs, driven by longer-dated positions that become 0DTE rather than same-day trading; intraday hedging-need changes predict stronger order-flow reversals, lower momentum, lower volatility. Bandi-Fusari-Renò ("0DTE option pricing") [findings uncertain]. (e) Cboe (Xu, 2 May 2025): SPX 0DTE ≈2M contracts/day (>5× in three years); retail ≈50-60% of SPX 0DTE trading (57% → 47% in the early-April 2025 vol spike, ≈60% by late April); >95% of 0DTE trades in limited-risk formats, ≈4% naked short; customer positioning "extremely balanced"; net MM gamma hedging "at best 0.2% of SPX daily liquidity". Exchange-published, single-venue, not peer-reviewed; the article does not give 0DTE's share of total SPX volume — the ≈50% share in 2024 and 59% for full-year 2025 per Cboe's 2026-01-06 release.

- Muravyev (2016, JF 71(2):673-708): decomposes option price impact into inventory-risk and asymmetric-information components; both large, inventory larger; inventory-driven order imbalances move option prices ≈5× more than earlier estimates; past order imbalances predict daily option returns better than other common predictors. Muravyev-Pearson (2020, RFS 33(11):4973-5014): option prices are predictable at high frequency and many traders time executions (buy when fair value is near the ask, sell near the bid); published abstract: timing traders' effective spreads are "less than 40% of the size of conventional measures", the overall average effective spread is ≈one-quarter smaller than conventional, and price-impact measures are also affected (working-paper abstract: "one-third smaller" average, "five times smaller" for timing trades, price impact overstated >2×); most option trades come from execution-timing (prop/institutional, algorithmic) investors [uncertain; WP summary]; materially changes after-cost profitability.

- Harvey-Liu-Zhu (2016, RFS 29(1):5-68): 316 factors from 313 papers (PDF-confirmed); adjusted hurdle |t|>3.0; "many published findings are likely false". Hou-Xue-Zhang (2020, RFS 33(5):2019-2133): 452 anomalies; 65% fail at |t|≥1.96, 82% fail |t|≥2.78 once microcaps are controlled (abstract-confirmed). Chen-Zimmermann (2022, Critical Finance Review 11(2):207-264): 319 characteristics; of the 161 clearly significant originally, 98% reproduce with |t|>1.96 using the original methods; 44 mixed-evidence predictors average t≈2; reproduced-on-original t-stat slope 0.90, R² 83%.

Disagreement with HXZ is about test design (Bayesian shrinkage and clustering vs frequentist hurdles).

Kurov-Wolfe-Gilbert (2021, FRL 40:101781): through Dec 2019, the drift "essentially disappeared after 2015" after first appearing in press-conference announcements [post-2015 mean ≈0, uncertain]. Boguth-Grégoire-Martineau (2019, JFQA 54(6):2327-2353): after press conferences began (April 2011), investors lowered expectations for non-press-conference meetings, attention/volume concentrated in press-conference meetings, and the market risk premium is larger on press-conference days. Hu-Pan-Wang-Zhu (2022, JFE 145(3):909-936): large overnight pre-announcement returns also before NFP, ISM and GDP without abnormal ex-ante variance; compensation for uncertainty about the announcement's impact [VIX run-up conditioning uncertain]. Savor-Wilson (2013, JFQA 48(2):343-375): 1958-2009; 11.4 bp on scheduled inflation/unemployment/interest-rate announcement days vs 1.1 bp otherwise; >60% of the cumulative annual equity premium; Sharpe ≈10× higher; risk-free rate measurably lower on those days (abstract-confirmed).
- Cieslak-Morse-Vissing-Jorgensen (2019, JF 74(5):2201-2248): since 1994 the equity premium is earned entirely in even weeks (0, 2, 4, 6) of FOMC-cycle time; even-week magnitude several tens of bp per week [uncertain]; mechanism: more accommodative-than-expected policy transmitted via informal Fed communication. Post-2016 stability [uncertain].

## Practitioner heuristics
- Index puts/straddles are net sellers' markets: a DELTA-HEDGED long index option bleeds ≈ vega × (IV − RV), on average ≈5.5-6.5 SPX vol points in 1990-2007 samples (Carr-Wu 1996-2003: 24.4 vs 18.8 = 5.6; BTZ 1990-2007: ≈20.0 vs 13.4 = 6.6), with the gap roughly proportional to the vol level and negative in crisis months (1998, 2008, 2020); an unhedged straddle's or put's P&L is dominated by the realized path (delta), not by the gap. Size short vol so a 1987/2008/2020 month is survivable — the average return is compensation for exactly those months. Post-2010 average gap [uncertain; only 1990-2007 samples quoted].
- Theta is not earned uniformly: short-premium P&L accrues overnight and across weekends/holidays (Muravyev-Ni; Jones-Shemesh), while long-premium delta-hedged positions have historically made money intraday. Mark backtests at open and close separately or the result is an artefact of the overnight/intraday mix.
- Single-name options are not expensive on average (GPP); their VRP is mostly the market component (Carr-Wu variance beta, which explains only ≈16% of the cross-section), and the index/single-name gap is a correlation premium (DMV 2009). Selling single-name vol is mostly selling index vol plus idiosyncratic noise; dispersion harvests the correlation premium in theory but DMV find it is not exploitable after frictions.
- HV−IV (Goyal-Saretto) is one of the most-replicated cross-sectional option signals but not uniquely so: option momentum (Heston et al. 2023), idiosyncratic volatility (Cao-Han 2013), IV term-structure slope (Vasquez 2017) and the Zhan et al. (2022)/Bali et al. (2023) characteristic sets are of similar order; treat HV−IV as a baseline, not the signal. Implement delta-hedged, rebalance monthly, and assume ≈75% of quoted cost for a naive taker and ≈35-40% for a disciplined passive/timing executor (Muravyev-Pearson); the strategies survive at the latter, not reliably at the former.
- Option-implied stock signals (CPIV, smirk, IV changes, O/S) are weekly-to-monthly signals with 1996-2010 magnitudes of 0.5-1%/month; assume at most half live (McLean-Pontiff), strongest in small, hard-to-short names, i.e. the least tradable.
- Pre-event: unusual short-dated OTM call volume is informative before M&A (ABS 2019) but false positives are common; prefer open/close-signed data, else volume vs own history plus IV change.
- Earnings: the sign of straddle P&L depends on the window. The run-up window (t−3 to the announcement date) is positive on average (+3.34%, GXZ 2018) because IV rises into the event; holding through and after the announcement is negative on average (implied moves slightly exceed realized; vol crush = market-maker premium), and retail, which does the latter and pays 10%+ spreads, loses 5-14% (de Silva et al. [uncertain]). The equity announcement premium (+61 bp/month) and PEAD are too small per event to trade through option spreads in large caps.
- Dealer gamma: the sign of aggregate dealer gamma is a usable regime indicator for intraday trend vs mean-reversion into the close (Baltussen et al.; Dim-Eraker-Vilkov for 0DTE) and for expiration-day pinning; but Cboe's 2025 estimate that 0DTE net hedging is ≤0.2% of SPX liquidity means do not attribute every intraday move to "gamma". Retail-driven dealer hedging does measurably raise single-stock volatility (Lipson-Tomio-Zhang).
- 0DTE: static premium-selling or buying has Sharpe ≈0 after costs (Vilkov [uncertain]); retail 0DTE loses despite price improvement, with transaction costs a large [uncertain] share; the only documented edge is timing/skewness-conditional, which is hard. Treat 0DTE flow data as positioning information, not a return source.
- FOMC/macro days: the scheduled-announcement-day premium (Savor-Wilson: 11.4 vs 1.1 bp/day, 1958-2009, >60% of the annual equity premium, Sharpe ≈10×) is statistically robust but economically small per event (≈10 bp) and cannot be harvested through option bid-ask spreads; the pre-FOMC 24-hour drift is the fragile, post-2015 casualty; the even-week FOMC-cycle pattern is unreplicated post-2016 [uncertain]. Size any macro-calendar bet as a weak prior, not a signal.
- Calendar effects: treat turn-of-the-month as the only one with reasonable survivorship; treat day-of-week, holiday and "Santa" effects as noise unless a data-snooping-adjusted test is shown.
- Any backtested signal: require |t|≥3 (HLZ), out-of-sample confirmation and a mechanism; expect 26-58% decay; if the sample ends before 2010 with no post-2015 replication, assume half-size at best.
- Where heuristics break: short-vol carry in long low-vol regimes — 2017 ended with Volmageddon on 5 Feb 2018 (VIX +115.6% to 37.32; XIV ETN terminated [details uncertain]); H1-2024 ended with the 5 Aug 2024 yen-carry unwind [VIX figures uncertain] — until a vol-of-vol shock; per Cheng (2019) the VIX premium falls as risk builds, so the carry disappears before the shock. Also: informed-trading signals when retail dominates option volume (post-2020) because order flow becomes noise-heavy; dealer-gamma heuristics when positioning data are stale or customer flow is balanced (Cboe 2025).

## Common misconceptions and pitfalls
- "Selling puts returns 39%/month": Bondarenko's figures are returns on premium; per unit of margin capital they are single-digit percent per month with 1987-type tails, and margin calls force liquidation at the worst moments (Santa-Clara-Saretto).
- "Raw OTM put returns prove mispricing": Broadie-Chernov-Johannes show raw put-return t-stats are uninformative at sample lengths of decades; use straddles/delta-hedged returns.
- Confusing VRP sign conventions (Carr-Wu negative, BTZ positive) and units: BTZ's 18.30 is monthly %² and ≈220 annualized, comparable to Carr-Wu's ≈228, not 12× smaller; vol points and logs differ again.
- "IV − RV bleed" applies to an unhedged straddle: it does not; only the delta-hedged expectation is ≈ vega × (IV − RV).
- Treating the skew premium as separately harvestable: it is not (Kozhan et al.).
- Treating theta as earned uniformly: SPX delta-hedged returns are ≈−1%/day overnight and ≈+0.3%/day intraday (Muravyev-Ni); a close-only backtest hides this.
- Using total put/call volume ratios as if they were Pan-Poteshman's open-buy ratio: the public ratio mixes hedging, closing and market-maker trades and has little predictive power.
- Assuming in-sample magnitudes: nearly every 1996-2005/2010 option-signal paper predates the retail/0DTE/PFOF regime; Cremers-Weinbaum already saw decay within their sample.
- Attributing all intraday momentum or pinning to dealer gamma: leveraged-ETF rebalancing, closing auctions and index flows leave similar signatures.
- "0DTE will blow up the market": no peer-reviewed evidence of systematic destabilization through 2025; Cboe data show balanced customer positioning and small hedging flows; Dim-Eraker-Vilkov and Adams et al. find positive MM gamma and dampened volatility, Brogaard-Han-Won find higher volatility; unresolved.
- "Retail is always wrong": retail options losses come mainly from spreads and timing (BPS; de Silva et al.), not reliably contrarian direction; Eaton et al. is about liquidity/herding, not return predictability.
- Equating "fails to replicate" (HXZ) with "false": much of HXZ's failure rate is microcap/weighting; JKP and Chen-Zimmermann find most factors replicate. All agree large-cap, value-weighted, post-publication magnitudes are far below headline numbers.
- Reading the pre-FOMC drift as live: it is the textbook example of an effect that disappeared after publication/regime change; do not lump the robust Savor-Wilson announcement-day premium in with it.
- Governance note for this repository: none of the above concerns SPY reactions at the prior session's high/low; the general support/resistance literature (Osler 2000; BLL 1992; STW 1999) is the correct prior, and it is weak after data-snooping adjustment.

## Contested or uncertain claims
- Rational vs behavioural origin of index-option expensiveness: BCJ 2009 and CJS 2013 (jump/crisis premia suffice) vs Bondarenko 2014 and Jones 2006 (no reasonable model matches short-dated OTM puts); demand/intermediary papers (GPP, CJN, Santa-Clara-Saretto) sit between: rational given constrained intermediaries, not given a frictionless representative agent. Bollerslev-Todorov (2011) quantify, model-free, that rare-event compensation is a large fraction of the VRP without settling whether the fear is rational.
- Whether option-implied stock predictability reflects private information (Pan-Poteshman; XZZ), leverage-driven slow diffusion (Ge-Lin-Pearson; An et al.), or since-arbitraged mispricing (Cremers-Weinbaum); post-2015 magnitudes [uncertain].
- Goyal-Saretto profitability net of costs: Do-Foster-Gray (2016) find profits largely eroded at quoted spreads and after margins on ASX data (not a US test); Muravyev-Pearson imply conventional effective-spread costs overstate true costs by ≈25% on average and >60% for execution-timing traders. Reviewer resolution (2026-10-14): the published RFS abstract gives "one-quarter smaller" and "<40% of conventional"; a working-paper abstract gives "one-third"/"five times"/price impact >2×; this file uses the published figures.
- BTZ units resolution (2026-10-14): one reviewer read BTZ's IV_t as annualized %²; Table 1's mean IV of 33.23 is only consistent with monthly %² (an annualized VIX² of 33 would mean VIX ≈ 5.8; sqrt(12×33.23) ≈ 20 matches the 1990-2007 VIX average), so monthly units are used here [scaling inferred; BTZ state only "squared VIX"].
- Whether HV−IV is "the" cross-sectional option signal: not established; option momentum, IVOL, term-structure slope and ML characteristic sets are comparable (Heston et al. 2023; Cao-Han 2013; Vasquez 2017; Zhan et al. 2022; Bali et al. 2023).
- 0DTE and underlying volatility: Brogaard-Han-Won (increase; instrumented 2011-2023 index 0DTE volume) vs Dim-Eraker-Vilkov and Adams et al. (positive MM gamma; 0DTEs dampen volatility via hedging-need shifts) vs Cboe (hedging flows de minimis). No peer-reviewed resolution as of 2025.
- Replication crisis: HLZ and HXZ (most factors fail higher hurdles) vs JKP and Chen-Zimmermann (most replicate); the disagreement is methodological (hurdles, weighting, Bayesian priors, clustering).
- Pre-FOMC drift: disappeared (Kurov et al.) vs heightened-uncertainty premium conditional on the uncertainty build-up (Hu et al. 2022) vs press-conference specific (Boguth-Grégoire-Martineau). FOMC even-week cycle: Fed information leakage (Cieslak et al.) vs sample-specific noise [uncertain; no specific rebuttal verified].
- Earnings announcement premium persistence post-2004 and PEAD in large caps post-2000: both weakened; current magnitudes [uncertain]. Retail dollar losses in options (BPS working-paper versions report a multi-billion 2019-2021 figure) [uncertain exact].
- Stress magnitudes: 1-month SPX realized vol in Oct 1987, Oct-Nov 2008 and Mar 2020 (≈80%+), XIV's termination loss, and 5 Aug 2024 VIX levels are [uncertain]; verified only: VIX closes 5 Feb 2018 (37.32), 20 Nov 2008 (80.86; the 80.74 figure is the 21 Nov 2008 intraday high), 16 Mar 2020 (82.69) and the 24 Oct 2008 intraday high (89.53).
