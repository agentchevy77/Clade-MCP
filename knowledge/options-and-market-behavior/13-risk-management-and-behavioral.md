## Core concepts
- **Fat tails / excess kurtosis**: kurtosis is the standardized fourth moment of returns; a normal has kurtosis 3 (excess 0). Index daily returns have excess kurtosis far above 0, so 4+ standard-deviation ("sigma") days occur orders of magnitude more often than a Gaussian predicts. Sample kurtosis is dominated by a handful of days (1987-10-19, Oct 2008, Mar 2020) and is unstable. "Sigma counts" of crash days are inflated because sigma (trailing realized vol) is lowest right before shocks; this is the root of why vol-based sizing understates risk.
- **Jump risk**: a discontinuous move that cannot be delta-hedged through. **Gap risk**: the overnight/weekend version; news arrives while the market is closed. Single names gap on earnings, M&A, FDA, fraud; indices gap on macro/geopolitical news and foreign-session contagion.
- **Vol-based sizing**: size = risk budget / (notional x forecast sigma). Understates risk because (1) sigma is backward-looking and conditional; (2) option P&L is nonlinear in the move (gamma, vanna, volga); (3) implied vol jumps with spot (daily correlation of SPX returns with VIX changes about -0.75 to -0.8 over 2004-2025, nearer -0.9 in stress clusters [approx]), hitting short-vega books the same day as gamma.
- **Stress test / scenario matrix**: full revaluation of the book over a grid of joint spot and vol shocks (e.g., spot -20/-10/-5/0/+5/+10/+20% by IV -10/-5/0/+5/+10/+20 points), plus term-structure shifts (front-end spike, inversion), skew shifts (put wing steepening), correlation-to-1 and liquidity haircuts (exits at bid/ask with spreads widened 2-5x). Output: worst-cell P&L and margin requirement.
- **VaR and expected shortfall (ES)**: 1-day 99% VaR is a loss quantile and says nothing about the loss beyond it; for short-gamma/short-vega books that loss is convex, so parametric (delta-normal) VaR understates risk and is nearly uninformative alone. Practice: full-revaluation historical simulation or Monte Carlo with stochastic vol/jumps, ES (mean loss beyond the quantile; Basel FRTB replaced VaR with ES) reported beside the stress matrix. VaR limits calibrated on calm windows did not prevent the Feb 2018 and Mar 2020 short-vol losses.
- **Greek limits**: desks also govern books by dollar gamma per 1% move, dollar vega per vol point by expiry bucket (front-month IV moves roughly 2-3x 6-month IV in a shock [approx]), normalized vega, beta-weighted delta and the theta/gamma breakeven move (formulas below).
- **Portfolio margin (PM)**: US risk-based margin under FINRA Rule 4210(g) using OCC's TIMS: the account is revalued at ten equidistant points over +6%/-8% (high-cap broad-based indices such as SPX), +/-10% (non-high-cap broad-based indices) or +/-15% (single equities, narrow-based indices), minimum $0.375 per contract times the multiplier; the requirement is the largest scenario loss. 4210(g) sets no minimum equity for a PM account holding only listed products; the $5m floor applies to customers carrying unlisted derivatives (opening orders stop from the fourth business day if not restored within three); PM accounts under $5m must be margined for intraday risk ((g)(1)(K)); broker house minimums of about $100k-$125k are policy, not rule [approx]. **Regulation T / strategy-based margin** (4210(f)(2)(E)): naked short listed option = 100% of option value plus 20% of underlying value (equity, narrow/industry index) or 15% (broad-based index), less the out-of-the-money amount, but not less than option value plus 10% (of the strike for puts, of the underlying for calls); OTC options 30%/20% with the same 10% floor; spreads: the lesser of the short-leg requirement or the maximum loss. **Buying-power reduction (BPR)**: margin a trade consumes. **Margin expansion**: BPR grows with vol and losses, so 50% usage in calm can exceed 100% in a crash (mechanism below).
- **Intraday margin / day-trading rules**: FINRA Notice 26-10 (2026-04-20; SR-FINRA-2025-017, SEC order 34-105226 of 2026-04-14) replaced the pattern-day-trader designation and its $25,000 minimum equity, effective 2026-06-04 (phase-in allowed to 2027-10-20), with an intraday margin deficit: on any day with an "IML-reducing transaction" the member computes the largest intraday shortfall of equity versus maintenance margin (one calculation per day suffices), to be satisfied "as promptly as possible" (outstanding to the 15th business day); habitual non-payment plus a deficit unpaid by the fifth business day brings a 90-day bar on new or larger short positions or debits (de minimis: lesser of 5% of equity or $1,000). Good-faith and PM accounts are outside the calculation.
- **Defined risk**: max loss known at entry (long options, verticals, iron condors, butterflies). **Undefined risk**: bounded only by the underlying going to zero (short put: strike x 100 minus premium per contract) or unbounded (short call, short stock). Short index strangles are undefined on both sides. Defined-risk BPR is not the worst-case cash need: early assignment on one leg creates a stock position with a Reg T requirement that can exceed net liquidation value (mechanism below).
- **Notional exposure**: a short put is long stock worth strike x 100 per contract on the downside; a book of short puts across tickers is a leveraged long-equity position whose correlation goes to about 1 in stress.
- **Liquidity sizing**: size per strike/expiry as a fraction of open interest and average daily volume, estimate days-to-liquidate at 3x normal spreads, and mark the book at the far side of the widened market; mid-marked NLV overstates recoverable equity.
- **Kelly criterion**: the bet fraction maximizing expected log growth; **fractional Kelly** (commonly 1/4-1/2 Kelly) trades growth for lower variance and robustness to parameter error. **Risk of ruin**: probability equity ever falls to a given fraction of start. **Drawdown asymmetry**: a loss of d needs a gain of d/(1-d) to recover.
- **Volatility targeting**: scale exposure inversely to forecast vol. **Fixed-fraction sizing**: risk a constant percentage of current equity (geometric, shrinks after losses). **Fixed-dollar sizing**: constant dollar risk (arithmetic; does not compound or shrink).
- **Variance/volatility risk premium (VRP)**: implied variance exceeds realized on average for index options; short premium harvests it. "Picking up pennies in front of a steamroller": high win rate, small gains, rare catastrophic loss (negative skew). Whether it is compensated depends on price and sizing, not on the win rate.
- **Survivorship bias**: track records come from strategies that have not yet met their tail; failed products and funds (XIV, LJM, Malachite, Allianz Structured Alpha, OptionSellers.com) leave the databases.
- **Probability of profit (POP)**: model probability a trade finishes above breakeven. **Expectancy**: E = p x W - (1-p) x L; a 70-80% POP says nothing about E until the loss distribution is specified.
- **Assignment risk**: short American-style options can be exercised any time (dividend capture on calls; deep ITM puts when extrinsic value is below carry). **Pin risk**: at expiry the underlying sits at the strike and the short does not learn until after the exercise deadline whether it was assigned, leaving unhedged delta over the weekend. **Exercise by exception**: OCC auto-exercises expiring equity and index options ITM by $0.01 or more absent contrary instructions; the options exchanges' cut-off for exercise notices is 4:30 pm CT (5:30 pm ET) and "most brokerage firms have an earlier cut-off" (OIC, verified; commonly 4:30-5:30 pm ET [approx]; OCC's own clearing-member deadline not reachable [uncertain]), so after-hours moves create assignment surprises.
- **Operational risk**: wrong strike/expiry/right/quantity/side, AM- vs PM-settled, weekly vs monthly; legging risk (one leg fills, market moves); broker/exchange outages; forced-liquidation mechanics. **Counterparty risk**: the Options Clearing Corporation (OCC) is central counterparty to every listed US option (novation), backed by member margin, a clearing fund and a default waterfall; designated a Systemically Important Financial Market Utility (SIFMU) in 2012. SIPC protects custody at a failed broker up to $500,000 including up to $250,000 cash; never market losses.
- **Behavioral terms**: *prospect theory* (value judged as gains/losses from a reference point; concave for gains, convex for losses; losses weigh about 2x; small probabilities overweighted); *loss aversion*; *disposition effect* (sell winners, hold losers); *overconfidence* (overestimating precision of one's information, causing overtrading); *lottery/skewness preference* (overpaying for low-probability large payoffs); *attention-driven trading*; *recency bias*; *anchoring* (judgments pulled toward a salient number such as entry price or a prior level); *sunk-cost fallacy*; *mental accounting / narrow framing* (Thaler 1985; Barberis-Huang 2001: each position evaluated in isolation, which is why ten "2% risk" trades become a 20% correlated book); *break-even effect* (Thaler-Johnson 1990: after a loss, gambles offering a chance to get back to even become attractive; the formal basis of *revenge trading*, escalating size to recover a loss); *house-money effect* (more risk after gains); *herding*; *outcome bias / "resulting"* (judging a decision by its outcome); *pre-commitment* (binding rules set in a cool state for a hot state); *kill switch* (automatic halt at a loss/error threshold).

## Mechanisms
- **Why tails are fat**: (1) vol is stochastic and clustered, so the unconditional distribution is a leptokurtic mixture of normals even if each day is conditionally normal; (2) leverage: losses raise margin requirements and cut equity at once, so liquidations cause the next leg down (margin spiral); (3) dealers hedging short-gamma inventory sell into declines (with futures, not options), and leveraged/inverse ETP end-of-day rebalancing adds to it; (4) liquidity withdraws when needed because market makers widen or pull quotes as their own inventory risk spikes; (5) correlations rise toward 1 as a common deleveraging factor dominates, so diversification fails exactly when needed.
- **Why vol-sizing fails for options**: P&L from a move dS is roughly Delta x dS + 1/2 Gamma x dS^2; for a short-gamma book the second term quadruples when the move doubles. Add vega x dIV with dIV positive and convex in the down move (vol-of-vol rises with vol), plus vanna and volga pushing the same way. A short strangle sized to lose 2% on a 2-sigma day can lose 15-30% on a 6-sigma day. ATM gamma scales roughly as 1/(S sigma sqrt(T)), so the last 2-3 weeks before expiry (the "gamma window") concentrate the risk.
- **Why gaps dominate**: intraday a trader can in principle close or hedge as the move develops; a gap delivers the whole move before any action, and the margin call is against a price that cannot be improved. Stop orders become market orders at the open. CME equity-index futures carry a dynamic price limit outside regular trading hours: +/-5% in March 2020, when ES locked limit-down overnight on several nights; CME later raised it to +/-7% [uncertain: from memory; CME's price-limit guide, reset quarterly, returned HTTP 503 on 2026-10-14]; during RTH the downside limits are 7/13/20% matching the cash halts and there is no RTH upside limit. When futures are locked the remaining overnight hedges are SPX/XSP/RUT/VIX options in Cboe Global Trading Hours (8:15 pm-9:25 am ET; a 4:15-5:00 pm electronic curb session also exists; RTH 9:30 am-4:15 pm; verified; GTH launched 2015 and moved to the 8:15 pm start in Nov 2021 [approx]) and VIX futures, all far thinner than RTH, so in March 2020 hedges were done at wide spreads or waited for the open and the 7% Level-1 halt. Market-wide circuit breakers: 7/13/20% declines in the S&P 500 from the prior close; 15-minute halts for Levels 1-2 before 3:25 pm; Level 3 halts the day; approved 2012, originally scheduled for 2013-02-04 and made operative 2013-04-08 with LULD Phase 1 (FINRA Notice 13-12, verified; the SEC bulletin predates the delay).
- **Single-stock halts (Limit Up-Limit Down)**: bands around the trailing five-minute average price of 5% (Tier 1: S&P 500, Russell 1000, select ETPs, above $3), 10% (Tier 2 above $3), 20% ($0.75-$3) or the lesser of $0.15/75% (below $0.75), doubled in the last 25 minutes (Tier 1 and sub-$3 Tier 2); a quote outside the band for 15 seconds triggers a 5-minute pause (extendable once; a pause in the last ten minutes runs to the close). Pilot approved 2012-05-31, permanent 2019-04-11 (LULD Plan, verified). Options on a halted stock halt too, so single-name short gamma cannot be hedged during the pause: GME had 40 LULD pauses in January 2021 versus one in all of 2020, 19 of them on 2021-01-28 (SEC staff report, verified).
- **Why BPR expands**: TIMS is a full revaluation at the +6%/-8% grid using the current implied-vol surface, so the -8% cell loss of a short-put book rises with IV (vega and vanna) while realized losses shrink equity; brokers add house scenarios (vol shocks within each spot scenario, wider spot ranges for concentrated, small-cap or hard-to-borrow names, concentration and liquidity add-ons) and may raise them intraday or before scheduled events (several brokers raised house requirements before the Nov 2020 election; CFE raised VIX-futures margins in Feb 2018 and CME raised energy margins in April 2020 [approx, unverified]). A book at 50% BPR/NLV in calm conditions can be above 100% after a -5% spot / +20-vol-point day with no new trades.
- **Why each stress dimension exists**: spot shocks capture gamma/delta; vol shocks vega; the joint grid captures their co-movement (spot down/vol up is the loss corner of short-premium books). Term-structure shocks hurt calendars and short-dated shorts when the front month spikes more than the back (inversion). Skew shocks matter because the put wing reprices more than ATM in a crash. Correlation-to-1 collapses a "diversified" book of 15 single-name short puts into one exposure. Liquidity haircuts matter because crash exits are at the ask with spreads several times normal. The margin overlay matters because when the scenario loss exceeds excess liquidity, the broker liquidates at the worst prices.
- **Liquidation mechanics**: brokers can liquidate without notice once equity is below maintenance and can raise house requirements intraday; liquidation routines close the largest margin consumers first (usually naked shorts) by buying at the ask in thin markets, converting a mark-to-market loss into a realized one. This is the proximate cause of most "I was right but got wiped out" stories.
- **Assignment on a spread leg**: a short put assigned early leaves long stock at K x 100 per contract (the long put stays open); a short call assigned leaves short stock and a dividend liability. The account must meet Reg T on the stock at once, which can exceed net liq many times over, so the broker liquidates, often at the open; exercise the long leg or close the stock immediately. Brokers also run expiration-day routines (typically early-to-mid afternoon [approx]) that close short options the account could not take assignment on, at whatever the market is. Standard AM-settled SPX monthlies stop trading on the business day (usually Thursday) before the settlement-value calculation and settle on the Friday opening print (SET), a gap that cannot be hedged in the option itself; PM-settled SPXW options trade to 4:00 pm ET on expiration day (1:00 pm on half days) (Cboe SPX specifications, verified).
- **Incentives of the actors**: brokers earn on volume, payment for order flow and margin interest (the SEC's Dec 2020 order found Robinhood's misleading PFOF/execution statements over 2015-late 2018, including a false execution-quality FAQ from Oct 2018 to Jun 2019, cost customers $34.1m in aggregate even after netting the commissions they saved; $65m civil penalty), so platform design pushes activity; market makers earn the spread and the VRP, so they are structurally short the lottery tickets retail buys; exchanges earn per contract (weeklies and 0DTE multiply fee events); educators/finfluencers sell high-win-rate narratives. None of them bear the tail risk of the customer's book.
- **When short vol is compensated**: persistent net demand for index puts (hedgers, structured products) must be warehoused by capital-constrained dealers, so implied vol embeds a premium over expected realized vol (Bollen-Whaley 2004; Garleanu-Pedersen-Poteshman 2009). The seller is paid for crash risk and balance sheet. It stops being compensated when (a) size makes a 1-in-10-year event fatal (no premium compensates for ruin), (b) crowding compresses the premium (2017: VIX averaged about 11, a record-low year, with record short VIX-futures positioning before Feb 2018), or (c) spreads, margin and taxes consume it.
- **Why short-vol funds die in clusters**: a long calm period mechanically produces high-Sharpe track records; capital and leverage follow; the crowd covers at once when the shock comes (XIV's own end-of-day rebalancing demand for VIX futures on 2018-02-05 contributed to the +115% VIX move it was short). Termination triggers (XIV's acceleration at an 80% intraday loss) turn a drawdown into a permanent loss.
- **Expectancy framing of "sell premium wins 70-80%"**: under a lognormal with no premium, a 1-SD strangle finishes between the strikes about 68% of the time with zero expected P&L before costs; the win rate is just the complement of the tail area and tail losses exceed the credits by construction. Positive expectancy comes only from implied exceeding realized vol. Managing at 50% of max profit and closing near 21 DTE raises the win rate but shrinks average wins; the tail distribution decides survival.
- **Assignment and pin as risk events**: short ITM calls over ex-dividend are exercised when the dividend exceeds remaining extrinsic value (the short becomes short stock and owes the dividend); deep ITM short puts are exercised early for carry when rates are high; pinned expiries leave uncertain delta over a weekend. SPX/XSP/NDX/RUT are European and cash-settled (no assignment or pin), with the SET gap risk above. Retail customers also exercise equity calls early when it is clearly irrational, forfeiting time value (Poteshman-Serbin 2003).
- **Behavioral chain**: prospect theory's convex loss region makes holding a loser feel like a chance to break even (disposition; break-even effect), and makes "defending" and rolling feel cheaper than realizing (sunk cost, anchoring on entry); narrow framing sizes each trade alone so the book is never summed; overweighting small probabilities makes far-OTM options attractive despite being priced above expected value (lottery preference); overconfidence raises turnover, and turnover transfers wealth to the spread; attention drives purchases of in-the-news names at the wrong time; recency makes a calm regime feel permanent so size creeps up into the shock; winning streaks raise risk tolerance (house money) and losses trigger escalation (revenge). Social media converts individual biases into correlated flows that sophisticated counterparties exploit. Outcome bias then labels a reckless winner a good decision and a sound loser a mistake, so the wrong lesson is learned. Pre-commitment rules, journals and kill switches work because they move the decision from the hot state to the cool state.

## Formulas and quantitative rules
```
Normal-distribution tail frequencies (two-sided, daily, 252 days/yr):
  |r| > 2 sigma : 4.55%     -> ~1 day in 22
  |r| > 3 sigma : 0.27%     -> ~1 day in 370 (~1.5 years)
  |r| > 4 sigma : 6.33e-5   -> ~1 day in 15,787 (~63 years)
  |r| > 5 sigma : 5.73e-7   -> ~1 day in 1.74 million (~6,900 years)
  |r| > 6 sigma : 1.97e-9   -> ~1 day in 507 million (~2.0 million years)
Daily SPX sigma ~ 1% long-run, so a 4-sigma day is roughly a 4% day.
1987-10-19 (-20.5%) was ~20 sigma on pre-crash vol: impossible under a normal.

Option P&L under a joint shock (second-order Taylor, per position):
  dP ~= Delta*dS + 0.5*Gamma*dS^2 + Vega*dIV + 0.5*Volga*dIV^2 + Vanna*dS*dIV + Theta*dt
  (dS in underlying units, dIV in vol points as decimals, Theta per day)
  Short-gamma example: a 1% move costing $1,000 via gamma implies ~$25,000 at 5% and
  ~$100,000 at 10% (quadratic). Short vega -$5,000/vol-pt x +20 pts = -$100,000.
  Spot -10% with IV +20 pts ~= -$200,000 before delta, vanna, volga (which usually add losses).

Greek limit sheet (n contracts, multiplier 100):
  $Gamma(1%) = 0.5 * Gamma * (0.01*S)^2 * 100 * n      (gamma P&L of a 1% move)
  $Vega = Vega * 100 * n per vol point, bucketed 0-7d / 8-30d / 31-90d / 90d+
  Normalized vega = Vega * sqrt(30/DTE) (or a regression vol-beta per tenor) to net across tenors
  Beta-weighted delta ($) = sum_i Delta_i * 100 * n_i * S_i * beta_i ; / S_index for index shares
  Theta/gamma breakeven (delta-hedged, r~0): Theta_day = -0.5*Gamma*S^2*sigma^2/252, so the move
    that pays for theta is |dS*| = sqrt(2|Theta_day|/Gamma) ~= S*sigma_iv/sqrt(252);
    a day at 2x breakeven costs ~3x the day's theta (gamma loss 4x theta, minus theta earned).
  ATM gamma ~= n(d1)/(S*sigma*sqrt(T)); ATM straddle ~= 0.8*S*sigma*sqrt(T) ~= expected |move|,
    so 1 SD ~= 1.25 x straddle ("0.85 x straddle" is a practitioner convention, not a derivation).
  Conservative retail limits [approx]: $Gamma(1%) and $Vega each <= 1-2% of NLV per 1% move /
    per vol point; front-bucket vega tighter than back; beta-weighted delta <= a set share of NLV.

VaR / ES:
  VaR(1d,q) = loss quantile at q; ES(q) = E[loss | loss > VaR(q)]. Delta-normal VaR assumes linear
  P&L and constant vol: wrong on both counts for short gamma/vega. Full revaluation only; report
  ES beside VaR; both are ordinary-day measures that the stress matrix overrides.
  Historical replay set (apply actual spot, IV-surface, term and skew changes):
    1987-10-19/20; 2008-10-06..10-10 and 10-15; 2011-08-08; 2015-08-24 (open print, ETF
    dislocation); 2018-02-05/06; 2020-02-24..03-16; 2022-11-10 (upside gap +5.5%);
    2024-08-05 (VIX print 65.73 on thin GTH quotes); 2025-04-03/04/09 (-4.84%, -5.97%, +9.52%).
  Report VaR, ES and worst replay next to the worst stress cell and the margin at that cell.

Stress-matrix recipe (full revaluation for the real thing):
  rows: spot {-20,-10,-5,0,+5,+10,+20}%   cols: IV {-10,-5,0,+5,+10,+20} pts
  overlays: front-month IV shocked 2x the back month; 25-delta put skew +3 to +5 pts vs ATM;
  single names moved by beta x index move (corr = 1); exits at bid/ask with spreads 3x;
  margin recomputed per cell.  Report worst-cell P&L / NLV, worst-cell margin / NLV,
  and the first cell at which liquidation starts.

Worst-case loss (undefined risk):
  short put:  (K - premium) * 100 * n
  short call: unbounded; for stress assume +50-100% in a single name (M&A, short squeeze: GME
    rose 71% in about three hours on 2021-01-22) and +20% for an index (largest SPX up days
    +11.6% 2008-10-13, +10.8% 2008-10-28, +9.52% 2025-04-09, so +20% is a two-day worst case)
  short strangle: the larger side, but margin counts both.
Notional: short put notional = K * 100 * n. 10 short SPY 500 puts = $500,000 notional;
  on a $100,000 account that is 5x downside leverage; a 20% gap loses ~$100,000 less premium.
Liquidity sizing [approx]: per strike/expiry n <= 1-5% of open interest and a few percent of ADV;
  days_to_liquidate = n / (ADV share you can take per day); haircut NLV by 0.5 x (3x normal
  spread) x 100 x n on every short leg before computing BPR/NLV.
Event sizing: expected |move| ~ straddle (above); size short premium so the loss at 2x the
  expected move stays inside the per-underlying cap (3-4x implied-move gaps are routine in names).

Kelly (discrete; risk 1 unit to win b units; win prob p, lose prob q = 1-p):
  f* = p - q/b   (f* <= 0 -> do not bet)
  A: p=0.70, avg win $100, avg loss $300 -> b=0.333 -> f* = 0.70 - 0.90 = -0.20 (negative EV)
  B: p=0.70, avg win $100, avg loss $150 -> b=0.667 -> f* = 0.25 (full Kelly); quarter-Kelly ~6%
Kelly (continuous; mu = annual expected excess return, sigma = annual vol):
  f* = mu/sigma^2 ;  g_full = r + mu^2/(2 sigma^2) ;  g(c) = r + (mu^2/sigma^2)(c - c^2/2)
  mu=5%, sigma=15% -> f* = 2.22x leverage, g = r + 5.56%/yr; half-Kelly 1.11x, g = r + 4.17%
  (75% of the growth at half the vol). 2x Kelly -> zero growth; more -> negative.

Risk of ruin:
  Kelly fraction c: P(equity ever reaches fraction x of start) = x^(2/c - 1)
    full Kelly: P(halve) = 50%;  half-Kelly: 12.5%;  quarter-Kelly: 0.8%
  Fixed 1-unit even-money bets, N units of capital, p > 0.5: RoR = (q/p)^N
    p=0.55: N=10 -> 13.4%;  N=20 -> 1.8%;  N=50 -> 0.004%

Drawdown recovery: gain needed = d/(1-d)
  10%->11.1%  20%->25%  30%->42.9%  50%->100%  70%->233%  90%->900%
  Years to recover at CAGR g: ln(1/(1-d))/ln(1+g); d=50%, g=10% -> 7.3 years.

Vol targeting: exposure = equity * sigma_target / sigma_forecast, capped at L_max
  (sigma_forecast is lowest right before shocks: cap leverage and add the stress overlay).
Fixed fraction: contracts = floor(equity * r / max_loss_per_contract)
  $100,000, r=2%, $5-wide vertical sold at $1.00 (max loss $400) -> 5 contracts.

Expectancy: E = p*W - (1-p)*L ; breakeven W/L = (1-p)/p
  p=0.70 -> W/L >= 0.43 ; 0.80 -> 0.25 ; 0.90 -> 0.11
  "Take profit at 50% of credit C, stop at 2C": W=0.5C, L=2C -> need p >= 0.80 just to break even
  before costs; early management typically lifts realized p to 80-90%, so net edge is thin and
  rests on the VRP and on the loss really being capped at 2C (gaps make it larger).
Delta as probability: risk-neutral P(expire ITM) is N(d2) (call) / N(-d2) (put); delta is N(d1) /
  -N(-d1) with d1 = d2 + sigma*sqrt(T), so call delta overstates and |put delta| understates it by
  an amount growing with vol and time; |delta| ~ P(ITM) only when sigma*sqrt(T) is small.
  16-delta strikes ~ 1 SD ~ 68% POP for the strangle (approximate). Real-world P differs again
  (drift, VRP, fat tails).

Prospect theory (Tversky-Kahneman 1992): v(x) = x^0.88 (x>=0); v(x) = -2.25(-x)^0.88 (x<0);
  w(p) = p^g/(p^g+(1-p)^g)^(1/g), g=0.61 gains / 0.69 losses: overweights small p.
Disposition (Odean 1998): PGR = realized gains/(realized+paper gains); PLR likewise for losses.
  Odean: PGR 0.148 vs PLR 0.098 -> gains realized ~1.5x as readily (except December).
```

## Empirical magnitudes
- **SPX daily returns**: unconditional daily sigma about 1% (15-20% annualized); excess kurtosis of daily returns since 1950 on the order of 20-30 with 1987 included and still well above 5 without it [uncertain: window-dependent]. Largest moves: 1987-10-19 -20.5%; 2020-03-16 -12.0%; 2020-03-12 -9.5%; 2008-10-15 -9.0%; 2008-12-01 -8.9%; up: 2008-10-13 +11.6%; 2008-10-28 +10.8%; 2020-03-24 +9.4%; 2025-04-09 +9.52% (after 2025-04-03 -4.84% and 2025-04-04 -5.97%, about -10.5% cumulative; verified). ## Practitioner heuristics
- **Size to the stress loss, not to vol**: define the reference shock (index -20% with front IV doubling and skew steepening; single names -40%) and cap the book's loss in that cell at a fraction of net liquidation value (conservative 5-10%; aggressive 15-25%). If the cell exceeds the cap, cut size or buy far-OTM wings; wings are the price of survival. Report VaR, ES and the worst historical replay beside the worst cell so no single number is trusted alone.
- **Greek limit sheet**: cap $Gamma(1%) and $Vega as fractions of NLV (conservative retail book: about 1-2% of NLV per 1% move and per vol point [approx]), front-bucket vega tighter than back, cap beta-weighted delta, and compare realized daily moves with the theta/gamma breakeven: a short-gamma book is profitable only while realized moves stay below |dS*| = S x sigma_iv/sqrt(252), and the last 2-3 weeks to expiry are where that test fails fastest.
- **Buying-power usage for short premium**: tastytrade-style guidance keeps total BPR for undefined-risk positions near 25-50% of net liq, scaled with VIX (about 25-35% when VIX is low, up to about 50% when it is high) [approx; tastylive not reachable]. Feb 2018 and Mar 2020 lesson: BPR on short index/equity options expanded roughly 2-3x within days, so 50% in calm became a margin call. Rule of thumb [practitioner folklore, no systematic sample]: keep undefined-risk BPR at or below 30-35% of net liq in low-VIX regimes so a 2-3x expansion leaves the account under 100%, holding the balance as cash or T-bills, not more positions.
- **Per-trade risk**: 0.5-2% of equity max loss per defined-risk trade; 1-5% per undefined-risk position measured in the stress cell; 5-10% per underlying; 10-15% per correlated theme; sum them at book level (narrow framing is the failure mode). The equity-trading "2% rule" assumes stops can be honored, which gaps defeat.
- **Kelly in practice**: half-Kelly at most; quarter-Kelly when the edge comes from fewer than about five years of data or a backtest; include the worst historical cell when estimating p and b. An edge that vanishes when crowded (2017 short vol) is not a stationary edge.
- **Count notional like stock**: sum strike x 100 x contracts over all short puts, apply beta, and treat it as leveraged equity; many "theta" traders learn they are 3-6x long equities only on the day it matters.
- **Liquidity and vol-of-vol**: no strike/expiry position larger than a few percent of its open interest or ADV [approx]; mark the book at the far side of a 3x spread before computing BPR/NLV; when VVIX is high (roughly above 110-120 versus a 2018-2025 norm near 85-100 [approx]) vega P&L is convex (volga) and short-vega limits should be smaller.
- **Diversify, then assume it fails**: ladder expiries, spread across underlyings and strategy types (short premium, long premium, directional), but size the equity-linked short-premium sleeve as one position at correlation 1; diversification helps on the ordinary 95% of days and not on the 5% that decide survival.
- **Event/earnings sizing**: read the implied move from the ATM straddle (expected |move| about the straddle price; 1 SD about 1.25x), size short premium to survive 2x the implied move inside the per-underlying cap, and note that retail buying clusters at peak pre-earnings IV (de Silva-Smith-So): the side to avoid, not join.
- **Tail hedges that get used**: a premium budget of roughly 0.5-2% of NAV per year in far-OTM index puts (about 5-15 delta, 2-6 months out), rolled before decay accelerates, with a pre-written monetization rule (sell a fraction at 5-10x cost or at a VIX level) [approx]; or VIX calls/call spreads against short SPX puts (cheaper per vega but with basis and roll risk). Size hedges to the margin shortfall in the stress cell (what stops the broker liquidating), not to full P&L neutrality, which the VRP makes unaffordable.
- **Crisis playbook (written in advance)**: (1) delta first, with ES/MES futures or index ETF shares, not options (no vol premium, no spread blow-out; dealers hedge the same way); (2) cut gross before net: close the positions with the largest margin consumption and widest expected liquidation spread first, which is what the broker will do anyway but at worse prices; (3) do not buy puts for protection once IV has doubled (VIX 50+) unless the stress cell shows a margin shortfall that only convexity can close; crisis-IV puts that then mean-revert are the commonest way a survivable drawdown becomes permanent; (4) no new short premium until BPR/NLV is back under the calm-regime cap and front-month IV has stopped rising; (5) one person has authority to flatten and no one has authority to add.
- **Short-premium management**: tastytrade-style rules (about 45-DTE, roughly 16-delta entries in SPX/liquid ETFs): take profits near 50% of credit; close or roll near 21 DTE. The principle is to exit when the theta/gamma ratio has fallen enough that the remaining credit no longer pays for the convexity; the specific numbers are backtest artifacts of that setup (gamma peaks later for far-OTM strikes, earlier for ATM) and should be re-derived for other tenors, deltas or products. Never add to losers; roll only if the new position passes the entry checklist on its own; cap roll chains at a preset count. These cut the gamma tail, not gap risk.
- **Prefer index options for size**: SPX/XSP/NDX/RUT are cash-settled, European, PM-eligible and deep (SPX is the liquid product; mini/micro XSP, MRUT and NANOS trade with wider spreads), and are Section 1256 contracts (60/40 capital-gains treatment with year-end mark-to-market) for US taxable accounts only; ETF options (SPY, QQQ, IWM) are not 1256 contracts, and the treatment is irrelevant in IRAs and to non-US traders. Single-name short premium belongs only in names whose worst-case gap the account can absorb.
- **Assignment/pin checklist**: close or roll short ITM calls before ex-dividend when extrinsic value is below the dividend (SPY's quarterly ex-dividend date falls on the third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec, i.e., expiration day, so short SPY calls in those expiries are exposed; mechanics only); do not carry near-the-money short equity options into expiry unhedged; remember the post-close exercise window (exchange cut-off 5:30 pm ET, brokers earlier); check AM vs PM settlement before expiry week; standard SPX monthlies stop trading Thursday; on assignment of a spread leg, act at the open rather than wait for the broker's routine.
- **Order-entry and outage discipline**: send spreads as one multi-leg limit order; leg only in liquid, calm markets; read back strike, expiry, right, quantity, side; use order preview; treat "broker down" as a scenario (second funded broker, phone numbers for closing orders, knowledge of erroneous-trade bust procedures); know whether the broker supports GTH order entry before it is needed.
- **Kill switches**: daily loss limit (commonly 2-3% of NAV), weekly limit, and a drawdown ladder (e.g., -10% -> halve size; -20% -> flat and review for a fixed period), written in numbers and tied to actions in advance. Hard size limits that do not move intraday are the control for revenge trading (break-even effect) and house-money escalation.
- **Process over outcome**: grade each trade on plan adherence (entry criteria, size, exit), not P&L; journal thesis, greeks at entry, planned exit, actual exit and deviation reason; review weekly; tag errors as process (fixable) vs variance. Most large losses turn out to be process failures (oversized, no exit rule, defended a loser).
- **Realistic edges**: after spreads, slippage, margin cost and taxes, systematic retail short premium in index products has delivered net Sharpe ratios on the order of 0.3-0.7 with negative skew [uncertain]; anything marketed above 1.5 is a calm-window artifact until proven otherwise. Retail cannot replicate professional desks' capital and portfolio margin at scale, near-free intraday hedging, OTC/variance-swap access, flow data, maker economics or spread-free execution. Retail can trade liquid index products, keep size survivable, avoid the lottery side of the market, and stop paying the spread repeatedly.
- **Regime awareness**: long calm periods (2017; much of 2023-2024; the post-April-2025 recovery) are when short-vol positioning peaks and the next spike hurts most; track VIX term-structure slope, VVIX, dealer-gamma estimates, derivative-income ETF and 0DTE growth (structural vol supply) and the book's own BPR/NAV ratio as "size down" signals instead of waiting for the spike; Aug 2024 (cross-asset deleveraging hitting an index-vol book) and Apr 2025 (two-day -10.5% with a VIX close above 50) are the current calibration events.

## Common misconceptions and pitfalls
- **"High POP means a good trade"**: POP is one minus a model tail area; a 90% POP trade losing 15x its credit has negative expectancy before costs.
- **"Vol-sizing controls risk"**: realized vol is lowest just before shocks and option losses are convex; without a stress overlay the book is oversized exactly before crashes. The same applies to VaR calibrated on a calm window.
- **"Defined risk is small risk"**: a vertical loses 100% of its width; ten "small" iron condors across correlated indices are one position; per-trade bounds are not book bounds; early assignment of the short leg can demand Reg T margin on full stock notional over a weekend.
- **"Twenty underlyings diversify a short-put book"**: in Mar 2020 average pairwise S&P 500 correlation (realized and Cboe implied-correlation indices) rose to about 0.8-0.9 for several weeks; in Feb 2018 the rise was shorter and smaller [approx; verify against Cboe COR1M/COR3M history]; either way the book behaved as one leveraged index position on the days that mattered.
- **"I'll close it before it gets bad"**: gaps, overnight futures limits, LULD and market-wide halts, broker outages and forced liquidation all happen before the trader can act; stops are not hedges.
- **"Rolling fixes a loser"**: a roll is a close plus a new trade; if the new trade would not be opened on its own, the roll is a sunk-cost decision; roll chains that collect credits while adding size or duration are the classic path to a catastrophic loss.
- **"The backtest since 2010 proves it"**: short-vol backtests that omit 1987, 2008, 2018, 2020 (and the next one) sample a calm regime; survivorship removes the products and funds that failed.
- **"Delta is the probability of expiring ITM"**: delta is N(d1); the risk-neutral ITM probability is N(d2), lower for calls and larger in magnitude for puts, and real-world odds differ again by drift, VRP and fat tails.
- **"Portfolio margin is more capital"**: it is more leverage plus a requirement that grows with vol and losses (TIMS at live IV plus house add-ons); margin expansion is a risk, not a feature.
- **"Puts are cheap insurance" / "puts are always too expensive"**: both are regime statements; continuously held puts drag on average (AQR), while sized and monetized tail hedges can lift compound growth (Universa). The holding rule, size and the investor's forced-selling risk decide; buying them at crisis IV is the worst of both.
- **"VIX calls are SPX puts"**: VIX derivatives carry basis and roll (contango) costs and respond to vol-of-vol; they can lose while SPX falls modestly.
- **"SIPC covers losses" / "the market maker is my counterparty"**: SIPC covers custody at a failed broker (up to $500k/$250k cash) and never losses; OCC is the counterparty to every listed option.
- **"A trade that made money was a good trade"**: outcome bias; grade the process.
- **"Copying social-media or congressional-trade trackers gives an edge"**: STOCK Act filings arrive with up to a 45-day lag (tracked by "Pelosi tracker" accounts and the NANC/KRUZ ETFs launched 2023), so copiers buy after the move; Kakhbod et al. (2023) find most finfluencers are unskilled or "antiskilled" (negative abnormal returns) and that these attract larger followings than the skilled minority [the often-quoted split of about 56% antiskilled / 28% unskilled / 16% skilled is uncertain].

## Contested or uncertain claims
- **Tail hedging**: Universa/Spitznagel and Taleb (barbell: most capital safe, a small slice highly convex) argue the geometric-growth benefit of cutting drawdowns outweighs the bleed; AQR (Israelov, Asness) argues the VRP makes puts a worse drawdown tool than de-risking or diversification for typical holding patterns, and disputes whether hedge-capital returns (the 3,612%/4,144% headlines) translate into whole-portfolio gains net of multi-year bleed. The sides do not hold sizing, monetization rules or forced-selling risk constant.
- **VRP: risk premium or mispricing?** Risk-premium view: compensation for crash and vol-of-vol risk borne by constrained dealers (Garleanu-Pedersen-Poteshman). Behavioral view: overpriced lottery demand and overweighted small probabilities (Boyer-Vorkink; prospect theory). Both predict a premium; they differ on whether it shrinks as retail flow changes.
- **Kelly vs expected utility**: Samuelson (1979) argued maximizing expected log wealth is not optimal for general utility; Thorp/Ziemba/MacLean defend it for long-horizon growth. Practitioners use fractional Kelly mainly as a hedge against parameter error.
- **Does "manage at 50% / 21 DTE" improve risk-adjusted returns?** tastytrade's in-house studies say yes; peer-reviewed confirmation is thin; the benefit may be mostly reduced gamma-window tail exposure traded against commissions, and the thresholds are setup-specific.
- **0DTE systemic risk**: some sell-side strategists warned in 2023 of an intraday "Volmageddon 2.0"; Cboe and others argue flows are balanced and net dealer gamma is small. Unresolved [as of 2025-2026].
- **Retail share of option volume and retail losses**: industry estimates of 40-45% versus Bryzgalova et al.'s over-60% peak differ by identification method (wholesaler-routed single-leg prints vs broker surveys), not fact; loss estimates vary by sample, period and method (spread cost vs alpha), so treat any single figure as order-of-magnitude.
- **CME overnight equity-index limit**: 5% applied in March 2020; whether it is now 7% could not be confirmed (CME pages unreachable); both critiques agree the current value must be checked against the quarterly price-limit guide before sizing overnight hedges.
- **VIX on 2018-02-05**: the SEC order says about 115% (17.31 -> 37.32 = +115.6%); Wikipedia's VIX article says +103.99%; the SEC figure is used here.
- **Aug 2024 VIX spike**: real stress vs artifact of thin early-session SPX options in the VIX formula; affects how much to trust VIX-triggered rules.
- **Fund-loss figures** for the LJM mutual fund, Malachite, Catalyst, OptionSellers.com and IBKR 2020 are from press coverage and approximate; only LJM's 80%+ loss and $1bn+ AUM come from a regulator.

## Relationships to other domains
- Volatility surface / skew and term structure: supplies the skew-steepening and term-inversion shocks and the sticky-strike vs sticky-delta assumptions used in the stress matrix.
- Greeks and higher-order risk: gamma, vega, vanna, volga drive the scenario math and the greek limit sheet; cross-greeks explain why joint shocks exceed the sum of single shocks.
- Variance-risk-premium / short-volatility strategies: provides the edge estimate (mu, sigma, tail) that feeds Kelly and stress sizing; the survivorship cases here are that domain's failure modes.
- Margin, clearing and settlement: FINRA 4210 (Reg T, TIMS, the 2026 intraday margin standards), OCC novation and exercise rules, SIPC, NSCC margin calls (Jan 2021) are the plumbing behind margin expansion, assignment and counterparty risk.
- Expiration and settlement mechanics: SET, Thursday last trade for AM-settled SPX, SPXW 4:00 pm close, exercise cut-offs and pin risk.
- Earnings and event volatility: implied-move sizing from the straddle and the de Silva-Smith-So evidence on retail buying at peak IV.
- Taxation: Section 1256 scope (broad-based index options yes, ETF options no) and year-end mark-to-market.
- Market microstructure and retail order flow: PFOF, wholesaler execution and bid-ask cost as the main channel of retail losses (Bryzgalova et al.); attention and herding flows.
- Dealer positioning / gamma exposure and 0DTE: the amplification mechanism for tails, why dealers hedge with futures (the crisis-playbook rationale), and the contested 0DTE claims.
- Volatility events and history: 1987, 2008, Feb 2018, Mar 2020, Aug 2024, Apr 2025 are the calibration set for stress scenarios and the recency-bias examples.
- Technical levels / support-resistance: anchoring on salient prices is cited here only in general terms; under this project's governance rule no statistics on SPY prior-session high/low behavior are computed or stored in this file.
